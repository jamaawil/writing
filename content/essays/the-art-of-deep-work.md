Most people treat distraction as a minor inconvenience. The reality is that shallow work has become the default, and depth has become rare.

Deep work is the ability to focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task. It produces results that matter. It builds skills. And it is increasingly hard to practice in an environment optimized for interruption.

The cost is invisible. You never see the ideas you didn't have, the writing you didn't finish, or the skills you didn't build — because you were in another meeting, checking another notification, or switching between tasks that each deserved your full attention.

The practice is simple but not easy: protect long blocks of uninterrupted time, eliminate the rituals of shallow visibility, and treat your attention as the scarce resource it actually is.
