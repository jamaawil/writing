There is a common misconception that writing is what you do after you've figured out what you think. You think, then you write it down.

The opposite is closer to the truth. Writing is how you figure out what you think. The act of putting words in sequence forces a precision that vague mental impressions never require. A half-formed idea can survive indefinitely in your head. The moment you try to write it, its weaknesses become visible.

This is why the best thinkers write — not to communicate what they already know, but to discover what they don't. The notebook, the draft, the margin note: these are not records of thought, they are instruments of it.

Write badly and often. The quality of your thinking will follow.
