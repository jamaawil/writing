Most of what matters in life is not done in heroic bursts. It is done in increments so small they feel insignificant at the time.

I have read enough productivity literature to be suspicious of it, but one insight from that literature has proven consistently true in my own life: the difference between people who accomplish things and people who don't is usually not talent, intelligence, or even effort in the dramatic sense. It is consistency — the willingness to show up, at roughly the same time, to do roughly the same thing, regardless of whether you feel like it.

Habits automate consistency. Once a behavior is habitual, you no longer have to decide to do it — the decision has been made in advance by the system. This is not about discipline in the virtue-ethics sense. It is more like infrastructure: you build it once, and then it runs.

The frustrating thing about habits is that they are slow to form and fast to break. Three weeks of consistent behavior creates something fragile. Three months creates something more robust. A year creates something that feels like part of who you are. The intervening time is tedious in proportion to how much you care about the outcome.

**The key insight I keep returning to is about identity.** The people who sustain good habits over long periods tend to understand those habits not as things they do but as expressions of who they are. The runner who identifies as a runner misses fewer runs than the person who is "trying to run more." The writer who identifies as a writer writes more consistently than the person who has "been meaning to write." The identity comes first, or it follows quickly from the behavior, and once it's established it does the motivational work that willpower cannot sustain.

This is not a trick. It is a description of how behavior and self-concept interact. You don't have to believe it in advance. You just have to act consistently long enough for the identity to form around the behavior.

Start small. Do it consistently. Adjust what needs adjusting. That is the whole framework. Everything else is commentary.
