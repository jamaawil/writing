One note, one idea. Atomicity is what makes notes linkable — if a note contains three ideas, you can only ever link to all three at once. If each idea lives in its own note, you can link precisely.

This forces clarity. Writing an atomic note means you must isolate the idea from everything surrounding it. That act of isolation is itself a thinking operation — it reveals whether you actually understood the idea or were just borrowing its language.
