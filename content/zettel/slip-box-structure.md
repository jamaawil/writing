Luhmann's original slip-box used a branching address system: note 1 led to 1a, 1a to 1a1, enabling unlimited expansion without reorganization. The address was not a location but a *pointer* — it told you where to find related notes, not what category the note belonged to.

Digital systems don't need addresses, but the underlying principle holds: structure should emerge from [[linking-ideas|linking]], not be imposed from the top down. A folder hierarchy is a pre-commitment to a worldview. A link network is a record of how thinking actually happened.
