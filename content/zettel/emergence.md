Emergence is what happens when local interactions between simple components produce global patterns that cannot be predicted from any single component. Ant colonies, markets, consciousness, culture — none of these exist at the level of individual agents, yet all are real effects of individual agents interacting by simple rules at scale.

The communication revolution is an emergent phenomenon. No individual actor designed echo chambers, epistemic fragmentation, or the collapse of shared context. These emerged from billions of people following simple local incentives — share what feels true, avoid what feels uncomfortable, return to what rewards you — through platforms that amplified those incentives.

Understanding emergence matters because it reframes where intervention is possible. You cannot eliminate the global pattern by correcting individual actors. You change emergent outcomes by changing the rules of local interaction — the incentive structures, the feedback loops, the architecture of the system itself.
