Wendy Wolford is a geographer whose book Governing Global Land Deals examines the contemporary global surge in large-scale land acquisitions, often called "land grabs," in which governments, sovereign wealth funds, and corporations acquire vast tracts of agricultural land in developing countries, frequently displacing local farming communities in the process. Her research analyzes the governance frameworks, voluntary guidelines, and regulatory efforts that have emerged in response to international criticism of land grabbing, questioning whether these governance mechanisms genuinely protect affected communities or primarily serve to legitimate and smooth the continuation of large-scale land acquisition. Her broader scholarship, grounded significantly in research on Brazilian land reform and agrarian social movements, examines the political economy of land, agriculture, and rural social movements across Latin America and beyond. She has held academic leadership positions in international studies at Cornell University. Her work contributes to critical agrarian studies and debates over global land governance.
