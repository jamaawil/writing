D. Graham Burnett is a historian of science whose work examines how practices of measurement, mapping, and classification have shaped both scientific knowledge and political power. His book Masters of All They Surveyed traces British surveying expeditions in colonial Guyana, showing how the seemingly technical work of mapmaking was entangled with imperial claims over territory and people. He is broadly interested in the history of exploration, natural history, and the instruments and institutions through which Western science claimed authority over unfamiliar landscapes and populations. His writing combines careful archival history with attention to the cultural meanings of scientific practice. He teaches history of science at Princeton and has also written on subjects ranging from whale science to the history of attention.
