Eve Troutt Powell is an American historian whose book A Different Shade of Colonialism examines Egypt's own colonial ambitions in Sudan, complicating narratives that cast Egypt purely as a victim of British imperialism by showing how Egyptian nationalists simultaneously constructed their own colonial and racial hierarchies over Sudanese populations. Her work draws on Arabic-language sources to trace how ideas of race, slavery, and civilization circulated within intra-African and intra-Arab colonial relationships, not only in the more commonly studied European-African axis. She has also written on the history of slavery and its memory in the modern Middle East. Her scholarship pushes back against binary colonizer-colonized frameworks by attending to overlapping and competing colonialisms. She teaches history at the University of Pennsylvania, where her work continues to shape the study of race, slavery, and empire in the Middle East and Northeast Africa.
