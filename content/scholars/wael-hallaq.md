Wael Hallaq is a scholar of Islamic law whose book The Impossible State argues that the modern nation-state, as a political form built on centralized sovereignty and the separation of religious and political authority, is fundamentally incompatible with the classical Islamic legal and political tradition, which distributed authority differently and integrated law, ethics, and governance in ways the modern state structure cannot accommodate. He contends that contemporary calls for an "Islamic state" therefore represent a kind of category error, attempting to graft premodern Islamic legal ideals onto a modern state apparatus whose very structure was developed within, and remains shaped by, a specifically European political and legal history. His broader scholarship offers detailed historical and legal analysis of the development of Islamic jurisprudence across many centuries, challenging both Orientalist dismissals and contemporary Islamist idealizations of classical Islamic law. His work has been influential and debated across Islamic studies, political theory, and comparative law. He has held academic positions including at Columbia University.
