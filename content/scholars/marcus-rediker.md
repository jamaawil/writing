Marcus Rediker is an American historian whose books Villains of All Nations and The Slave Ship examine, respectively, the world of Atlantic piracy and the material and social conditions of the transatlantic slave trade, both approached through a distinctive "history from below" method centered on the experiences of sailors, pirates, and enslaved people rather than merchants, admirals, and statesmen. In The Slave Ship, he reconstructs in granular detail the physical space, daily brutality, and social relations aboard slave ships, treating the vessel itself as a key site for understanding the machinery of the slave trade and the resistance enslaved people mounted even within its confines. His work on piracy examines pirate ships as sites of rough egalitarian and multiracial social organization that challenged the hierarchies of the merchant shipping world they preyed upon. His scholarship combines rigorous archival research with a consistent commitment to recovering the agency of history's most marginalized participants. He has taught history at the University of Pittsburgh for much of his career.
