Subhankar Banerjee is an Indian-American photographer and writer whose book Arctic National Wildlife Refuge documents the ecology and Indigenous communities of the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge through extensive photographic fieldwork, work that became directly entangled in American political debates over oil drilling in the refuge when his images were cited in congressional debate over the area's environmental value. His photography and accompanying writing emphasize both the refuge's ecological significance as a functioning, seasonally dynamic wilderness and the Indigenous Gwich'in and Iñupiat communities whose ways of life are directly tied to the land and caribou herds his images document. His work has been credited and criticized in equal measure for its direct political impact, illustrating how photography and environmental advocacy can become entangled with high-stakes resource policy debates. His broader work continues to combine visual art with environmental and Indigenous rights advocacy across Arctic and other threatened landscapes. He remains an active photographer and writer on climate change and Indigenous environmental justice.
