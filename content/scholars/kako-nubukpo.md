Kako Nubukpo is a Togolese economist whose writing, including work translated as Sortir l'Afrique de la servitude monétaire, argues that African monetary systems, particularly the CFA franc used across much of Francophone West and Central Africa, continue to constrain African economic sovereignty long after formal decolonization. A former minister in the Togolese government and central bank official, he brings direct policymaking experience to his academic critique of currency arrangements that peg African currencies to the euro under conditions largely set by France. He advocates for monetary reforms that would give African states genuine control over exchange rates and monetary policy as tools of development rather than external stability guarantees benefiting former colonial powers. His work sits at the center of ongoing debates over CFA franc reform and broader African monetary sovereignty. He continues to be an influential voice in both academic and policy circles on African economic self-determination.
