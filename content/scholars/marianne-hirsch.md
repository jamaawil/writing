Marianne Hirsch is a literary scholar whose concept of "postmemory," developed most fully in The Generation of Postmemory, describes the relationship that children and later generations of survivors of collective trauma, particularly the Holocaust, have to experiences they did not personally live through but that were transmitted to them so powerfully through family stories, images, and behaviors that they take on the qualities of memories in their own right. She examines how this inherited, mediated relationship to trauma shapes identity, art, and political consciousness across generations, drawing on literature, photography, and visual culture to trace its workings. Her framework has been widely adopted well beyond Holocaust studies, applied to the intergenerational transmission of trauma from slavery, genocide, and other collective catastrophes. Her broader scholarship spans feminist theory, visual culture, and the study of memory and testimony. She has taught comparative literature and gender studies at Columbia University.
