Nandita Dinesh is a scholar and theatre practitioner whose writings on voluntourism critically examine the growing global industry of short-term volunteer travel, in which typically Western participants pay to volunteer briefly in development or conservation projects in the Global South. She argues that much voluntourism prioritizes the personal experience and resume-building of volunteers over the actual needs and self-determination of host communities, often causing more disruption than benefit despite good intentions, from unqualified volunteers performing skilled work to short attachments disrupting institutional continuity, particularly in settings like orphanages. Her work draws on applied theatre and participatory methods to examine these dynamics both critically and constructively, exploring how volunteer and development encounters might be restructured around genuine community accountability. Her scholarship contributes to a growing critical literature on the ethics and effects of international volunteering and voluntourism. She works across academic research and applied theatre practice focused on conflict and development contexts.
