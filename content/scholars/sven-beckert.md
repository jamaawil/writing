Sven Beckert is a historian whose book Empire of Cotton offers a sweeping global history of the cotton industry, arguing that cotton production and trade were central to the emergence of global industrial capitalism, and that this system depended from its earliest stages on coercion, from slavery in the American South to colonial labor regimes across Egypt, India, and Central Asia, rather than on free markets and free labor alone. He terms this early, coercion-dependent phase "war capitalism," arguing that the eventual transition to industrial capitalism in Europe was built on foundations laid by this violent global cotton economy rather than emerging independently within Europe itself. His research draws on archival sources from multiple continents to trace cotton's role in connecting slavery, colonialism, and industrialization into a single global economic system. The book received the Bancroft Prize and has been influential in the broader "new history of capitalism" movement within American historical scholarship. He teaches history at Harvard University.
