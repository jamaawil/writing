bell hooks was an American author and social critic, writing under a deliberately lowercased pen name honoring her great-grandmother, whose book Ain't I a Woman offered a foundational Black feminist critique arguing that mainstream American feminism had historically centered the concerns and experiences of white, middle-class women while marginalizing Black women, even as Black liberation movements simultaneously often marginalized Black women's specific gendered concerns within a male-centered vision of racial struggle. She argued for an intersectional feminist analysis, developed well before that specific term became widely used, that took race, class, and gender together as mutually constitutive rather than separable categories of oppression and analysis. Her subsequent prolific body of work spanned feminist theory, pedagogy, love, and popular culture criticism, consistently written in an accessible, direct prose style intended to reach readers well beyond academic audiences. She taught at multiple institutions over her career, including as a distinguished professor at Berea College, while remaining one of the most widely read and publicly engaged feminist theorists of her generation. She died in 2021, having authored dozens of influential books across feminist theory, education, and cultural criticism.
