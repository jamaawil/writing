Robert Phillipson is a linguist whose book "Linguistic Imperialism" became a foundational text in critical language policy studies. He argues that the global spread of English has not been a neutral or purely voluntary process but has been actively promoted by British and American institutions as an instrument of cultural, economic, and political influence, often at the expense of local languages. His work examines how language education policy, funding, and international development programs have entrenched English's dominance in postcolonial societies. He situates contemporary debates over English-medium education within longer histories of colonial language policy. His scholarship remains central to debates over linguistic diversity, language rights, and the politics of global English.
