Kristin Ross is an American literary scholar and historian whose book Fast Cars, Clean Bodies examines postwar French culture, arguing that France's rapid modernization and consumer culture in the 1950s and 1960s served, in part, to help the nation psychologically manage and forget its brutal colonial war in Algeria, displacing collective reckoning with decolonization onto an embrace of new cars, appliances, and domestic comfort. She traces how cultural narratives of French modernity were constructed in ways that actively obscured the violence of empire happening simultaneously. Her broader scholarship examines the Paris Commune, May 1968, and the relationship between literature, politics, and everyday life in modern France. She combines close literary and cultural analysis with sustained attention to the political unconscious of national modernization narratives. She teaches comparative literature at New York University.
