Gordon Hempton is an American acoustic ecologist and sound recordist whose book One Square Inch of Silence documents his campaign to preserve a single, deliberately symbolic square inch of undisturbed natural quiet in Washington's Olympic National Park, arguing that truly silent natural soundscapes, free of human-generated noise, have become vanishingly rare and are worth protecting as an endangered resource in their own right. He combines memoir-style travel narrative with sustained advocacy for noise pollution regulation, treating silence not as mere absence of sound but as an ecological and experiential value under threat from expanding aviation, traffic, and industrial noise. His decades of field recording natural soundscapes around the world have made him a leading figure in acoustic ecology. His advocacy work has drawn attention to noise as an underrecognized form of environmental degradation. His writing bridges scientific concern for soundscapes with a more contemplative, almost spiritual attention to quiet.
