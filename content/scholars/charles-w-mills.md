Charles W. Mills was a Jamaican-born philosopher who spent his career in the United States, working at the intersection of analytic political philosophy, critical race theory, and Africana philosophy. He is best known for The Racial Contract, which argued that the social contract tradition running from Hobbes and Locke through Rawls has functioned historically as a racial contract, one that secured white supremacy while presenting itself as a raceless, universal theory of political legitimacy. Later books such as Blackness Visible, From Class to Race, and Black Rights/White Wrongs extended this critique, pressing mainstream liberal theory to reckon with slavery, colonialism, and Jim Crow rather than treating them as peripheral to its core concerns. His work gave the philosophy of race a rigorous foothold within mainstream Anglo-American philosophy departments, and it remains a touchstone for scholars linking liberal political theory to the historical structures of empire and racial domination. He died in 2021, having reshaped how a generation of philosophers think about race, rights, and the unfinished business of contractarian thought.
