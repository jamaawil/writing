R. Murray Schafer was a Canadian composer and educator who founded the discipline of acoustic ecology, the study of the relationship between living beings and their sonic environments. His book "The Soundscape" (also published as "The Tuning of the World") introduced the concept of the soundscape and argued for treating the total sonic environment, natural and human-made, as an object of aesthetic and ecological concern in its own right. He coined widely used terms in the field, including "schizophonia," to describe the separation of sound from its original source through recording and transmission technology. As a composer he wrote experimental and theatrical works that often incorporated environmental sound and site-specific performance. His work laid the intellectual groundwork for the fields of soundscape studies and sound art.
