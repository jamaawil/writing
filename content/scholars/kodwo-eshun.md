Kodwo Eshun is a British-Ghanaian writer, theorist, and filmmaker whose essay "Further Considerations on Afrofuturism" helped develop and formalize Afrofuturism as a critical framework, examining how Black artists across music, literature, and visual art have used science fiction imagery, imagined futures, and alternative technological histories to reimagine Black identity outside the constraints of a past defined by slavery and colonialism. He argues that Afrofuturist works do not simply escape into fantasy but perform serious speculative and political work, reclaiming the future as a site of Black possibility against historical narratives that confine Black experience to trauma and survival. His broader work spans music criticism, particularly on electronic and experimental Black music, and experimental documentary filmmaking through the collective The Otolith Group. He combines rigorous cultural theory with an artist's sensibility across multiple media. His writing remains foundational to Afrofuturist studies as an academic field.
