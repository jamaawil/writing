Wang Hui is a Chinese intellectual historian whose book China's New Order examines the transformations in Chinese political and economic thought following the country's post-1978 embrace of market reforms, arguing that this shift produced new and significant inequalities that mainstream Chinese political discourse, whether official or liberal, has struggled to adequately name or address. Associated with China's "New Left" intellectual current, he is critical both of unrestrained market liberalization and of any simple nostalgic return to Maoist-era politics, instead calling for renewed attention to questions of equality, democracy, and popular political participation within China's specific historical and institutional context. His broader scholarship examines the intellectual history of modern Chinese thought and its relationship to global currents of political theory. He has held prominent academic positions at Tsinghua University and has been an influential, sometimes controversial, voice in Chinese public intellectual debates over reform, inequality, and China's global role. His work is significant for offering an internal Chinese critical perspective on Chinese development rather than one framed primarily through external Western categories.
