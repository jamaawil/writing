Larry Tye is an American journalist and author whose book The Father of Spin examines the life and career of Edward Bernays, the pioneer of modern public relations who drew on his uncle Sigmund Freud's psychoanalytic theories to develop techniques of mass persuasion on behalf of corporations, governments, and political causes. Tye traces how Bernays's campaigns, from normalizing women's public smoking to shaping Cold War-era political messaging, helped establish the modern public relations industry and its underlying philosophy that democratic publics need to be professionally managed rather than simply informed. He situates Bernays's career within the broader twentieth-century history of advertising, propaganda, and mass communication. His other biographical work covers figures including Robert F. Kennedy and the Pullman porters of Rising from the Rails. His writing combines rigorous historical research with accessible narrative biography.
