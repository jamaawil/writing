Chip Colwell is an American archaeologist and museum curator whose work examines the ethics of collecting, displaying, and repatriating Indigenous human remains and cultural objects. His book Plundered Skulls and Stolen Spirits recounts the long history of museums acquiring Native American remains and sacred items, often through violence, deception, or the assumption that Indigenous peoples had no claim over their own dead. He also documents the more recent, often difficult process of repatriation under laws like the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act, drawing on his own experience negotiating returns as a museum curator. His writing combines institutional insider knowledge with sustained ethical reflection on what museums owe the communities whose heritage they hold. He has become an influential voice in debates over decolonizing museum practice.
