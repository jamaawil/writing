Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o is a Kenyan writer widely regarded as one of Africa's most significant living novelists and literary theorists, known for a body of fiction, including Something Torn and New, that examines the psychological and cultural fractures colonialism inflicted on Kenyan and broader African identity. His memoir and manifesto Decolonising the Mind argued that African writers should abandon European colonial languages in favor of writing in their own African languages, contending that continuing to write primarily in English or French, however skillfully, perpetuates a colonial relationship to language and thought itself, a position he put into practice by shifting his own fiction to writing first in Gikuyu. His imprisonment by the Kenyan government in the 1970s for his politically critical theatre work, recounted in Detained: A Writer's Prison Diary, reflects the direct political risks his writing has carried throughout his career. He has taught comparative literature and African studies at major American universities while remaining in exile from Kenya for extended periods. His work remains foundational to debates over language, decolonization, and African literary identity.
