Abeba Birhane is an Ethiopian-born cognitive scientist whose work sits at the intersection of complex adaptive systems, machine learning, and the politics of artificial intelligence. She is best known for research exposing how large-scale datasets used to train AI systems encode and amplify racial and colonial bias, arguing that supposedly neutral technical infrastructures reproduce older hierarchies of power under a new vocabulary of automation and efficiency. Her writing draws on relational and African philosophical traditions to challenge the individualist assumptions baked into mainstream data science and behavioral prediction. She has become an influential voice in debates over algorithmic auditing, dataset curation, and the ethics of AI research, pushing the field to reckon with whose knowledge and whose bodies are treated as raw material. Her interventions matter because they connect the technical particulars of machine learning pipelines to the longer history of extraction and classification that postcolonial and decolonial scholars have long analyzed in other domains.
