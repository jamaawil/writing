Richard Spruce was a British botanist and explorer who spent over a decade collecting plants in the Amazon basin and Andes during the mid-nineteenth century. His posthumously published "Notes of a Botanist on the Amazon and Andes" combines meticulous scientific observation with vivid travel narrative, documenting thousands of plant specimens as well as indigenous knowledge of medicinal and psychoactive plants. He played an important role in the transfer of cinchona, the source of quinine, from South America to British colonial plantations in Asia, a transfer with major consequences for imperial tropical medicine. His fieldwork exemplifies the close relationship between nineteenth-century botanical science and the commercial and administrative needs of empire. His detailed records remain valuable to botanists and historians of science alike.
