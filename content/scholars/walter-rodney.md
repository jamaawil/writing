Walter Rodney was a Guyanese historian and political activist whose book How Europe Underdeveloped Africa became one of the most influential works of dependency theory, arguing that Africa's contemporary poverty was not a natural or inevitable condition but the direct historical result of centuries of European exploitation, first through the slave trade and later through colonial extraction of resources and labor that actively developed Europe while systematically underdeveloping Africa. He contended that "underdevelopment" should be understood as an active historical process done to Africa by external forces rather than a passive starting condition Africa needed to overcome on its own. His later work, The Groundings with My Brothers, reflected his direct political organizing and popular education work among working-class communities in Jamaica and Guyana, connecting his historical scholarship to grassroots political consciousness-raising. He was assassinated in Guyana in 1980, widely believed to have been killed for his political organizing against the ruling government. His work remains foundational to dependency theory and Pan-African political economy.
