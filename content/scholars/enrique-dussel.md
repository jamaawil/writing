Enrique Dussel is an Argentine-Mexican philosopher and one of the founders of the philosophy of liberation, a tradition that seeks to think philosophically from the perspective of the colonized and oppressed rather than from the vantage point of European modernity. His book The Invention of the Americas argues that the conquest of the New World was constitutive of European modernity itself, not merely incidental to it, positioning 1492 as a founding moment of the modern world system built on domination. In Philosophy of Liberation and later work, he develops an ethics centered on the excluded "other" as the starting point for genuine philosophical and political thought, in explicit contrast to Eurocentric philosophical traditions. His extensive scholarship spans philosophy, theology, and the history of Latin American thought, and he has been a central influence on decolonial theory more broadly. He remains an active and prolific thinker, having taught for decades in Mexico.
