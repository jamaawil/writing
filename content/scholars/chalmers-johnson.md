Chalmers Johnson was an American political scientist and public intellectual who began his career as a Cold War-era Asia specialist before becoming one of the most trenchant critics of American empire writing in English. He is best known for his "Blowback" trilogy, which traced how the vast network of American overseas military bases, covert interventions, and clandestine operations generated the very forms of resentment and violent backlash it was meant to forestall, with the September 11 attacks giving his earlier warnings an unwelcome vindication. His later volumes extended the argument into a broader diagnosis of militarism eroding republican government at home, describing a national security state increasingly unaccountable to the citizens it claimed to protect. Trained in the study of Japan and East Asian political economy, Johnson brought a comparativist's eye to the study of American power, treating the United States as a case study in imperial overstretch rather than an exception to it. His work remains a touchstone for readers interested in the mechanics and costs of informal empire, garrisoned globalism, and the domestic consequences of permanent war footing.
