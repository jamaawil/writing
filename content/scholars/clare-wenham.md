Clare Wenham is a scholar of global health policy whose work examines how gender shapes the politics and practice of responding to epidemics and pandemics. Her research on feminist global health security argues that outbreak preparedness and response have historically ignored the gendered dimensions of disease spread and caregiving, from the disproportionate burden placed on women as frontline health workers and caregivers to the neglect of reproductive health services during crises like Ebola and COVID-19. She draws on case studies from multiple epidemics to show how gender-blind policy can worsen both health outcomes and existing inequalities. Her work has been influential in pushing global health institutions to incorporate gender analysis into pandemic planning. She continues to research and advise on health security policy at the London School of Economics.
