Naomi Klein is a Canadian journalist and author whose book The Shock Doctrine argues that free-market economic policies, including privatization, deregulation, and austerity, have historically been imposed most successfully during moments of crisis, whether natural disasters, wars, or political upheaval, when disoriented and traumatized populations are less able to mount effective resistance to rapid, often unpopular economic restructuring. She traces this pattern across numerous case studies, including Pinochet's Chile, post-Soviet Russia, and post-Katrina New Orleans, arguing that Milton Friedman's Chicago School of economics provided the intellectual framework for what she terms "disaster capitalism." Her earlier book No Logo examined corporate branding and anti-globalization activism, while her later work has turned to climate change and the political economy of environmental crisis. Her writing combines investigative journalism with sweeping historical and economic argument aimed at a broad general readership. She remains one of the most widely read contemporary critics of neoliberal economic policy.
