Nelson Maldonado-Torres is a Puerto Rican philosopher whose book Against War develops the concept of the "coloniality of being," extending Aníbal Quijano's coloniality of power framework to argue that colonialism produced not just economic and political domination but a lasting ontological hierarchy in which colonized peoples were and continue to be treated as less than fully human within Western thought and institutions. He examines war and violence as central, rather than exceptional, features of modernity's colonial foundations, arguing that the supposed peace of the modern world order has always depended on continuous war against colonized and racialized populations. His scholarship draws on Frantz Fanon, Emmanuel Levinas, and Latin American decolonial theory to develop this ontological critique of modernity. He has been an active organizer of decolonial theory networks and conferences alongside his scholarly writing. He teaches Latino and Caribbean studies and philosophy at Rutgers University.
