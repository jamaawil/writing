Carl A. P. Ruck is an American classicist known for controversial but influential scholarship on the role of psychoactive substances in ancient religion. Co-authored with Albert Hofmann and R. Gordon Wasson, The Road to Eleusis proposes that the ancient Greek Eleusinian Mysteries, one of antiquity's most important religious rites, involved a psychoactive potion derived from ergot fungus, offering a pharmacological explanation for the visionary experiences participants reportedly underwent. His broader scholarship examines the intersection of ethnobotany, mythology, and classical religion, arguing that altered states of consciousness played a larger role in ancient Mediterranean religious practice than mainstream classical scholarship has generally acknowledged. The thesis remains debated among classicists but has been highly influential in the study of entheogens and ancient religion. His work sits at an unusual crossroads of classics and psychedelic studies.
