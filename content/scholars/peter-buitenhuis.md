Peter Buitenhuis was a literary scholar who examined the entanglement of writers and propaganda during the First World War. His book "The Great War of Words" traces how British, American, and Canadian authors were recruited, formally and informally, into wartime propaganda efforts, and how this shaped a generation of war fiction and reportage. He was particularly interested in the moral and professional compromises literary figures made in service of state messaging, and in how propaganda techniques bled into ostensibly independent literary production. His work contributes to the broader history of information warfare and the mobilization of culture in the service of the state. It remains a reference point for scholars studying the relationship between literature, media, and war.
