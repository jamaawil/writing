Betsy Esch is a historian whose scholarship examines the transnational connections between Black radical movements in the United States and revolutionary politics in Asia during the twentieth century. Her essays on Afro-Asian solidarity, including work co-authored with Robin D. G. Kelley, trace how figures within the Black freedom movement drew inspiration from and built alliances with Chinese, Vietnamese, and other Asian revolutionary movements. This scholarship contributes to a broader historiographical turn examining Black internationalism beyond the U.S.-centric frame of the civil rights movement. Her work highlights how ideas of liberation circulated across the decolonizing world during the Cold War. It remains an important contribution to the study of Third World solidarity and Black radical internationalism.
