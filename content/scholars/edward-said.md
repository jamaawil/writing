Edward Said was a Palestinian-American literary and cultural critic whose 1978 book Orientalism is widely regarded as one of the founding texts of postcolonial studies. He argued that centuries of Western scholarship, art, and popular culture had constructed the "Orient" as a passive, exotic object to be studied, ruled, and possessed, a discourse that was inseparable from the machinery of colonial power rather than a neutral body of knowledge. He extended this analysis in Culture and Imperialism, showing how narrative forms like the novel helped naturalize empire even when they never addressed it directly. A longtime professor at Columbia University, he was also an outspoken public intellectual on Palestinian self-determination, writing books like The Question of Palestine and Covering Islam that connected his theoretical work to contemporary politics and media representation. His insistence on linking literary and cultural analysis to political consequence made him a defining figure for postcolonial and Middle East studies alike.
