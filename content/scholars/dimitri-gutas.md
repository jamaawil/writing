Dimitri Gutas is a scholar of classical Arabic and Islamic intellectual history whose book Greek Thought, Arabic Culture examines the great translation movement in which Greek philosophical and scientific texts were rendered into Arabic in eighth- through tenth-century Baghdad. He argues that this massive translation effort, sponsored by the Abbasid caliphate, was driven by specific political, cultural, and intellectual motivations rather than simple curiosity, and that it laid the foundation for centuries of original Arabic philosophical and scientific work later transmitted back to medieval Europe. His scholarship pushes back against narratives that treat Islamic civilization as a passive conduit for Greek knowledge, emphasizing instead the active, transformative intellectual labor involved. He has long taught Arabic and Islamic philosophy at Yale University. His work is foundational for understanding the historical relationship between Greek, Arabic, and later European philosophical traditions.
