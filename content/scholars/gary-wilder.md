Gary Wilder is a historian and anthropologist whose book Freedom Time examines the political thought of Négritude intellectuals Aimé Césaire and Léopold Sédar Senghor, arguing that their vision of decolonization was more radical and imaginative than the eventual outcome of national independence, since they at times imagined forms of federated, non-national political community that could preserve solidarity across the French empire's former colonies while dismantling its racial hierarchy. He treats this as a case of unrealized political possibility, a road not taken that offers resources for rethinking the limits of the nation-state as the only legitimate form of postcolonial sovereignty. His broader scholarship addresses French colonial history, republicanism, and the intellectual history of decolonization. He teaches history at the CUNY Graduate Center. His work is notable for taking seriously the political theory produced within anticolonial movements rather than treating it merely as background to eventual independence.
