Vincent Brown is a historian whose book Tacky's Revolt examines a major 1760 slave uprising in Jamaica, treating it not as an isolated local rebellion but as part of a broader, transatlantic war connecting West African political and military conflicts to the Caribbean plantation system, since many of the enslaved rebels brought military experience and political organization from wars in their African homelands. He argues that understanding slave rebellions requires tracing this Atlantic-spanning web of African political history, plantation labor systems, and imperial military conflict, rather than treating enslaved resistance as a purely reactive response to plantation conditions alone. His research combines detailed military history with attention to the specific African ethnic and political identities of the rebellion's participants. His broader scholarship, including earlier work on death and mourning in Jamaican slave society, examines the social and cultural world enslaved people built under conditions of extreme violence and mortality. He teaches history and African American studies at Harvard University.
