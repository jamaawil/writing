Lewis Gordon is an American philosopher and one of the leading figures in Africana philosophy and Fanon studies, whose book What Fanon Said offers a systematic philosophical reading of Frantz Fanon's entire body of work, situating him as a major existentialist and phenomenological thinker in his own right rather than merely a political theorist of anticolonial violence. Gordon argues that Fanon's psychiatric training and philosophical engagement with existentialism and phenomenology are essential to understanding his analysis of colonial racism's effects on Black consciousness, insisting on reading Fanon as rigorous philosophy rather than reducing him to a set of political slogans. His broader scholarship spans existentialism, philosophy of race, and Black existential thought more generally. He has held prominent academic positions in philosophy and Africana studies, including at the University of Connecticut. His work has been central to establishing Africana philosophy as a recognized field within academic philosophy.
