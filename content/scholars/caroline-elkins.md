Caroline Elkins is an American historian whose research exposed the scale of British colonial violence in Kenya. Imperial Reckoning documents the systematic use of detention camps, torture, and forced labor by British colonial authorities to suppress the Mau Mau uprising in the 1950s, drawing on archival research and survivor testimony that challenged the long-dominant, sanitized narrative of British decolonization as comparatively benign. Her scholarship played a significant role in the successful legal case brought by Kenyan torture survivors against the British government, which resulted in an official acknowledgment and compensation. The book won a Pulitzer Prize and reshaped historical and public understanding of the end of the British Empire. She remains a leading historian of the violence underlying British decolonization.
