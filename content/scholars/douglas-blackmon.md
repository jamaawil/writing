Douglas Blackmon is an American journalist and historian whose Pulitzer Prize-winning book Slavery by Another Name documents how, after the formal abolition of slavery, Southern states and businesses developed systems of convict leasing and debt peonage that re-enslaved tens of thousands of Black Americans well into the twentieth century. Through arrests on trivial or fabricated charges, forced labor camps, and complicit local courts, he shows how corporations, including major American companies, profited directly from this coerced labor system for decades after the Thirteenth Amendment. His investigative reporting drew on extensive archival research to reconstruct a history that had been largely erased from mainstream accounts of Reconstruction and its aftermath. The book and its accompanying documentary significantly shaped public understanding of the persistence of forced labor after emancipation. His work remains central to reckonings with corporate complicity in the long afterlife of slavery.
