Donna Haraway is an American scholar whose work sits at the crossroads of biology, feminist theory, and the history and philosophy of science. She first drew wide attention for her critique of primatology in "Primate Visions," which showed how ideas about race, gender, and empire shaped supposedly neutral scientific accounts of nature. She is best known for "A Cyborg Manifesto," a landmark essay that used the figure of the cyborg to unsettle rigid boundaries between human and machine, organism and technology, and nature and culture, and that became foundational reading across feminist theory, STS, and cultural studies. Later books such as "When Species Meet" and "Staying with the Trouble" extended her attention to companion species, multispecies entanglement, and ecological crisis, developing concepts like situated knowledges and sympoiesis. Her writing is prized for refusing easy dichotomies and insisting that science, technology, and politics are always bound up with questions of power, embodiment, and responsibility.
