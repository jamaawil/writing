Paul Gilroy is a British sociologist and cultural theorist best known for "The Black Atlantic: Modernity and Double Consciousness," which reconceives Black diasporic culture as a transnational, hybrid formation forged through the ship-borne circuits of the Atlantic rather than as an expression of any single national or ethnic tradition. The book argues against "ethnic absolutism," the tendency to treat Black culture as a bounded, essential inheritance, proposing instead a model of cultural exchange running through slavery, music, and political thought. His earlier work, including "There Ain't No Black in the Union Jack," examined race and nationalism in Britain. He has continued to write on race, humanism, and empire's legacies in books addressing what he calls "postcolonial melancholia" in contemporary Europe. His work is foundational to Black Atlantic and diaspora studies as an interdisciplinary field.
