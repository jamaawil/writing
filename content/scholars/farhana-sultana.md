Farhana Sultana is a geographer whose research examines the concept of climate coloniality, arguing that the global climate crisis cannot be understood apart from the histories of colonialism and racial capitalism that produced it and continue to shape who bears its costs. Her work traces how formerly colonized nations, despite contributing least to historical carbon emissions, face the most severe climate impacts while often being subjected to new forms of climate governance, aid conditionality, and green extractivism dictated by wealthier nations. She draws on feminist political ecology and South Asian case studies, particularly around water politics in Bangladesh, to ground these broader theoretical claims in specific struggles over resources. Her scholarship connects environmental justice movements with decolonial and postcolonial theory. She continues to publish widely on water governance, climate justice, and the political ecology of the Global South.
