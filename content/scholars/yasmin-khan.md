Yasmin Khan is a British historian whose book The Great Partition: The Making of India and Pakistan reframes the 1947 partition of British India not as an orderly transfer of power between the British and successor Indian and Pakistani governments but as a violent, chaotic, and largely improvised rupture whose human costs, mass displacement, sectarian violence, and lasting political division, continue to shape South Asian politics today. She emphasizes the experiences of ordinary refugees, women, and displaced communities alongside the high political negotiations between British, Congress, and Muslim League leadership that dominate more conventional accounts. Her subsequent work, The Raj at War, examines how the Second World War's demands on colonial India, including labor conscription, famine, and wartime repression, further destabilized British rule and accelerated the terms on which independence and partition eventually occurred. Her scholarship consistently insists on writing decolonization's history from the ground up rather than solely through elite political negotiation. She teaches history at the University of Oxford.
