Souleymane Bachir Diagne is a Senegalese philosopher who works across African philosophy, Islamic intellectual history, and francophone thought, long teaching at Columbia University after earlier posts in Senegal and France. In African Art as Philosophy, he reconsiders Négritude and the legacy of Léopold Sédar Senghor through Henri Bergson's philosophy of creativity and duration, arguing that African aesthetic and philosophical thought deserves to be read as rigorous philosophy in its own right rather than as ethnographic material. His writing returns often to translation as a philosophical act, tracing how concepts move and are transformed across African, Arabic, and European languages and traditions rather than simply diffusing outward from a single European center. In In Search of Africa(s), co-written with Jean-Loup Amselle, he takes up the tension between universalism and decolonial particularism, arguing for a plural, open universalism rather than either a Eurocentric or a narrowly nationalist alternative. He remains an influential voice in debates over decolonizing philosophy and bridging African, Islamic, and Western intellectual traditions.
