Jatin Dua is an anthropologist whose book Captured at Sea examines Somali piracy in the Indian Ocean not simply as criminal predation but as embedded within long-standing regional practices of protection, taxation, and maritime commerce that predate and outlast the specific piracy crisis of the 2000s and 2010s. He argues that understanding piracy requires attention to the wider political economy of the western Indian Ocean, including relationships between coastal communities, clan structures, and international shipping, rather than treating it purely as a security problem to be solved through naval intervention. His ethnographic fieldwork draws on extensive research in Somalia, Kenya, and among international anti-piracy naval forces to offer a multi-sided account of the piracy economy. His work situates Somali piracy within longer historical patterns of protection and exchange in the region. He teaches anthropology at the University of Michigan.
