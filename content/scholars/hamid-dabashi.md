Hamid Dabashi is an Iranian-American scholar of Islamic and Middle Eastern studies whose book Islamic Liberation Theology argues for reading political Islam not solely through the lens of terrorism or fundamentalism but as one strand within a broader global tradition of liberation theology, comparable to Latin American Christian liberation movements, that seeks emancipation from colonial and postcolonial domination. He is sharply critical both of Western Orientalist framings of Islam and of authoritarian political Islamist movements that he sees as betraying this emancipatory potential. His extensive scholarship spans Iranian cinema, comparative literature, and postcolonial theory, always combining rigorous academic argument with outspoken public commentary. He has long taught at Columbia University, where he holds an endowed chair in Iranian studies. His work is a consistent, sometimes polarizing, effort to reframe debates about Islam and the Middle East outside the terms set by the War on Terror.
