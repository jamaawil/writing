Taiaiake Alfred is a Kanien'kehá:ka (Mohawk) political theorist whose book Wasáse argues for Indigenous resurgence built on the active revival of traditional Indigenous governance, spirituality, and land relationships, rather than on seeking rights or recognition within settler colonial legal and political frameworks that he contends are fundamentally incompatible with genuine Indigenous self-determination. He is sharply critical of what he sees as an accommodationist tendency within some Indigenous rights movements to negotiate for limited concessions within colonial structures rather than pursuing more fundamental decolonization and the reconstitution of Indigenous nationhood on Indigenous terms. His work draws heavily on Haudenosaunee political philosophy and traditional governance principles as living resources for contemporary Indigenous resurgence rather than as historical artifacts. His scholarship has been influential and sometimes controversial within Indigenous studies for its uncompromising stance on rejecting negotiation with settler states. He has held academic positions in Indigenous governance at the University of Victoria.
