Urvashi Butalia is an Indian writer, publisher, and historian whose book The Other Side of Silence recovers the largely unrecorded personal experiences of ordinary people, particularly women, who lived through the violence and displacement of India's 1947 partition, work that was notably absent from official political histories focused on elite negotiations between colonial and nationalist leadership. Drawing on extensive oral history interviews conducted over many years, she documents experiences of sexual violence, forced conversion, and family separation that survivors, and often historians, had left largely unspoken due to shame, trauma, or the inconvenience such stories posed to triumphant nationalist narratives on both sides of the new border. Her work helped pioneer feminist oral history methodology within South Asian historiography specifically. She co-founded Kali for Women, India's first feminist publishing house, reflecting her broader career-long commitment to platforming women's voices and perspectives. Her scholarship remains foundational to the study of partition violence and historical memory in South Asia.
