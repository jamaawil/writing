Tariq Ali is a Pakistani-British writer, historian, and longtime left political activist whose books, including Bush in Babylon and The Clash of Fundamentalisms, offer sharply critical accounts of American and Western foreign policy in the Middle East, arguing that the rhetoric of democracy promotion and counterterrorism has repeatedly masked pursuit of strategic and economic interests, from oil access to regional dominance. His concept of "clash of fundamentalisms" deliberately reframes Samuel Huntington's influential "clash of civilizations" thesis, arguing that the real conflict is not between Islam and the West as civilizations but between different fundamentalist political projects, both religious and secular imperial, each exploiting the other to justify its own violence. A longtime editor of the New Left Review and prominent figure in the British and international New Left since the 1960s, he combines historical and political analysis with decades of direct activist engagement. His broader writing spans history, fiction, and political commentary across a prolific career. He remains an active and outspoken commentator on global politics from a consistently anti-imperialist perspective.
