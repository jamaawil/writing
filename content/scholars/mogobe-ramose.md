Mogobe Ramose was a South African philosopher whose book African Philosophy Through Ubuntu developed a systematic philosophical account of ubuntu, the Southern African ethical and metaphysical concept often summarized as "a person is a person through other persons," treating it not merely as a cultural value but as a rigorous alternative philosophical framework to Western individualist conceptions of personhood, ethics, and justice. He argued that ubuntu offers a coherent basis for rethinking law, politics, and reconciliation in postcolonial and post-apartheid African societies, one grounded in relational rather than atomistic conceptions of the self. His broader scholarship addressed the decolonization of philosophy as a discipline, arguing that African philosophy needed to be recognized and taught on its own terms rather than as a subfield of comparative Western philosophy. He held academic positions in South Africa and internationally throughout his career. His work remains foundational to ubuntu philosophy and African philosophical decolonization.
