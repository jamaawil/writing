Jason W. Moore is an environmental historian and world-systems theorist whose book Capitalism in the Web of Life argues that capitalism should be understood not simply as an economic system that acts upon a separate natural world, but as a way of organizing nature itself, one that has historically relied on appropriating "cheap" nature, labor, food, energy, and raw materials, to fuel its expansion. He proposes replacing the conventional term Anthropocene, which implies humanity as a whole is responsible for ecological crisis, with "Capitalocene," to emphasize that a specific economic system and its beneficiaries, rather than humanity in general, drove the current crisis. His work draws on world-systems theory and ecological Marxism to integrate environmental and economic history into a single analytical framework. He has been an influential, if debated, voice in environmental history and political ecology. He teaches sociology at Binghamton University.
