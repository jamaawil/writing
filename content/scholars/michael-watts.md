Michael Watts is a geographer whose book Curse of the Black Gold examines oil extraction in Nigeria's Niger Delta, documenting how decades of petroleum wealth extracted by multinational oil companies in partnership with the Nigerian state have brought environmental devastation and political violence to Delta communities while delivering little of the resulting revenue to the region itself. He develops the broader concept of the "resource curse," a paradox in which resource-rich regions and nations frequently experience worse governance, more conflict, and greater poverty than resource-poor areas, tracing how oil wealth concentrated in state hands can undermine accountable governance and fuel corruption and militarized conflict over control of revenue. His scholarship combines political ecology with detailed regional case study analysis of the Niger Delta specifically. His broader work addresses famine, agrarian change, and political ecology across Africa and beyond. He has taught geography at the University of California, Berkeley.
