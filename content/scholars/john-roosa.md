John Roosa is a historian whose book Pretext for Mass Murder examines the events of September 30, 1965, in Indonesia, an alleged coup attempt that was used to justify the Indonesian military's subsequent mass killing of hundreds of thousands of suspected communists and the establishment of Suharto's decades-long dictatorship. He critically reassesses the standard official narrative of the coup's origins, questioning both Indonesian military accounts and the historical record more broadly, while situating the massacre within Cold War-era anticommunist violence often supported or tolerated by Western governments. His work draws on close archival and documentary analysis to challenge simplified explanations of one of the twentieth century's largest and least internationally acknowledged mass killings. He has contributed to broader efforts at historical reckoning with the 1965-66 violence in Indonesia. He teaches history at the University of British Columbia.
