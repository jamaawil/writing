Catherine Lutz is an American anthropologist whose research examines militarism, empire, and everyday life around U.S. military installations. The Bases of Empire, which she co-edited, brings together case studies of American overseas military bases across the world, examining their social, environmental, and political effects on host communities and the broader architecture of American global power. Her work treats military infrastructure as a lens for understanding the informal and often underexamined dimensions of empire that operate through bases and logistics rather than direct colonial administration. She combines ethnographic fieldwork with structural analysis of U.S. global military presence. Her scholarship has been influential in anthropology's turn toward studying militarism and empire as everyday, lived phenomena.
