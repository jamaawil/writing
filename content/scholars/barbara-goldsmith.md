Barbara Goldsmith was an American writer and journalist known for biographical works on notable historical figures. Obsessive Genius: The Inner World of Marie Curie offers a portrait of the physicist that pays close attention to the personal costs and social obstacles she faced as a woman working in early twentieth-century science, alongside her scientific achievements in radioactivity. Goldsmith's writing style favored accessible narrative biography aimed at a general audience over specialist scientific history. Her broader body of work included biographies and social histories of nineteenth and twentieth-century American figures and movements. The Curie biography remains a widely read popular account of women's history in science.
