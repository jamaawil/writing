Michelle Alexander is an American legal scholar and civil rights lawyer whose book The New Jim Crow argues that mass incarceration in the United States, disproportionately targeting Black men through the War on Drugs, functions as a new racial caste system that has replaced, rather than eliminated, the explicit legal segregation of the Jim Crow era. She contends that the criminal justice system's ostensibly race-neutral language obscures its systematically racially discriminatory effects, from policing patterns to sentencing disparities to the lifelong disenfranchisement and discrimination that felony convictions impose even after release from prison. The book became enormously influential in shaping public understanding of mass incarceration as a civil rights issue and helped catalyze broader criminal justice reform movements. Her analysis situates contemporary policing and incarceration within the long historical arc of American racial control systems evolving in response to civil rights victories. She continues to write and advocate on criminal justice reform and racial justice.
