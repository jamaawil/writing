Johannes Fabian is a Dutch-Belgian anthropologist whose book Time and the Other delivered an influential and enduring critique of how anthropology as a discipline has represented the peoples it studies. He argued that anthropological writing has habitually denied its subjects "coevalness," placing them rhetorically in an earlier stage of human development or a timeless ethnographic present even while the anthropologist studies them in the same historical moment, a temporal distancing that reinforced colonial hierarchies between observer and observed. This critique became foundational to later reflexive and postcolonial turns within anthropology, pushing the discipline toward greater self-awareness about its own colonial entanglements and representational practices. His broader ethnographic work focused on language, performance, and popular culture in the Democratic Republic of Congo. He held academic positions in the United States for much of his career. His work remains essential reading in the history and theory of anthropology.
