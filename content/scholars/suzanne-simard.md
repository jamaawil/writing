Suzanne Simard is a Canadian forest ecologist whose book Finding the Mother Tree recounts her decades of research demonstrating that trees in a forest are connected through underground fungal networks, known as mycorrhizal networks, through which they share nutrients, water, and chemical signals, effectively communicating and supporting one another across species in ways that challenge simple models of trees as purely competitive individual organisms. Her research identified the existence of "mother trees," older, more established trees that appear to preferentially support younger and related seedlings through these fungal networks, a finding with significant implications for forestry practices that traditionally treated individual trees as isolated units of resource competition. Her memoir combines this scientific research with a personal account of her career navigating a male-dominated forestry science establishment often resistant to her findings. Her work has been influential both scientifically and in broader popular understanding of forest ecosystems as cooperative, interconnected systems. She teaches forest ecology at the University of British Columbia.
