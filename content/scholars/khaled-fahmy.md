Khaled Fahmy is an Egyptian historian whose book All the Pasha's Men examines the creation of the modern Egyptian army under Muhammad Ali in the early nineteenth century, arguing that this military modernization project was central to the broader formation of the modern Egyptian state and its relationship to its own population. He shows how conscription, military discipline, and new medical and administrative institutions built around the army reshaped Egyptian society well beyond the battlefield, extending state power deep into everyday life in ways that both modernized and constrained ordinary Egyptians. His scholarship draws on close archival research in Ottoman and Egyptian sources to complicate standard narratives that credit Muhammad Ali's reforms straightforwardly as enlightened modernization. His broader work addresses the history of crime, punishment, and everyday life in nineteenth-century Egypt. He has held academic positions at Cambridge and in the United States, and remains a leading historian of the modern Middle East.
