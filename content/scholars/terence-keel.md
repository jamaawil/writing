Terence Keel is a historian of science and religion whose book Divine Variations examines how Christian theological assumptions about human nature and difference continued to shape supposedly secular scientific race science well into the modern era, challenging accounts that treat the history of scientific racism as a purely secular development disconnected from religious thought. He traces how ideas about divinely ordained human variation and hierarchy persisted, often unacknowledged, within the ostensibly objective methods of biology and anthropology as these fields sought scientific explanations for human racial difference. His work bridges the history of science, religious studies, and critical race theory, arguing for a more historically honest account of how theological and scientific racism have been intertwined rather than sequential or separate developments. His broader scholarship continues to examine the entanglement of race, religion, and science in American intellectual history. He teaches at the University of California, Los Angeles.
