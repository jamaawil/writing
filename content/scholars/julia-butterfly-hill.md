Julia Butterfly Hill is an American environmental activist who became internationally known for living for over two years in the canopy of an ancient redwood tree she named Luna, in Northern California, to prevent Pacific Lumber Company from cutting it down. Her memoir The Legacy of Luna recounts this extended tree-sit, describing both the physical hardships of sustained platform living high in the canopy and the broader campaign against old-growth redwood logging that her protest came to symbolize. Her action succeeded in securing a permanent easement protecting Luna and a buffer zone around it, making her one of the most visible figures in the redwood preservation movement of the 1990s. She has continued environmental and social justice activism since descending from the tree. Her prolonged, highly public act of civil disobedience remains one of the most widely recognized episodes in American environmental direct action.
