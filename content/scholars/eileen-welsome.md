Eileen Welsome is an American investigative journalist whose Pulitzer Prize-winning reporting and book The Plutonium Files exposed a series of secret Cold War-era experiments in which the United States government injected unwitting patients, including hospital patients and children, with plutonium and other radioactive materials to study their effects on the human body. Her years of archival research and interviews with survivors and their families uncovered a program that had been buried in classified records for decades, and her reporting directly contributed to congressional investigations and a formal government apology. The book situates these experiments within the broader ethical failures of Cold War nuclear research, in which scientific and military urgency repeatedly overrode informed consent. Her work stands as a landmark case study in investigative journalism's capacity to force government accountability. It remains a key text on the human costs of secret state science.
