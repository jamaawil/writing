Nicholas Shaxson is a British journalist whose book Poisoned Wells examines the political and economic consequences of oil wealth in several African nations, arguing that oil revenue, rather than driving development, has frequently entrenched corrupt elites, fueled conflict, and left ordinary citizens worse off, a pattern he connects to the broader phenomenon of the resource curse. His later, highly influential book Treasure Islands extended this analysis of extractive wealth into a systematic investigation of offshore tax havens, arguing that a globally interconnected network of secrecy jurisdictions facilitates the flight of wealth from both developed and developing economies alike, undermining tax systems and democratic accountability worldwide. His investigative journalism combines detailed case study reporting with broader structural analysis of how global financial architecture enables extraction and concealment of wealth. His work has been influential in shaping public and policy debates over tax havens and offshore finance. He continues to write and advocate on issues of financial transparency and tax justice.
