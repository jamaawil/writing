Albert Hofmann was a Swiss chemist who first synthesized LSD in 1938 while researching ergot alkaloids at Sandoz Laboratories, and who famously discovered its psychoactive properties after accidentally absorbing it in 1943. His memoir LSD: My Problem Child recounts that discovery and his subsequent decades of reflection on the drug's promise and misuse, arguing for its careful therapeutic and spiritual application against its recreational and countercultural excesses. With Gordon Wasson and Carl Ruck he co-authored The Road to Eleusis, proposing that the ancient Eleusinian Mysteries involved an ergot-derived psychoactive potion, a controversial but influential thesis in the study of ancient religion. His work sits at the crossroads of pharmacology, the history of psychedelics, and the study of altered states of consciousness. He remains the central figure in any history of psychedelic science.
