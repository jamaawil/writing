Giorgio Agamben is an Italian philosopher whose book State of Exception examines how modern states, including ostensibly liberal democracies, repeatedly suspend ordinary legal and constitutional protections during declared emergencies, creating a zone in which sovereign power operates outside the very law it claims to uphold. Drawing on Carl Schmitt and Walter Benjamin, he argues that the state of exception has become less an occasional emergency measure and more a permanent, normalized technique of contemporary governance, visible in phenomena from indefinite detention to expansive executive war powers. This work extends his broader Homo Sacer project, which theorizes how modern politics increasingly governs through the production of "bare life," people stripped of political standing and rendered exposed to sovereign power without legal protection. His dense, historically erudite style draws on classical, theological, and legal sources across a long philosophical career. His work has been widely influential, and contested, across political philosophy, legal theory, and critical theory.
