Amartya Sen is an Indian economist and philosopher awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for his contributions to welfare economics. Poverty and Famines overturned the assumption that famines are caused simply by food shortages, showing through case studies including the 1943 Bengal famine that they are frequently produced by failures of entitlement and distribution even amid adequate food supply. This entitlement approach reshaped how economists and policymakers think about hunger, development, and social provisioning. He later developed the capability approach with Martha Nussbaum, reframing development as the expansion of substantive human freedoms rather than income growth alone. His work bridges economics, ethics, and political philosophy in ways that have influenced global development policy.
