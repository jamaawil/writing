Patrick Chabal was a historian and political scientist specializing in African politics, notably lusophone Africa. His biography of Amílcar Cabral examines the Guinea-Bissauan revolutionary's political thought and leadership of the independence struggle against Portuguese colonial rule. With Jean-Pascal Daloz, he co-authored "Africa Works," an influential and controversial argument that political disorder in many African states functions as a deliberate instrument of rule rather than simply a symptom of institutional failure. His scholarship pushed against developmentalist assumptions that treated African political forms as deviations from a Western institutional norm. He was an influential figure in African studies in Britain until his death in 2014.
