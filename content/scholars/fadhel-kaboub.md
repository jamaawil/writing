Fadhel Kaboub is a Tunisian-American economist whose work applies Modern Monetary Theory to the specific constraints facing developing economies, particularly in Africa and the Middle East. His research on monetary sovereignty argues that many postcolonial states remain structurally dependent on foreign currency, debt, and export commodities in ways that limit their real policy independence, regardless of formal political sovereignty, and he examines arrangements like the CFA franc as concrete instances of this constrained sovereignty. He advocates for policies that build domestic productive capacity and food and energy security as preconditions for genuine economic self-determination. As president of the Global Institute for Sustainable Prosperity, he works at the intersection of academic economics and policy advocacy. His scholarship connects heterodox monetary theory directly to debates over decolonization and economic justice in the Global South.
