Patrick Wolfe was an Australian historian and anthropologist who became one of the foundational theorists of settler colonial studies as a distinct field of analysis. His widely cited essay "Settler Colonialism and the Elimination of the Native" argued that settler colonialism should be understood not as a past historical event but as an ongoing structure organized around the elimination, rather than exploitation, of indigenous populations, distinguishing it from colonial rule built on extracting labor. This formulation gave scholars of Indigenous dispossession in places like Australia, North America, and Palestine a shared conceptual vocabulary. His work has been central to debates over the relationship between race, land, and sovereignty in settler societies. He remains one of the most influential reference points for anyone writing about settler colonialism today.
