Paul Farmer was an American physician and medical anthropologist who cofounded Partners In Health, an organization delivering healthcare to some of the world's poorest communities, most prominently in Haiti. His book "Pathologies of Power" develops the concept of structural violence to explain how poverty, racism, and political inequality are embedded in the distribution of illness and death, arguing that human rights frameworks must be linked to material questions of health access. He combined clinical practice with scholarly and public advocacy, working across Haiti, Peru, Rwanda, and elsewhere to challenge the idea that complex medical care was unsuitable for poor settings. His writing insists that global health disparities are the product of specific political and economic choices rather than natural facts. He died in 2022, leaving a body of work central to critical global health scholarship.
