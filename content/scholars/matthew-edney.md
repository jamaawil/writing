Matthew Edney is a historian of cartography whose book Mapping an Empire examines the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India, the massive British project to precisely map the Indian subcontinent during the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. He argues that this survey was never simply a neutral scientific undertaking but was fundamentally an instrument of colonial control, one that helped constitute India as a legible, governable territorial object in British eyes even as the survey's own maps homogenized and simplified the vast diversity of actual Indian geography and society. His work builds on and extends J. B. Harley's critical approach to cartography, applying it in extensive archival detail to a single major imperial mapping project. He shows how the technical, scientific prestige of surveying helped legitimate colonial rule as rational and orderly administration. He teaches history and holds the position of Osher Curator of Cartography at the University of Southern Maine.
