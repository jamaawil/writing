C. L. R. James was a Trinidadian historian, journalist, and Marxist theorist whose work profoundly shaped Pan-Africanist and Black radical thought. The Black Jacobins is his landmark history of the Haitian Revolution, centering Toussaint Louverture and the enslaved people who overthrew French colonial rule to argue that the revolution was a central, not marginal, event in the history of the Atlantic world and of revolutionary politics generally. His writing combined rigorous historical research with an explicitly anticolonial and revolutionary political commitment, and he was active in Pan-African and Trotskyist political circles across Britain, the U.S., and the Caribbean. He also wrote Beyond a Boundary, an unusual and influential work using cricket as a lens for examining colonialism, class, and culture in the Caribbean. He remains one of the most important Black intellectuals of the twentieth century.
