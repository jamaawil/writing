Frank Dikötter is a Dutch historian of modern China whose book The Age of Openness challenges standard narratives of early twentieth-century China as uniformly closed and xenophobic, arguing instead that the Republican era was marked by significant cosmopolitan exchange, cultural experimentation, and engagement with the outside world before the Communist Party's later consolidation of power. He is also known for a trilogy on Mao-era China, including Mao's Great Famine, which used newly available archival sources to document the catastrophic human costs of the Great Leap Forward with unusual empirical detail. His work generally pushes against simplified or ideologically convenient narratives of Chinese history, insisting on close attention to primary sources over received wisdom. He has taught at the University of Hong Kong for much of his career. His scholarship has been influential, if occasionally controversial, in reshaping accounts of twentieth-century Chinese history.
