Linda Tuhiwai Smith is a Māori scholar whose book Decolonizing Methodologies became a foundational text for Indigenous studies and critical research methodology worldwide, arguing that Western research practices have historically functioned as instruments of colonial power, treating Indigenous peoples and their knowledge as objects to be studied, categorized, and extracted from rather than as agents with their own legitimate claims to self-representation. She calls for research conducted by and accountable to Indigenous communities themselves, grounded in Indigenous values and protocols rather than imposed Western academic frameworks that often disregard Indigenous concerns about consent, benefit, and cultural protocol. Her work has been enormously influential across Indigenous studies globally, providing a shared vocabulary and set of concerns for Indigenous researchers working in vastly different national and cultural contexts. She has held senior academic leadership positions in New Zealand, including at the University of Waikato. Her book remains one of the most widely cited texts in Indigenous and decolonial research methodology.
