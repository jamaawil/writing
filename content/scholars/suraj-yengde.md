Suraj Yengde is an Indian Dalit scholar and activist whose book Caste Matters combines memoir, political essay, and social analysis to examine the persistence of caste discrimination in contemporary India and across the global Dalit diaspora, arguing that caste continues to structure economic opportunity, social status, and violence in ways that mainstream Indian and international discourse often minimizes or ignores. Drawing on his own experience growing up as a Dalit in India before pursuing graduate education internationally, he examines both the specific texture of everyday caste discrimination and the broader political movements, drawing on figures like B. R. Ambedkar, that have organized against it. His work situates caste as a global rather than purely Indian phenomenon, tracing its presence within the Indian diaspora and connecting Dalit liberation struggles to broader antiracist and anticolonial movements internationally. He has held academic positions at Harvard University focused on caste and Indian political thought. His writing brings Dalit perspectives directly into international scholarly and public conversation.
