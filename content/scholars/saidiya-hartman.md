Saidiya Hartman is an American scholar whose work reimagines how the lives of enslaved and formerly enslaved people, largely absent from the official archive, might be narrated. Her book "Lose Your Mother" combines personal travel narrative with historical inquiry as she journeys along a former slave route in Ghana, confronting both the impossibility and the necessity of recovering a lost genealogical past. In "Wayward Lives, Beautiful Experiments," she develops what she calls "critical fabulation," a method of speculative narration that imaginatively fills the archival silences surrounding the lives of young Black women in early twentieth-century American cities, without claiming false certainty about facts the record cannot supply. Her earlier book "Scenes of Subjection" examined how supposedly benevolent practices after emancipation continued to constrain Black freedom. Her innovative methodology has been widely influential across literary studies, history, and Black studies for grappling with the violence of the archive itself.
