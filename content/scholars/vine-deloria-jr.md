Vine Deloria Jr. was a Standing Rock Sioux writer, lawyer, and activist whose book Custer Died for Your Sins delivered a sharp, often satirical critique of federal Indian policy, anthropology's treatment of Native peoples, and white American liberal paternalism toward Indigenous communities, becoming a foundational and widely influential text of the Red Power movement. His later book God Is Red argued that Indigenous religious and philosophical traditions, grounded in specific relationships to land and place rather than abstract universal doctrine, offered a coherent and valuable alternative to Western Christian and scientific worldviews, particularly regarding humanity's relationship to the natural world. His writing combined rigorous legal and political analysis of treaty rights and federal Indian law with broader philosophical and theological argument for the validity of Indigenous ways of knowing. He was a highly influential public intellectual and organizer central to the resurgence of Native American political and cultural assertion beginning in the late 1960s. His work remains foundational to Native American studies and Indigenous political thought.
