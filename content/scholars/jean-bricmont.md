Jean Bricmont is a Belgian physicist and political commentator whose book Humanitarian Imperialism argues that the rhetoric of human rights and humanitarian intervention has been repeatedly used since the end of the Cold War to justify Western military interventions that serve geopolitical and economic interests rather than genuine humanitarian goals. He is sharply critical of what he sees as a liberal-left embrace of interventionist foreign policy under humanitarian cover, drawing parallels to earlier imperial justifications for conquest framed as civilizing missions. Trained as a theoretical physicist, he became known outside his own field for co-authoring a critique of the misuse of scientific concepts in postmodern theory before turning to political writing on foreign policy and free speech. His positions on intervention and international politics have been controversial and widely debated. He has taught theoretical physics at the Catholic University of Louvain.
