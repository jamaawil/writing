Jay Kirk is an American writer whose book Kingdom Under Glass tells the story of Carl Akeley, the naturalist and taxidermist whose innovative techniques transformed museum diorama displays and who conducted expeditions to Africa to collect specimens, including gorillas, for major American natural history museums. The book examines Akeley's obsessive pursuit of capturing what he saw as a vanishing natural world through taxidermy, while also probing the era's often violent and ethically fraught relationship between Western scientific institutions and African wildlife and landscapes. Kirk's narrative nonfiction style blends adventure storytelling with a more critical eye toward the colonial-era assumptions embedded in early twentieth-century natural history collecting. He teaches creative writing and has contributed longform journalism to various American magazines. His work sits within a broader genre of narrative nonfiction reexamining the history of natural history museums and scientific collecting.
