Mary Harper is a British journalist and the BBC's long-serving Africa editor whose book Getting Somalia Wrong? challenges dominant international media narratives that portray Somalia solely as a failed state defined by piracy, famine, and terrorism, arguing instead that beneath the visible chaos, Somali society has continued to function through resilient clan networks, a thriving informal economy, and an increasingly connected global diaspora. She contends that international interventions have often failed precisely because they misunderstand these underlying Somali social and economic structures, imposing external state-building models poorly suited to Somali realities. Her account draws on decades of reporting and direct engagement with Somali communities both in Somalia and across the diaspora. Her work pushes back against simplistic "failed state" framings common in international media and policy circles. She remains one of the most experienced Western journalists covering the Horn of Africa.
