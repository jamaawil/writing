Peter Moore was an American photographer best known for his extensive documentation of the New York avant-garde art and performance scene of the 1960s, including Judson Dance Theater and Fluxus happenings. His photographs of the demolition of the original Pennsylvania Station, collected in the book "The Destruction of Penn Station," became one of the most striking visual records of a building's loss and helped galvanize the American historic preservation movement that followed. His work occupies an unusual position between art documentation and architectural history, since so few comprehensive photographic records exist of the demolition of a major public building. The images are frequently invoked in discussions of urban renewal, civic memory, and the political stakes of what a city chooses to preserve. He died in 1993, but his archive remains a key visual resource for art and architectural historians alike.
