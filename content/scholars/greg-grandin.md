Greg Grandin is an American historian whose work examines the long history of United States intervention in Latin America and its ideological justifications. His book Empire's Workshop argues that Latin America served as a testing ground for policies and doctrines, from counterinsurgency to free-market economic restructuring, that the United States later exported to other regions, making the hemisphere a kind of workshop for American empire. In The Last Colonial Massacre, he examines the 1954 CIA-backed coup in Guatemala and its long, bloody aftermath as a case study in Cold War intervention's human costs. His broader scholarship, including The End of the Myth, situates American expansionism within a longer history stretching from the frontier to the border wall. He teaches history at Yale University. His work consistently connects specific historical episodes to larger arguments about the ideological structure of American empire.
