Kwame Anthony Appiah is a Ghanaian-British-American philosopher whose memoir-inflected book In My Father's House examines questions of African identity, Pan-Africanism, and race through reflection on his own family history spanning Ghana and England, ultimately arguing against essentialist notions of racial or pan-African identity in favor of more cosmopolitan and individually grounded conceptions of belonging. His later book The Lies That Bind extends this critique to identity categories more broadly, including nationality, religion, and culture, arguing that such categories are far more constructed, contested, and internally diverse than popular political rhetoric typically allows. His broader philosophical work spans ethics, political philosophy, and the philosophy of race, consistently favoring nuanced cosmopolitanism over rigid identity politics from any direction. He has taught philosophy and law at Princeton and New York University. He is also a prominent public intellectual, writing an ongoing ethics advice column for the New York Times.
