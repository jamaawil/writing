Nell Irvin Painter is an American historian whose book The History of White People traces how the very category of "whiteness" has been repeatedly redefined and contested over centuries, showing that groups now considered unambiguously white, including Irish, Italian, and Jewish immigrants to the United States, were historically excluded from full whiteness and subjected to significant racial discrimination before eventually being incorporated into the category. Her research demonstrates that racial categories, far from being fixed biological facts, are historically contingent social and political constructions that have expanded and contracted according to shifting economic and political needs. Her broader scholarship spans African American history and the history of slavery, including a notable biography of Sojourner Truth. She taught history for many years at Princeton University before pursuing a second career as a visual artist later in life. Her work remains widely cited in the study of race as a constructed rather than natural category.
