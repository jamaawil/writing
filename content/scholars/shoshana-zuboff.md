Shoshana Zuboff is an American scholar whose book "The Age of Surveillance Capitalism" named and theorized a new economic logic in which technology companies extract behavioral data from users as raw material, refine it into predictive products, and sell the ability to modify future behavior, typically without meaningful user consent or awareness. She argues that this "surveillance capitalism" represents a novel and dangerous mutation of capitalism itself, one that threatens both individual autonomy and democratic self-governance. Her earlier academic career focused on the psychological and organizational effects of information technology in the workplace. Her later work synthesizes economics, psychology, and technology criticism into an influential and widely debated account of the political economy of the internet. Her framing of surveillance capitalism has become a standard reference point in debates over big tech regulation.
