Homer is the name traditionally given to the composer, or composers, of the Iliad and the Odyssey, the foundational epic poems of ancient Greek literature, though his historical existence and the precise circumstances of the poems' composition remain uncertain and debated among classicists. The Odyssey recounts the long, obstacle-strewn journey of Odysseus home from the Trojan War, a narrative structure of exile, trial, and return that has influenced storytelling across every subsequent era of Western literature. Scholars generally believe the poems drew on a long oral tradition of formulaic verse composition before being fixed in something like their current written form sometime in the eighth century BCE. The Homeric epics shaped Greek education, religion, and identity for over a thousand years and remain central to the study of epic poetry, oral tradition, and the classical world. Their influence extends through the entire subsequent history of world literature.
