Vincent Bevins is an American journalist whose book The Jakarta Method documents the United States government's support for a wave of anticommunist mass killings and coups across the Global South during the Cold War, taking its title from the 1965-66 Indonesian massacres in which hundreds of thousands of suspected communists were killed with American intelligence support, an operation that Bevins argues became a template subsequently applied, with American backing, in Latin America and elsewhere. He argues that this systematic pattern of supporting anticommunist violence, largely overlooked in mainstream American historical memory, was foundational to establishing the post-Cold War global economic order rather than an incidental byproduct of Cold War competition. His investigative reporting draws on interviews with survivors across multiple countries alongside archival research to reconstruct this transnational pattern of violence. His work situates these episodes within a broader reassessment of how the Cold War's "hot" violence in the Global South shaped the contemporary world order. He continues investigative journalism focused on global politics and historical memory.
