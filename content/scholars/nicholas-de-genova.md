Nicholas De Genova is an anthropologist whose edited volume examining The Borders of "Europe" argues that European border and migration policy should be understood as an active site of ongoing political struggle and violence rather than a neutral administrative boundary, one that produces the very categories of "illegality" it then polices. He develops the concept of the "border spectacle," arguing that the highly visible, often militarized policing of certain borders serves symbolic and political functions, dramatizing sovereignty and threat, that exceed their practical effectiveness at actually preventing migration. His broader scholarship examines immigration enforcement, deportation, and the racialization of migrant "illegality" in the United States and Europe alike. His work draws on critical border studies and postcolonial theory to challenge naturalized assumptions about state sovereignty and migration control. He has held academic positions across the United States and Europe focused on migration and border studies.
