Denis Wood is a geographer and cartographer whose book The Power of Maps argues that maps are never neutral representations of space but are always shaped by the interests, choices, and omissions of their makers. He shows how decisions about what to include, exclude, emphasize, or label on a map encode particular political and social claims, from national borders to advertising and tourism maps. His work draws attention to the map as a rhetorical object that naturalizes certain views of territory and power while obscuring others. He has also produced experimental, alternative mapping projects that foreground the perspectives and histories usually left off conventional maps. His scholarship has been influential in critical cartography and the broader study of how visual representations of space shape political understanding.
