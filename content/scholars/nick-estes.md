Nick Estes is a citizen of the Lower Brule Sioux Tribe and a historian whose book Our History Is the Future situates the 2016 Standing Rock protests against the Dakota Access Pipeline within a much longer history of Indigenous resistance to settler colonial dispossession and environmental destruction on the Great Plains, stretching back through generations of treaty violations, forced removal, and struggle over the Missouri River itself. He argues that Indigenous resistance movements should be understood not as isolated, reactive protests but as continuous expressions of Indigenous political traditions and land relationships that long predate and will long outlast any single confrontation with a particular pipeline or policy. His work draws on oral history, archival research, and his own direct participation in the Standing Rock movement to bridge scholarly history and activist testimony. His scholarship has been influential in Indigenous studies and environmental justice movements alike. He teaches American studies at the University of Minnesota.
