W. Boyd Rayward is a historian of library and information science whose book The Universe of Information reconstructs the life and work of Paul Otlet, the Belgian visionary who, in the early twentieth century, developed the Universal Decimal Classification system and envisioned a global network for organizing and sharing all human knowledge, an ambition often described as an early conceptual precursor to the internet and hypertext. Rayward's scholarship documents Otlet's extensive but ultimately underfunded and largely forgotten institutional efforts, including the Mundaneum project in Brussels, to build the physical and organizational infrastructure for this universal information system decades before the technology existed to realize it. His research helped recover Otlet's historical significance for scholars of information science and documentation history, a field in which Otlet's contributions had been substantially overshadowed by later, more commercially successful information technologies. His broader work addresses the history of documentation and information organization as a distinct intellectual tradition preceding modern computer science. He has held academic positions in library and information science, including at the University of Illinois and University of New South Wales.
