R. Gordon Wasson was an American banker turned amateur mycologist whose fieldwork among the Mazatec people of Mexico brought psilocybin mushrooms and their ritual use to Western scientific and popular attention in the mid-twentieth century. Working with the chemist Albert Hofmann and classicist Carl Ruck, he co-authored "The Road to Eleusis," which controversially proposed that the ancient Eleusinian Mysteries of Greece involved a psychoactive substance derived from ergot, arguing that altered states of consciousness lay at the root of much ancient religious experience. His work helped found the field of ethnomycology and shaped later scholarly and popular interest in psychedelics, comparative religion, and the anthropology of altered states. Though some of his specific claims about Eleusis remain contested among classicists, his broader influence on the study of psychoactive substances and religion is widely acknowledged. He combined amateur enthusiasm with genuine scholarly rigor in a way that opened an entire field of inquiry.
