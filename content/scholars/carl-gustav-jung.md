Carl Gustav Jung was a Swiss psychiatrist and the founder of analytical psychology, one of the most influential figures in the history of depth psychology alongside his early collaborator and later rival Sigmund Freud. His concepts of the collective unconscious, archetypes, and individuation reshaped how psychology approached myth, religion, and symbolism as expressions of universal psychic structures rather than purely personal neurosis. Memories, Dreams, Reflections, compiled from his own recollections late in life, offers an intimate account of his inner development and the experiences that shaped his theories. The Red Book, a private journal of visionary experiences kept during a period of intense personal crisis and only published posthumously, reveals the psychological and symbolic material from which much of his later theoretical work grew. His influence extends well beyond clinical psychology into literature, religious studies, and the study of myth.
