Daniel Chamovitz is an Israeli plant biologist whose book What a Plant Knows explores the sensory capacities of plants, examining how they perceive light, smell, touch, and their surroundings despite lacking brains or nervous systems in the animal sense. Drawing on his own research in molecular biology alongside a wide survey of plant science, he argues that plants possess sophisticated, if fundamentally different, ways of sensing and responding to their environment. The book is written for a general audience and has been influential in popularizing plant science and reshaping public conceptions of plant behavior and cognition. He has served as director of Tel Aviv University's Manna Center for Plant Biosciences. His work sits at the intersection of serious research and accessible science communication.
