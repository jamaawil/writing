Felwine Sarr is a Senegalese economist, writer, and philosopher whose co-authored report on The Restitution of African Cultural Heritage, prepared with French art historian Bénédicte Savoy at the request of the French government, argued for the systematic return of African artifacts taken during the colonial period and held in French museums. The report's uncompromising stance, that restitution should be the default rather than the exception, sent shockwaves through the European museum world and helped catalyze a broader international conversation about repatriation. Beyond this influential report, Sarr's wider body of work in economics and philosophy critiques Western development models and calls for African societies to imagine their own paths to flourishing rooted in African epistemologies. He combines rigorous economic training with a literary, essayistic style of writing. He continues to be an influential voice in debates over decoloniality, African futures, and cultural restitution.
