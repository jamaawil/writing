Alex de Waal is a British researcher and writer known for his work on famine, humanitarian crisis, and conflict in Africa, particularly the Horn of Africa. Famine Crimes argues that mass starvation is rarely a natural disaster but the product of political failure and, often, deliberate political calculation, a thesis that reframed how famine is understood in humanitarian and policy circles. He has spent much of his career studying Sudan, Somalia, and Ethiopia, combining fieldwork with sharp critique of the international humanitarian system's own blind spots. As director of the World Peace Foundation he has continued to link scholarship directly to policy engagement on mass atrocity and conflict prevention. His work is essential for understanding how politics and famine are intertwined in the region.
