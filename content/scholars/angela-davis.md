Angela Davis is an American philosopher, activist, and scholar whose work has shaped Black feminist thought and the modern prison abolition movement. Women, Race & Class traced the intertwined histories of the struggles against slavery, for women's suffrage, and for labor rights, showing how race and class have repeatedly divided movements that might otherwise have found common cause. Are Prisons Obsolete? argues that mass incarceration functions as a continuation of racial control descended from slavery, and calls for abolition rather than reform of the carceral system. Her own history as a target of state surveillance and prosecution in the 1970s, connected to her membership in the Communist Party and association with the Black Panther movement, has made her both a scholarly and a symbolic figure in movements against state violence. She remains one of the most influential living theorists of abolition, feminism, and racial capitalism.
