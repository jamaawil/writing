Lidwien Kapteijns is a Dutch historian whose book Clan Cleansing in Somalia examines the systematic violence targeting specific clans during Somalia's civil war, arguing that this violence, particularly against the Isaaq and other groups in the early 1990s, constituted a deliberate pattern of what she terms clan cleansing rather than simply chaotic, undifferentiated warlordism. Her research draws on oral testimony and close historical analysis to reconstruct how clan identity was mobilized as a basis for targeted violence and displacement during the collapse of the Somali state. She situates this violence within the longer history of Somali nationalism and the political manipulation of clan identity by the Siad Barre regime and its opponents alike. Her scholarship pushes back against accounts that treat Somali conflict as simply an inevitable expression of ancient clan rivalry, insisting on its specific political construction. She has taught history at Wellesley College, focusing on the Horn of Africa.
