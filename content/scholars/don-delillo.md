Don DeLillo is an American novelist widely regarded as one of the major figures of postwar American fiction, known for prose that probes consumer culture, media saturation, conspiracy, and technological anxiety. His 1985 novel White Noise, a satire centered on a professor of Hitler studies and an "airborne toxic event," is considered a landmark work of postmodern fiction, anticipating later cultural preoccupations with environmental disaster, media spectacle, and the fear of death in a hyper-mediated society. His broader body of work, including Underworld and Libra, engages with Cold War paranoia, the Kennedy assassination, and the accumulation of waste and information in American life. He is known for a distinctive, incantatory prose style that treats mundane consumer language with an almost religious intensity. His fiction remains a touchstone for understanding the cultural anxieties of late twentieth-century America.
