Reece Jones is a political geographer whose work examines how international borders are constructed, enforced, and contested. His book "Violent Borders: Refugees and the Right to Move" argues that border enforcement regimes, rather than simply responding neutrally to migration, actively produce much of the violence and death associated with global displacement, functioning as a mechanism for maintaining wealth and privilege in the Global North. He situates contemporary border policy within longer histories of colonial boundary-making and racialized exclusion. His research draws on fieldwork at borders around the world to connect abstract policy debates to the lived experience of migrants and refugees. His work is a significant contribution to critical border and migration studies.
