Merlin Sheldrake is a British biologist whose book Entangled Life explores the science of fungi, arguing that these often overlooked organisms play a far more central and active role in shaping ecosystems, plant life, and even human culture than popular understanding typically recognizes. He examines phenomena including mycorrhizal networks that connect and allow communication between trees, fungi's role in decomposition and nutrient cycling, and the psychoactive properties of certain mushroom species, blending rigorous scientific explanation with a sense of wonder at fungal biology's strangeness relative to more familiar plant and animal life. His writing draws on his own doctoral research on underground fungal networks in tropical forests. The book was widely praised for making highly technical mycological science accessible and engaging to general readers. His work has contributed to a broader popular and scientific reassessment of fungi's ecological importance.
