Edward Baptist is an American historian whose book The Half Has Never Been Told argues that American slavery, particularly in the cotton-growing South, was not a premodern or economically backward institution but a highly capitalist and continuously intensifying system of forced labor central to the growth of American and global capitalism. He contends that slaveholders systematically used torture and violence to extract ever-increasing productivity from enslaved workers, a claim that generated significant debate among economic historians over his use of cotton-picking data. The book situates slavery's expansion at the heart of the nineteenth-century American economy rather than treating it as separate from national economic development. His work is part of the broader "new history of capitalism" that examines slavery's centrality to modern economic growth. He teaches history at Cornell University.
