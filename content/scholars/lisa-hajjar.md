Lisa Hajjar is a sociologist whose book Torture: A Sociology of Violence and Human Rights examines the legal, political, and institutional structures that enable state torture to persist despite its formal universal prohibition under international law. She traces how governments, including liberal democracies, have developed elaborate legal justifications and bureaucratic mechanisms to permit torture while nominally maintaining commitment to human rights norms, focusing significant attention on American practices during the War on Terror alongside Israeli interrogation practices in the occupied Palestinian territories. Her sociological approach treats torture not as an aberration from otherwise functioning legal systems but as something legal and political institutions actively work to accommodate and legitimate. Her broader scholarship examines human rights law, military and security institutions, and the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. She teaches sociology at the University of California, Santa Barbara.
