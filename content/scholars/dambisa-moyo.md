Dambisa Moyo is a Zambian-born economist known for her provocative critique of foreign aid to Africa. Her book Dead Aid argues that decades of large-scale development assistance have entrenched corruption, discouraged accountable governance, and crowded out the private investment and trade relationships that she contends are the real drivers of sustainable growth. Trained in economics at Oxford and Harvard and formerly with Goldman Sachs, she brings a market-oriented perspective that has made her both influential and controversial among development economists and aid advocates. Her broader body of work addresses global economic governance, resource competition, and the future of capitalism. She remains a prominent voice in debates over how, or whether, wealthy nations should intervene in African economic development.
