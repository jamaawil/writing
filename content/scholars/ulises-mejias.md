Ulises Mejias is a media studies scholar and co-author, with Nick Couldry, of The Costs of Connection, which develops the concept of "data colonialism" to argue that contemporary digital platforms extract value from human life and social relations through data collection in ways that parallel, while also differing from, historical colonial resource extraction. He argues that the framing of data as a naturally occurring resource simply waiting to be "mined" obscures the way this extraction actively appropriates something intimate, human social life and relationships, without the informed consent or fair compensation that would apply to other forms of economic exchange. Their analysis connects surveillance capitalism's business model directly to a longer history of colonial appropriation, arguing this connection is analytically necessary rather than merely rhetorical. His broader scholarship examines networked media, social movements, and the political economy of digital technology in Latin America and beyond. He teaches communication studies at the State University of New York at Oswego.
