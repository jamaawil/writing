Linda-Ruth Salter is a researcher whose co-authored book Spaces Speak, Are You Listening? examines aural architecture, the study of how built spaces shape the way sound is experienced and how, in turn, acoustic properties of space affect human social behavior and wellbeing. Working with Barry Blesser, she explores how features like reverberation, echo, and sound absorption in cathedrals, concert halls, and everyday buildings create distinct spatial and emotional experiences that architects have often overlooked in favor of purely visual design considerations. Their work draws on acoustics, architecture, psychology, and cultural history to argue that the aural dimension of space deserves the same design attention traditionally reserved for visual aesthetics. This interdisciplinary approach has been influential in architectural acoustics and sound studies. Her broader work continues to explore the relationship between the built environment, sound, and human experience.
