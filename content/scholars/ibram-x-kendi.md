Ibram X. Kendi is an American historian whose book Stamped from the Beginning traces the history of racist ideas in America from the colonial period to the present, winning the National Book Award for its argument that racist ideas have historically emerged less from ignorance or hatred and more as after-the-fact justifications for existing discriminatory policies and economic interests. He identifies and critiques three historical positions on race, segregationist, assimilationist, and antiracist, arguing that even seemingly progressive assimilationist thought has often still assumed Black inferiority requiring correction. His later book How to Be an Antiracist popularized a framework distinguishing between passively "not racist" positions and actively antiracist practice, becoming a major bestseller amid the 2020 racial justice protests. He founded and directs centers for antiracist research, most recently at Boston University. His work has become one of the most widely read and debated contemporary frameworks for understanding and confronting racism in America.
