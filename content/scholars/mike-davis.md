Mike Davis was an American urban historian and political ecologist whose book Late Victorian Holocausts examined a series of massive famines that struck India, China, and Brazil in the late nineteenth century, arguing that these deaths, often numbering in the tens of millions, resulted not simply from drought but from the deliberate policy choices of colonial and market-oriented governments that prioritized grain exports and free-market ideology over famine relief for colonized and peripheral populations. He treated these famines as a foundational, often overlooked chapter in the making of what became known as the Third World, arguing that colonial economic policy actively produced the underdevelopment later blamed on the affected regions themselves. His broader body of work, including City of Quartz, applied similarly unsparing political-economic analysis to urban history, particularly Los Angeles. He combined rigorous historical and ecological research with an unflinching commitment to leftist political critique across a wide-ranging career. He died in 2022, having remained an influential and provocative voice in radical history and urban studies until the end of his life.
