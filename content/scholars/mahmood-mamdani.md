Mahmood Mamdani is a Ugandan political scientist whose book Citizen and Subject argues that colonial rule in Africa operated through a bifurcated state, governing urban populations through a form of direct, "civilized" citizenship-based law while ruling rural populations through indirect rule and customary chieftaincy structures that colonial authorities themselves largely invented or distorted, a division whose legacy continues to shape postcolonial African governance and ethnic conflict. In Saviors and Survivors, he offers a sharply critical account of Western humanitarian intervention narratives around the Darfur crisis, arguing that framing complex political conflicts as simple stories of genocide and rescue obscures local political dynamics and can justify counterproductive foreign intervention. His broader scholarship, including on the war on terror and postcolonial citizenship, consistently interrogates how colonial-era categories of governance and identity persist in shaping contemporary African and global politics. He has held prominent academic positions including at Columbia University and Makerere University. His work remains among the most influential in African political science.
