Susan Buck-Morss is an American philosopher whose essay and later book Hegel, Haiti, and Universal History argues that G. W. F. Hegel's famous master-slave dialectic, a cornerstone of his philosophy of freedom and self-consciousness, was directly shaped by contemporary news reports of the Haitian Revolution that Hegel would have encountered while writing, a connection she contends later Hegel scholarship almost entirely erased or ignored. She argues that recognizing this connection radically changes how the dialectic should be read, tying Hegel's abstract philosophical account of freedom to a concrete, world-historical event of enslaved people winning their own liberation through revolutionary struggle. Her broader scholarship spans critical theory, visual culture, and the history of political thought, including influential work on Walter Benjamin. Her intervention has been highly influential in bringing the Haitian Revolution into direct conversation with canonical Western philosophy. She has taught political philosophy and social theory at Cornell University and the CUNY Graduate Center.
