Jeremy Scahill is an American investigative journalist whose book Dirty Wars documents the expansion of covert American military operations, including targeted killings and drone strikes conducted by special forces and the CIA, across multiple countries in the years following September 11. His reporting traces how the War on Terror increasingly relied on secretive, unaccountable programs of lethal force operating outside conventional battlefields and often outside meaningful congressional oversight, drawing on firsthand reporting from Yemen, Somalia, and Afghanistan among other sites. He was a co-founder of the news outlet The Intercept, which continued this tradition of investigative reporting on national security and surveillance. His earlier book Blackwater examined the rise of private military contractors in Iraq. His work has been influential in shaping public understanding of the expansion of executive war powers and covert warfare in the post-9/11 era.
