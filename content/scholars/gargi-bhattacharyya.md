Gargi Bhattacharyya is a British sociologist whose book Rethinking Racial Capitalism extends the concept of racial capitalism, developed by earlier Black radical thinkers like Cedric Robinson, to analyze how contemporary global capitalism continues to be organized through racialized hierarchies of labor, value, and disposability. She examines phenomena including migration control, austerity, and the gig economy as sites where race and capital remain deeply intertwined, arguing against approaches that treat racism as separable from economic structure. Her work draws on sociology, cultural studies, and critical race theory to trace how older colonial logics persist within seemingly race-neutral economic and political arrangements. She has written extensively on gender, migration, and the war on terror as well. Her scholarship contributes to an active and growing field of racial capitalism studies within contemporary sociology.
