Lorraine B. Diehl is an American writer whose book The Late, Great Pennsylvania Station chronicles the construction of New York City's original Pennsylvania Station, a Beaux-Arts architectural landmark, and its controversial demolition in the 1960s to make way for a new Madison Square Garden and a diminished underground replacement station. She treats the building's destruction as a pivotal moment in American architectural and urban history, one that galvanized the modern historic preservation movement and directly led to the creation of the New York City Landmarks Preservation Commission. Her narrative combines architectural history with cultural commentary on changing twentieth-century American attitudes toward urban grandeur, civic space, and preservation. The book remains a frequently cited account of one of the most significant losses in American architectural history and its lasting consequences for preservation policy. Her work situates a single building's fate within broader debates over urban renewal and historical memory.
