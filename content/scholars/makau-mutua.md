Makau Mutua is a Kenyan legal scholar whose book Human Rights: A Political and Cultural Critique argues that the dominant international human rights movement, despite its universalist claims, often operates within a "savages-victims-saviors" metaphor that casts non-Western states as inherently prone to rights abuses, their populations as helpless victims, and Western institutions and NGOs as the necessary saviors, a framework he contends reproduces colonial hierarchies under a human rights vocabulary. He calls for a human rights framework more genuinely attentive to the historical and cultural specificity of different societies rather than one that implicitly universalizes a particular Western liberal model. His broader scholarship addresses African constitutionalism, international law, and the politics of human rights institutions. He has held significant roles in both academia and Kenyan legal and constitutional reform efforts. He is a professor of law at the University at Buffalo and remains a prominent critical voice on the politics of international human rights discourse.
