Ifi Amadiume is a Nigerian anthropologist and poet whose book Male Daughters, Female Husbands examines flexible gender roles in precolonial Igbo society, showing that categories like "male daughter" or "female husband," roles that allowed women to occupy positions of authority and even take wives, existed within Igbo social structure before European colonialism and Christian missionary influence imposed rigid Western gender binaries. Her work argues that colonialism did not simply oppress African women within existing patriarchal structures but actively introduced and hardened gender categories that had previously been far more fluid, complicating any simple narrative of precolonial patriarchy being merely intensified by colonial rule. Her scholarship has been foundational to African gender studies and to broader debates over whether Western feminist categories apply straightforwardly to African contexts. She combines anthropological fieldwork with poetry and political essay writing. Her work remains widely cited in African women's studies and postcolonial feminist theory.
