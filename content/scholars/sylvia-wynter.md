Sylvia Wynter is a Jamaican novelist, playwright, and theorist whose wide-ranging body of work, gathered and contextualized in the volume On Being Human as Praxis, argues that the modern Western category of "the human" is not a universal biological given but a historically specific and racially exclusionary construction, one built through the interlocking histories of colonialism, slavery, and Enlightenment thought that positioned white European "man" as the template for full humanity while relegating others to its outside. She calls for a new, genuinely inclusive conception of the human to be actively invented and practiced rather than simply granted through inclusion in existing categories designed to exclude. Her dense, wide-ranging scholarship draws on literature, biology, philosophy, and history to trace how this exclusionary humanism has been constructed and maintained across centuries. Her earlier career as a novelist and playwright in Jamaica and Britain informs the literary sensibility running through her later theoretical work. Her ideas have become increasingly influential in Black studies and critical theory, especially since being systematized and popularized through Katherine McKittrick's edited collection of her work.
