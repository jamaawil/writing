Stephen Jay Gould was an American evolutionary biologist and paleontologist whose book The Mismeasure of Man offered a sustained critique of the history of scientific attempts to rank human races and groups by measured intelligence, from nineteenth-century craniometry through twentieth-century IQ testing, arguing that these efforts consistently reflected and reinforced the racial and class biases of their researchers rather than objective biological fact. He examined specific historical cases of scientific racism in detail, showing how supposedly rigorous quantitative methods were repeatedly bent, consciously or unconsciously, to confirm predetermined racial hierarchies. Alongside this work in the history and critique of science, he was one of the most prominent popularizers of evolutionary biology of his generation, co-developing the theory of punctuated equilibrium and writing extensively for general audiences on natural history and the philosophy of science. He taught paleontology and the history of science at Harvard University for most of his career. His combined work in scientific research, history of science, and public science writing made him one of the most widely read scientists of the twentieth century.
