Douglas J. Emlen is an evolutionary biologist whose book Animal Weapons examines the evolution of extreme physical weaponry in animals, from beetle horns to deer antlers, and draws provocative parallels to arms races in human warfare and geopolitics. Drawing on his own research on horned beetles, he explains how sexual selection and competition for resources or mates can drive the runaway evolution of extravagant, costly weapons, and argues that similar competitive dynamics underlie escalating arms races among nations and institutions. The comparison between biological and human weapon evolution gives the book a distinctive interdisciplinary reach beyond conventional popular science writing. He is a professor of biology at the University of Montana. His work bridges evolutionary theory and broader questions about competition, cost, and escalation in both natural and human systems.
