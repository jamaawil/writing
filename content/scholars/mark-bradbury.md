Mark Bradbury is a researcher whose book Becoming Somaliland examines the unrecognized state of Somaliland's relatively successful, largely home-grown process of building peace and functioning governance institutions following the collapse of the wider Somali state, without significant international assistance and without international diplomatic recognition of its independence. He argues that Somaliland's experience, built substantially on traditional clan-based conflict resolution mechanisms integrated with modern democratic institutions, offers an important and understudied case of successful state-building from below, in contrast to externally imposed state-building efforts elsewhere in Somalia and beyond. His analysis draws on extensive fieldwork and engagement with Somaliland's political process over many years. His work situates Somaliland's case within broader debates over sovereignty, recognition, and effective statehood. He has worked extensively with research and humanitarian organizations focused on the Horn of Africa.
