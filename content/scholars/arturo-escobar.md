Arturo Escobar is a Colombian anthropologist known as a founding figure of post-development theory. Encountering Development critically examined how the discourse of economic development, particularly as promoted by Western institutions after World War II, constructed the so-called Third World as a problem to be managed and modernized, often erasing local knowledge and alternatives in the process. Designs for the Pluriverse extends this critique into design theory, proposing frameworks for imagining futures grounded in the plurality of worlds and ontologies that development discourse tends to flatten. His work draws heavily on political ecology and social movements in Latin America, particularly Afro-Colombian and Indigenous struggles. He remains one of the most influential critics of development as an ideology of global governance.
