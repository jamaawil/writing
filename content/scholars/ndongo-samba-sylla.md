Ndongo Samba Sylla is a Senegalese economist whose co-authored book Africa's Last Colonial Currency examines the CFA franc, arguing that this monetary arrangement, used by fourteen African nations and requiring them to deposit a substantial share of foreign reserves with the French Treasury, constitutes an ongoing structure of colonial-era economic control disguised as monetary stability. His earlier book The Fair Trade Scandal similarly offered a skeptical, empirically grounded critique of fair trade certification schemes, arguing that they deliver far less benefit to smallholder farmers in the Global South than their marketing suggests while primarily benefiting Northern retailers and certifying bodies. Across both works, his research combines rigorous economic analysis with a consistent focus on how well-intentioned international economic arrangements can obscure continued extraction and inequality. He has worked with development research institutions across West Africa and internationally. His scholarship remains central to contemporary debates over African monetary sovereignty and the politics of international trade and aid schemes.
