adrienne maree brown is an American writer, facilitator, and organizer whose book Emergent Strategy draws on adaptive patterns found in nature, particularly the biology of fungal networks, flocking behavior, and other complex adaptive systems, as a framework for thinking about how social movements can organize effectively without relying on rigid, centralized hierarchy. Drawing significantly on the science fiction of Octavia Butler, she argues that small, local interactions and adaptations, guided by shared values rather than top-down control, can produce large-scale transformative social change over time, an approach she contrasts with more conventional command-and-control organizing models. Her writing style deliberately blends movement facilitation practice, personal reflection, and theoretical argument, reflecting her background as a longtime organizer and facilitator working with grassroots social justice movements. Her broader work, including on pleasure activism, connects personal wellbeing and joy directly to sustainable long-term movement building, arguing against models of activism built on self-sacrifice and burnout. Her writing style deliberately eschews capitalization in her name as a stylistic and philosophical choice reflecting her broader values. Her work has become highly influential within contemporary grassroots organizing and social justice movement circles.
