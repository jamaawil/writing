Sowande' Mustakeem is a historian whose book Slavery at Sea examines the transatlantic slave trade specifically through the lens of the Middle Passage voyage itself, treating the slave ship as a distinct site of historical analysis where enslaved Africans' bodies, health, and psychological states were violently managed as commodities in transit. She pays particular attention to gender, showing how enslaved women and men experienced the voyage differently, including through the specific and pervasive threat of sexual violence faced by enslaved women aboard ship. Her research draws on ship logs, medical records, and other maritime documentation to reconstruct the physical and psychological realities of the crossing in granular detail. Her work extends the historiography of Atlantic slavery, building on scholars like Marcus Rediker and Stephanie Smallwood, with a sustained gendered analysis of the Middle Passage experience. She teaches African American studies and history at Washington University in St. Louis.
