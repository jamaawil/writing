Mark Honigsbaum is a British medical historian and journalist whose book The Fever Trail recounts the nineteenth-century race among European explorers and botanists to locate and smuggle cinchona tree seeds and bark, the source of quinine, out of South America in order to break Andean nations' effective monopoly on the only known effective treatment for malaria. He situates this botanical espionage within the broader context of European imperial expansion into malaria-prone tropical regions, arguing that securing a reliable quinine supply was directly tied to the practical feasibility of European colonization of Africa and Asia. His narrative combines adventure history with attention to the biological and medical stakes of the story. His broader body of work examines the history of epidemic disease, including influenza and coronavirus outbreaks. His writing consistently connects the history of medicine to the broader political and imperial contexts in which medical knowledge was pursued and applied.
