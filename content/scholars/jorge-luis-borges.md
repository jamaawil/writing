Jorge Luis Borges was an Argentine writer whose short fiction and essays, collected in volumes including Ficciones and Selected Non-Fictions, made him one of the most influential literary figures of the twentieth century despite never writing a novel. His stories, built around labyrinths, infinite libraries, mirrors, and philosophical paradoxes, blurred the line between fiction, philosophy, and literary criticism, often presenting invented books and authors with the deadpan authority of genuine scholarship. His nonfiction essays ranged across world literature, philosophy, and theology with an erudition that drew on wide, idiosyncratic reading across many languages and traditions. Nearly blind for much of his later life, he became a librarian and eventually director of Argentina's National Library, an irony he noted himself in his poetry. His work profoundly influenced later postmodern and magical realist literature across Latin America and beyond.
