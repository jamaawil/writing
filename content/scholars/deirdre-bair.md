Deirdre Bair was an American biographer known for exhaustively researched lives of major cultural figures, including Samuel Beckett, Simone de Beauvoir, and Carl Jung. Her biography of Jung drew on extensive archival research and interviews to reconstruct his personal and intellectual life, including his complicated relationship with Freud and the controversies surrounding his ideas on the unconscious and his conduct during the Nazi era. She was known for a meticulous, years-long research process that combined archival documentation with firsthand interviews of surviving associates and family members. Her Beckett biography won the National Book Award, establishing her reputation as a major literary biographer. Her body of work reflects a career-long commitment to bringing rigorous historical method to the genre of biography.
