Walden Bello is a Filipino sociologist and political activist whose book Dark Victory critiques the structural adjustment policies imposed on developing nations by the International Monetary Fund and World Bank during the 1980s and 1990s, arguing that these policies, marketed as necessary paths to development, primarily served to open developing economies to foreign capital and reduce state capacity for independent economic policy, deepening poverty and inequality across much of the Global South. His broader scholarship and activism have focused on deglobalization, arguing for economic policies oriented toward domestic and regional self-sufficiency rather than integration into a global economic order he sees as structurally biased toward wealthy nations and multinational corporations. He has combined sustained academic writing with direct political engagement, including service in the Philippine House of Representatives and prominent roles in the global economic justice and anti-globalization movements. His work remains an influential critique of neoliberal globalization from a Global South perspective. He continues to write and organize on trade, development, and economic sovereignty.
