Roxanne Dunbar-Ortiz is an American historian and longtime activist whose book "An Indigenous Peoples' History of the United States" reframes the standard narrative of American history as a story of settler colonial conquest, treaty violation, and genocide directed against Native nations. The book explicitly counters triumphalist "frontier" narratives of American expansion by centering Indigenous resistance and the deliberate policies used to dispossess Native peoples of their land. Her earlier activism and scholarship engaged with the American Indian Movement and broader Indigenous rights struggles from the 1970s onward. Her work has been influential in bringing settler colonial analysis into mainstream historical writing about the United States. She continues to write on Indigenous history, land rights, and the ideology of American exceptionalism.
