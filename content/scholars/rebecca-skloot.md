Rebecca Skloot is an American science journalist best known for "The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks," which traces the origin of the HeLa cell line, taken without consent from a Black woman treated for cervical cancer in 1951, and its enormous, unacknowledged contribution to modern medical research. The book combines rigorous science reporting with a decade of investigation into the Lacks family's history, exposing the racial and economic injustices surrounding the use of her cells and the family's exclusion from any benefit or even basic information. It became a widely read entry point for broader public conversations about medical ethics, informed consent, and the exploitation of Black bodies in scientific research. Skloot's reporting also helped spur reforms in how research institutions handle tissue donation and family notification. The book remains one of the most influential works of narrative science journalism of the twenty-first century.
