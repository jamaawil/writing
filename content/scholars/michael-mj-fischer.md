Michael M. J. Fischer is an anthropologist whose book Modernity Disavowed traces the intellectual and social ferment in Iran leading up to and beyond the 1979 revolution, examining how competing visions of modernity, religious, secular, and revolutionary, contested and reshaped Iranian society and politics. He argues against simplistic narratives that treat the Iranian Revolution as either a straightforward return to religious tradition or an inevitable clash between modernity and Islam, insisting instead on the complex, internally contested character of Iranian modernity itself. His broader anthropological work, including influential writing on ethnographic methodology and science studies, has helped shape the discipline's approaches to representing complex, multivocal societies. He has held academic positions spanning anthropology and science, technology, and society programs, including at MIT. His scholarship consistently resists reductive East-West or tradition-modernity binaries in favor of more textured historical and ethnographic analysis.
