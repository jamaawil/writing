Zachary Lockman is a historian whose book Contending Visions of the Middle East examines the history of Middle East studies as an academic field in the United States, tracing how successive intellectual and political frameworks, from Orientalist scholarship through Cold War-era modernization theory to Edward Said's influential critique and its aftermath, have shaped how the region has been studied and understood in American universities and policy circles. He situates ongoing academic and political debates over Middle East studies, including controversies over the field's relationship to US foreign policy and to Israel-Palestine, within this longer intellectual history rather than treating them as purely contemporary disputes. His scholarship combines intellectual history with attention to the institutional and political contexts, including government funding and Cold War area studies programs, that shaped the field's development. His broader research addresses labor history and nationalism in the modern Middle East, particularly Egypt and Palestine. He teaches Middle Eastern and Islamic studies at New York University.
