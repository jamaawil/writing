Audra Simpson is a Mohawk anthropologist whose work has reshaped Indigenous studies and settler colonial theory. Mohawk Interruptus, based on fieldwork with the Kahnawà:ke Mohawk community, develops the concept of refusal, examining how Indigenous nations assert sovereignty by declining to fully participate in the citizenship and recognition frameworks offered by settler states. Her analysis challenges anthropology's own methods, questioning what it means to study a community that partly refuses the anthropological gaze itself. The book has become a key theoretical reference across Indigenous studies, anthropology, and political theory. Her work insists on Indigenous political refusal as a mode of sovereignty rather than a lack of engagement.
