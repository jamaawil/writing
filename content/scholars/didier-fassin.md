Didier Fassin is a French anthropologist and physician whose work examines how humanitarian reason, the moral logic of compassion and care for suffering others, has become a dominant framework for governing inequality and crisis in the contemporary world. His book Humanitarian Reason argues that humanitarianism, despite its benevolent intentions, often depoliticizes suffering by treating it as a matter of individual compassion and emergency relief rather than structural injustice requiring political redress. Drawing on ethnographic fieldwork in settings ranging from French asylum policy to South African AIDS politics, he traces how humanitarian and biomedical frameworks have reshaped the moral government of the poor and displaced. His broader scholarship spans medical anthropology, the anthropology of punishment, and moral anthropology. He holds joint appointments in anthropology and social science at the Institute for Advanced Study and the Collège de France.
