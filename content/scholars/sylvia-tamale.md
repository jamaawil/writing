Sylvia Tamale is a Ugandan legal scholar whose book Decolonization and Afro-Feminism argues that meaningful gender justice in Africa requires confronting both the patriarchal structures inherited from precolonial and colonial history and the ongoing influence of Western feminist frameworks that often fail to account for African women's specific historical and cultural contexts. She calls for an explicitly African feminist approach that draws on African philosophical and cultural resources while remaining sharply critical of patriarchal practices justified through appeals to "authentic" African tradition. Her broader legal scholarship addresses sexuality, human rights, and law across African contexts, including significant work on LGBTQ rights and sexual autonomy that has often placed her at odds with conservative political and religious forces in Uganda and beyond. She was the first woman dean of the law faculty at Makerere University in Uganda. Her work bridges rigorous legal scholarship with sustained public advocacy for gender and sexual rights across Africa.
