Saba Mahmood was a Pakistani-American anthropologist whose book "Politics of Piety" studied the women's mosque movement in Cairo, examining participants' cultivation of pious virtue and religious discipline. The book mounted an influential critique of secular liberal feminist theory, arguing that its assumptions about agency as inherently oriented toward resistance and autonomy failed to make sense of women who found meaning and self-realization through submission to religious practice and norms. Her later work, "Religious Difference in a Secular Age," examined how secular states in the Middle East, intended to protect religious minorities, have often instead entrenched sectarian difference and inequality. Her scholarship pushed anthropology and feminist theory to reconsider their liberal secular assumptions about freedom and selfhood. She died in 2018, having become one of the most influential anthropologists of religion, gender, and secularism of her generation.
