Heather Sharkey is a historian whose book Living with Colonialism examines everyday life, education, and identity formation in Anglo-Egyptian Sudan under British colonial rule in the early twentieth century. She focuses particularly on how colonial education produced a new class of Sudanese effendis, educated intermediaries who both served and eventually challenged colonial authority, showing how colonialism's own institutions could generate the intellectual resources for later nationalist resistance. Her broader scholarship examines Christian missionary activity, religious minorities, and cultural exchange across the Nile Valley and the wider Middle East and North Africa region. She combines close archival research with attention to the lived, everyday textures of colonial rule rather than focusing solely on high politics. She teaches Middle Eastern and Islamic studies at the University of Pennsylvania.
