Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak is an Indian literary theorist and one of the most influential figures in postcolonial studies, best known for her essay "Can the Subaltern Speak?" which asks whether the most marginalized and oppressed subjects of colonial and postcolonial society, particularly women, can ever truly represent themselves within structures of knowledge dominated by colonial and elite discourse. Drawing on deconstruction, Marxism, and feminist theory, she argues that even well-intentioned efforts to "give voice" to the subaltern risk reproducing the very structures of representation that silence them in the first place. Her translation of Jacques Derrida's Of Grammatology and her broader engagement with continental philosophy have made her work notoriously dense but foundational across literary theory, postcolonial studies, and feminist theory. She has long taught comparative literature at Columbia University. Her rigorous, often difficult prose has shaped how generations of scholars think about representation, subalternity, and the ethics of speaking for others.
