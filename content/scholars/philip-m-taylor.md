Philip M. Taylor was a British historian specializing in the history of propaganda, psychological warfare, and international communications. His book "British Propaganda During the First World War, 1914-18" is a standard scholarly account of how Britain organized information and persuasion campaigns during the war, shaping both domestic opinion and international perceptions. He extended this interest across the twentieth century, writing on propaganda in the Cold War and Gulf War eras as well, treating information warfare as a continuous thread running through modern conflict. His scholarship helped establish the history of propaganda and public diplomacy as a serious academic subfield rather than a marginal curiosity. He taught at the University of Leeds until his death in 2010.
