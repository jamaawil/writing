Aníbal Quijano was a Peruvian sociologist whose concept of the coloniality of power became one of the foundational ideas of Latin American decolonial theory. He argued that race was not merely a byproduct of colonialism but one of its organizing structures, invented alongside the conquest of the Americas to classify and hierarchize populations in ways that outlasted formal colonial rule. His work, developed alongside scholars such as Walter Mignolo and Enrique Dussel in the modernity/coloniality research program, distinguished coloniality from colonialism, insisting that colonial power relations persist in global capitalism, knowledge production, and social hierarchy long after political independence. This distinction reframed how Latin American and global South scholars think about race, capitalism, and epistemology. He remains one of the most influential theorists in the decolonial turn across the humanities and social sciences.
