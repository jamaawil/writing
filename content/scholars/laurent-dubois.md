Laurent Dubois is an American historian whose book Avengers of the New World offers a detailed narrative history of the Haitian Revolution, the successful slave uprising that established Haiti as the first Black republic and the second independent nation in the Americas. He argues that the Haitian Revolution deserves recognition as one of the most significant events in the history of the Atlantic world and of democratic revolution more broadly, one that took Enlightenment ideals of liberty and equality more seriously and applied them more radically than the American or French revolutions that inspired it, yet has been persistently marginalized in mainstream historical memory. His account combines rigorous archival research with attention to the enslaved revolutionaries' own political thought and strategic action. His broader scholarship spans Caribbean and French colonial history more generally. He teaches history at the University of Virginia, where his work continues to shape scholarship on Haiti and Atlantic revolution.
