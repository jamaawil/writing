Léopold Sédar Senghor was a Senegalese poet, philosopher, and statesman who, alongside Aimé Césaire and Léon Damas, co-founded the Négritude movement, which affirmed the distinct value and dignity of Black African culture, aesthetics, and ways of knowing against French colonial denigration. His poetry, collected across volumes gathered as Prose and Poetry, celebrated African rhythm, orality, and spirituality as sources of genuine philosophical and aesthetic value, sometimes framing these as complementary to, rather than simply opposed to, European rationalism, a position that drew both admiration and criticism for its perceived essentialism. Beyond his literary career, he became the first president of independent Senegal in 1960, governing for two decades while remaining an active poet and member of the Académie française, the first Black African admitted to that institution. His dual legacy as both a foundational theorist of Black cultural pride and a head of state closely tied to French political and cultural institutions remains a subject of ongoing debate. He is remembered as one of the most significant literary and political figures of twentieth-century Francophone Africa.
