George Michelsen Foy is an American writer whose book Zero Decibels: The Quest for Absolute Silence chronicles his personal journey to find the quietest places on Earth, from anechoic chambers to remote wilderness, while exploring the science and cultural meaning of silence and noise. Motivated partly by his own hearing damage, he combines memoir, science writing, and travel narrative to examine how industrial and urban life has filled the modern world with near-constant noise, and what is lost, physiologically and spiritually, in the absence of quiet. His investigation touches on acoustics, neuroscience, and the history of noise pollution regulation. The book sits within a small but notable genre of writing on silence and sound as environmental and cultural concerns. His work brings serious scientific and historical inquiry to a subject often treated only impressionistically.
