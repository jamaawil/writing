Jason Hickel is an economic anthropologist whose book The Divide argues that the persistent gap in wealth between the Global North and Global South is not primarily the result of the South's own developmental failures but of centuries of colonial extraction followed by ongoing mechanisms, including debt, trade rules, and structural adjustment, that continue to transfer resources from poorer to wealthier nations. In Less Is More, he extends this critique of the global economic order into an argument for degrowth, contending that infinite economic growth on a finite planet is both ecologically catastrophic and unnecessary for human wellbeing, and that wealthy nations in particular need to scale down resource consumption. His work combines rigorous data analysis with an explicitly political argument for global economic justice and ecological limits. He has become an influential public voice in degrowth and global inequality debates. He holds academic positions spanning anthropology and international development.
