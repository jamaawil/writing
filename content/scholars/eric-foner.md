Eric Foner is an American historian whose book Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution is considered the definitive modern history of the post-Civil War period, reframing Reconstruction not as a failed experiment or vindictive punishment of the South, as older historiography had claimed, but as a genuinely radical and ultimately betrayed attempt to build interracial democracy in the United States. He documents the political agency of formerly enslaved people in shaping Reconstruction-era institutions, alongside the violent white backlash that dismantled them. His broader body of work examines the history of American freedom, slavery, and political ideology across multiple periods. A longtime professor at Columbia University, he has been one of the most influential voices in reshaping the popular and scholarly understanding of Reconstruction. His work remains central to debates over the unfinished legacy of emancipation in American political life.
