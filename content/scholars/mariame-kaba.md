Mariame Kaba is an American organizer, educator, and writer whose book We Do This 'Til We Free Us collects essays and interviews articulating the practice and philosophy of prison industrial complex abolition, arguing that policing and incarceration cannot be reformed into justice but must be replaced by community-based systems of accountability, safety, and care built from the ground up. Rather than treating abolition as a purely theoretical horizon, she emphasizes the practical, often unglamorous work of building alternative structures, such as transformative justice practices and mutual aid networks, in the present rather than waiting for a distant revolutionary moment. She has spent decades organizing directly around issues including domestic violence, youth incarceration, and police violence, grounding her writing in this sustained grassroots practice. Her work has become widely influential in popularizing abolitionist thought beyond academic and activist circles into broader public discourse, particularly following the 2020 protests against police violence. She continues to write, teach, and organize on transformative and restorative justice.
