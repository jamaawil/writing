Emmanuel Chukwudi Eze was a Nigerian-born philosopher whose edited volume Race and the Enlightenment: A Reader made a landmark contribution to the study of race in Western philosophy by compiling and interrogating the writings of Enlightenment thinkers, including Kant and Hume, on race and human hierarchy. His work demonstrated that racial classification was not peripheral to Enlightenment philosophy's celebrated universalism but was actively theorized by some of its most canonical figures, complicating any simple narrative of Enlightenment reason as racially neutral. He also wrote extensively on African philosophy, arguing for its recognition as rigorous philosophical work in its own right rather than as anthropology. He taught philosophy in the United States before his early death in 2007. His scholarship remains foundational for critical philosophy of race and African philosophy alike.
