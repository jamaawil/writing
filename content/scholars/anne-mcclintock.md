Anne McClintock is a scholar of gender and postcolonial studies whose work examines how race, gender, and sexuality were mutually constituted through the practices of empire. Imperial Leather is a landmark study showing how domestic imagery, advertising, and gendered metaphors of the body were mobilized to naturalize colonial hierarchies in Victorian Britain and its empire. Her analysis insists that categories of race and gender cannot be studied in isolation from one another or from the material history of imperialism. The book has become a foundational text in postcolonial and feminist theory alike. Her work continues to influence scholarship on the cultural dimensions of colonial power.
