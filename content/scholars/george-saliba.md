George Saliba is a historian of science whose work reconstructs the depth and originality of astronomical and scientific research in the medieval Islamic world. His book Islamic Science and the Making of the European Renaissance argues that Arabic astronomical models developed centuries before Copernicus directly influenced European Renaissance science, challenging the common narrative that Islamic science was merely a passive custodian of Greek knowledge before Europe's own scientific revolution. His companion work, A History of Arabic Astronomy, provides a detailed technical account of the observational and mathematical achievements of Islamic astronomers across several centuries. He draws on close analysis of original Arabic manuscripts, many previously understudied, to substantiate these claims about direct intellectual transmission. He has taught Arabic and Islamic science at Columbia University for decades. His scholarship has been influential in revising Eurocentric narratives of the history of astronomy.
