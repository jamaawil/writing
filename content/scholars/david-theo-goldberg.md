David Theo Goldberg is a philosopher and critical race theorist whose book The Racial State examines how modern states have historically been constituted through racial categorization and management, not merely as sites where racism occurs incidentally. He argues that the very techniques of modern governance, census-taking, immigration control, policing, were developed in ways deeply entangled with racial classification and exclusion, making race central to state formation rather than peripheral to it. His broader work spans philosophy, law, and cultural studies, addressing topics from multiculturalism to the persistence of racism in supposedly post-racial societies. He has held positions bridging academic research and public humanities institutions. His scholarship remains influential in critical race theory and philosophy of race for its systematic account of race and statecraft.
