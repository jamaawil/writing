Judith Butler is an American philosopher whose theory of gender performativity, developed in Gender Trouble, argued that gender is not a stable, innate identity but something continually produced and reproduced through repeated social performance, a claim that became foundational to queer theory. In later work including Precarious Life and Frames of War, Butler turned to questions of violence, mourning, and political recognition, examining how some lives are publicly grievable while others, particularly those killed in wars conducted by Western powers, are rendered ungrievable and effectively unrecognized as fully human losses. This analysis extended their theoretical interest in how power constitutes and differentially values human life into direct engagement with war, terrorism, and state violence after September 11. Butler's dense, philosophically rigorous prose draws on Hegel, Foucault, and psychoanalysis while remaining engaged with contemporary political crises. They have long taught rhetoric and comparative literature at the University of California, Berkeley, and remain among the most influential living philosophers working on gender, ethics, and political violence.
