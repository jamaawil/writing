Noam Chomsky is an American linguist and political critic whose foundational work in theoretical linguistics, establishing generative grammar, fundamentally reshaped the scientific study of language, while his parallel decades of political writing have made him one of the most prominent and prolific critics of American foreign policy. His book Hegemony or Survival argues that the pursuit of unchallenged global military and economic dominance has consistently driven American foreign policy across administrations of both parties, often at the expense of international law, democratic self-determination abroad, and even the United States' own long-term security interests. His broader political writing, including Manufacturing Consent co-authored with Edward Herman, examines how corporate media ownership and structural incentives systematically shape news coverage to align with elite political and economic interests, filtering out perspectives that challenge dominant power structures. He has remained an active public intellectual and lecturer well into his nineties, addressing topics from Israel-Palestine to climate change to nuclear weapons policy. His combined legacy in linguistics and political critique makes him one of the most cited living intellectuals in either field.
