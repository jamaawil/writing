Françoise Vergès is a French political scientist from Réunion whose book A Decolonial Feminism argues that mainstream Western feminism has too often ignored or actively participated in the exploitation of women of color, from colonial-era domestic labor to contemporary global supply chains, and calls for a feminism explicitly grounded in anticolonial and antiracist struggle rather than liberal individual rights. She situates her analysis within the history of French colonialism in the Indian Ocean and the Caribbean, drawing attention to often-overlooked French imperial spaces beyond the more commonly studied African and Asian contexts. Her broader scholarship addresses memory, museums, and the politics of race in contemporary France, including critical engagement with restitution debates. She has held prominent public roles in French cultural institutions while remaining a sharp critic of French colonial memory politics. Her work bridges academic theory and public political engagement on race and gender in the Francophone world.
