Georges Nzongola-Ntalaja is a Congolese political scientist whose book The Congo from Leopold to Kabila offers a comprehensive political history of the Democratic Republic of Congo from Belgian colonization through the long dictatorship of Mobutu Sese Seko and the wars that followed his fall. He situates Congo's persistent instability not as a product of inherent African dysfunction but as the direct legacy of extraordinarily brutal colonial exploitation followed by Cold War-era foreign interference, including the assassination of Patrice Lumumba, which he treats as a foundational wound in Congolese political history. His scholarship combines rigorous political analysis with sustained engagement in Congolese and pan-African political advocacy. He has held both academic positions and roles advising Congolese and international bodies on governance and human rights. His work remains a central reference for understanding Congo's twentieth-century political trajectory.
