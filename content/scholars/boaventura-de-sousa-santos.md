Boaventura de Sousa Santos is a Portuguese sociologist and legal scholar who has become a leading figure in decolonial and Southern epistemology debates. Epistemologies of the South argues that dominant Western knowledge systems have historically dismissed or erased alternative ways of knowing produced in the Global South, and calls for an ecology of knowledges that treats these traditions as legitimate rather than subordinate. The End of the Cognitive Empire extends this project, critiquing the epistemic dominance of Northern academic institutions and proposing frameworks for genuinely pluralistic global knowledge production. His work spans law, sociology, and social movement studies, and he has been closely involved with the World Social Forum and related global justice networks. He remains one of the most cited theorists connecting epistemic justice to broader struggles against neoliberal globalization.
