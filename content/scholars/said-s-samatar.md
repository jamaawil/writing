Said S. Samatar was a Somali-American historian known for his scholarship on Somali oral literature and nationalism. His book "Oral Poetry and Somali Nationalism" examines the political role of oral poetry, particularly the verse associated with Sayyid Mohammed Abdullah Hassan's early twentieth-century resistance movement, arguing that poetry functioned as a central vehicle of political mobilization and historical memory in a society organized around oral rather than written tradition. His work highlighted the sophistication of Somali oral literary culture at a time when Western scholarship often treated oral societies as lacking the intellectual complexity of literate ones. He taught for many years in the United States and was a leading figure in the small but significant field of Somali studies. His scholarship remains foundational for understanding the relationship between poetry, clan politics, and nationalism in Somali history.
