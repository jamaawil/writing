Desmond Tutu was a South African Anglican archbishop and Nobel Peace Prize laureate who became one of the most prominent moral voices against apartheid, using his religious authority to campaign internationally for economic sanctions and political change. After apartheid's end, he chaired South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission, an institution his book No Future Without Forgiveness reflects on at length, arguing that public acknowledgment of past atrocity combined with amnesty for perpetrators who told the truth offered a path toward national healing distinct from either forgetting or retributive justice. His model of restorative rather than punitive justice influenced later truth commissions worldwide, even as critics questioned whether reconciliation came at the expense of full accountability. He remained an outspoken advocate for human rights globally until his death in 2021, criticizing injustices from Israeli policy toward Palestinians to LGBTQ discrimination. He is remembered as much for his moral clarity and wit as for his institutional achievements.
