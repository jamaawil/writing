I. M. Lewis was a British social anthropologist and one of the most influential Western scholars of Somali society, whose decades of fieldwork produced foundational studies of Somali kinship, religion, and political organization. His book A Modern History of the Somali became a standard reference text tracing Somali political development from precolonial clan structures through colonial partition, independence, and the collapse of the Somali state, and it has been repeatedly revised and updated across multiple editions to address ongoing crises. Working with B. W. Andrzejewski, he also helped establish Somali oral poetry as a serious subject of scholarly study in Somali Poetry: An Introduction. His broader anthropological work on spirit possession and segmentary lineage systems drew significantly on his Somali fieldwork and influenced anthropological theory more widely. Despite later criticism from some Somali scholars over aspects of his clan-based framework, his work remains among the most cited in Somali studies.
