Samar Al-Bulushi is a scholar of East Africa whose research examines security, militarization, and counterterrorism politics in Kenya and the broader Horn of Africa region. Her work analyzes the expansion of US security infrastructure in East Africa, including through AFRICOM, and how the global "war on terror" has reshaped everyday policing, surveillance, and political life in the region. She situates local Kenyan and Somali experiences of counterterrorism within broader critiques of American military expansion and its entanglement with postcolonial African states. Her scholarship draws on ethnographic and historical methods to examine how security discourse circulates between international and local actors. She contributes to a growing body of critical scholarship on militarized humanitarianism and security governance in Africa.
