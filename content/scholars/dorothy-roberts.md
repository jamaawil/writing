Dorothy Roberts is an American legal scholar and sociologist whose work examines the intersections of race, gender, and reproductive and family policy in the United States. Her book Fatal Invention critiques the resurgence of biological race science in medicine and genetics, arguing that treating race as a genetic category rather than a social and political construct risks reviving discredited scientific racism under a veneer of modern technology. Her earlier and continuing work, including on the child welfare system, examines how state institutions have disproportionately surveilled, regulated, and separated Black families, which she analyzes as a continuation of histories of reproductive control dating back to slavery. She holds a joint appointment in law and sociology at the University of Pennsylvania. Her scholarship has been influential in both legal academia and public debates over race, science, and family policing.
