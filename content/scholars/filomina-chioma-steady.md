Filomina Chioma Steady is a Sierra Leonean-American anthropologist and one of the pioneering scholars of African feminist thought. Her edited volume The Black Woman Cross-Culturally was among the first major academic works to bring together comparative perspectives on Black women's experiences across Africa, the Caribbean, and the Americas, challenging both mainstream feminist theory's neglect of race and Black nationalist movements' neglect of gender. She argued for understanding Black women's oppression and resistance through frameworks rooted in African and diasporic experience rather than imported wholesale from white Western feminism. Her broader work spans anthropology, gender studies, and African diaspora studies, with a long engagement in United Nations development work as well. She has held academic positions in the United States while maintaining strong ties to African intellectual networks. Her scholarship remains foundational to Black feminist and African women's studies.
