Siba Grovogui was a Guinean-American political theorist whose scholarship examined the racialized foundations of international law and sovereignty doctrine. His book "Sovereigns, Quasi-Sovereigns, and Africans" argues that the modern international legal order was constructed to grant African polities only a diminished, "quasi" form of sovereignty, structurally different from and subordinate to that granted European states, with lasting consequences for African states' standing in the postcolonial international system. His work drew on international law, political theory, and African history to challenge the presumed neutrality of the state system inherited from European colonialism. He was an active contributor to debates in postcolonial and decolonial international relations theory. His scholarship remains an important reference for understanding how formal legal equality between states can mask deep structural inequality.
