Ilan Kapoor is a scholar of international development whose book Celebrity Humanitarianism offers a psychoanalytically inflected critique of celebrity involvement in global aid and advocacy, from benefit concerts to celebrity ambassadorships for international organizations. He argues that celebrity humanitarianism, despite its apparent generosity, often functions to depoliticize global poverty and inequality, converting structural problems requiring political and economic change into matters of individual charity and consumer feel-good gestures that leave underlying power relations undisturbed. Drawing on postcolonial theory and psychoanalysis, particularly the work of Slavoj Žižek, he examines how such spectacles allow audiences to feel morally engaged while avoiding harder questions about their own complicity in global inequality. His broader scholarship applies postcolonial and psychoanalytic frameworks to development discourse more generally. He teaches critical development studies at York University in Toronto.
