Macarena Gómez-Barris is a sociologist whose book The Extractive Zone examines how extractive industries, mining, logging, monoculture agriculture, transform landscapes across Latin America into what she terms extractive zones, sites where land and Indigenous and local communities are reduced to sources of raw material value for global capital. She develops the concept of the "submerged perspective," drawing attention to the alternative, often artistic and Indigenous ways of seeing and relating to these landscapes that persist and resist the extractive gaze imposed by corporations and states. Her analysis moves across visual culture, ethnography, and political economy to examine both the violence of extraction and the forms of resistance and alternative vision that emerge alongside it. Her broader scholarship addresses race, sexuality, and memory in the context of Latin American authoritarianism and neoliberalism. She teaches sociology at Pratt Institute.
