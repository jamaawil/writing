Stephen Kinzer is an American journalist and historian whose book Overthrow surveys more than a century of American-orchestrated regime change operations, from Hawaii and Cuba in the late nineteenth century through Iran, Guatemala, and Chile during the Cold War, to more recent interventions in the Middle East. He argues that these interventions have repeatedly followed a recognizable pattern, justified at the time by appeals to democracy or security but ultimately driven by American corporate and geopolitical interests, and that they have frequently produced long-term instability and anti-American sentiment in the affected countries. His detailed case studies draw on declassified documents and historical research to reconstruct the specific mechanics of individual coups and interventions. His broader body of journalism and history writing has focused extensively on American foreign policy in the Middle East and Latin America. His work remains a widely cited synthesis connecting individual episodes of American intervention into a broader historical pattern.
