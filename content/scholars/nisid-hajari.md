Nisid Hajari is a journalist and historian whose book Midnight's Furies recounts the violent 1947 partition of British India into India and Pakistan, focusing particularly on the political failures and personal rivalries among key leaders, including Jawaharlal Nehru and Muhammad Ali Jinnah, that contributed to the catastrophic communal violence accompanying partition, in which hundreds of thousands died and millions were displaced. His narrative traces how partition's chaotic and rushed execution, combined with deep mutual distrust between Hindu, Muslim, and Sikh political leadership, set in motion patterns of hostility between India and Pakistan that continue to shape the subcontinent's politics today, including the ongoing dispute over Kashmir. His account combines political history with vivid narrative reconstruction of the violence experienced by ordinary people caught in partition's upheaval. His journalism background contributes to an accessible narrative style aimed at general readers. The book remains a widely read account connecting partition's immediate history to its enduring geopolitical consequences.
