Harriet Washington is an American medical ethicist and writer whose book Medical Apartheid documents the long, disturbing history of medical experimentation on Black Americans without informed consent, from slavery-era surgical experiments through the Tuskegee syphilis study and beyond. She argues that this history is not a series of isolated aberrations but a sustained pattern that has left a lasting and justified legacy of distrust between Black communities and the American medical establishment, a legacy that continues to shape health disparities and vaccine hesitancy today. Her research draws on extensive archival documentation to recover cases often omitted from mainstream medical history. The book received the National Book Critics Circle Award and has become a standard reference in medical ethics and the history of racial science. Her broader work continues to examine the intersections of race, environment, and health.
