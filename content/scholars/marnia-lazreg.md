Marnia Lazreg is an Algerian-American sociologist whose book Torture and the Twilight of Empire examines the systematic use of torture by French forces during the Algerian War of Independence, arguing that torture was not an aberration or excess committed by a few rogue soldiers but a deliberate, officially sanctioned policy integral to French counterinsurgency strategy. She situates this history within a broader analysis of how colonial violence reveals the underlying moral and political contradictions of a colonial power claiming to represent civilization and human rights while relying on systematic brutality to maintain control. Her research draws on French military records, testimony, and existing scholarship to reconstruct both the practice and the institutional justification of torture during the war. Her broader scholarship addresses gender, Islam, and colonialism in North Africa, including critical engagement with Western feminist representations of Algerian and Muslim women. She has taught sociology at Hunter College and the CUNY Graduate Center.
