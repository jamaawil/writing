Alexander G. Weheliye is a literary and cultural theorist whose work reworks Black studies' engagement with biopolitics and posthumanism. In Habeas Viscus he critiques the way theorists like Giorgio Agamben and Michel Foucault theorize bare life and biopolitics without reckoning with race and the history of racial slavery, arguing instead for a framework of racializing assemblages grounded in Black feminist thought. His scholarship draws on figures such as Sylvia Wynter and Hortense Spillers to insist that any general theory of power and the body must be built from, rather than around, Black experience. He also writes on sound, music, and Black diasporic culture. His work has become a key reference point in debates over race, biopolitics, and critical theory.
