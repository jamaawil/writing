Madhusree Mukerjee is an Indian-American physicist turned journalist and historian whose book Churchill's Secret War examines the 1943 Bengal famine, which killed an estimated three million people, arguing that British wartime policy under Winston Churchill's government significantly worsened the famine's severity through decisions to continue exporting food from India and to prioritize military and European stockpiles over relief for starving Bengalis. Her research draws on British archival records to challenge more forgiving accounts of Churchill's wartime leadership, presenting a detailed case that colonial indifference and racial prejudice shaped a policy response that turned a serious food shortage into a catastrophic famine. The book generated significant debate among historians of the British Empire and of Churchill's legacy specifically. Her work situates the Bengal famine within the broader history of colonial famine policy across the British Empire. She combines a scientist's attention to evidence with sustained investigative historical research.
