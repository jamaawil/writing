George Lipsitz is an American studies scholar whose book The Possessive Investment in Whiteness examines how federal housing policy, urban planning, and labor markets in the twentieth-century United States systematically transferred wealth and opportunity to white Americans while excluding Black Americans, creating a durable racial wealth gap that persists today. He argues that whiteness functions as a kind of collective asset that its holders have a material stake in protecting, which helps explain the persistence of racial inequality even after the formal end of legal segregation. His analysis draws on housing policy, popular culture, and social movement history to trace how racial privilege is reproduced through seemingly race-neutral institutions. He has taught Black studies and American studies for decades, including at the University of California, Santa Barbara. His work remains widely cited in scholarship on structural racism and racial wealth inequality.
