Ken Saro-Wiwa was a Nigerian writer, television producer, and environmental and minority rights activist who led the Movement for the Survival of the Ogoni People in its campaign against the environmental devastation wrought by oil extraction in the Niger Delta, much of it conducted by Shell in partnership with the Nigerian state. His writing, including his account documented in work like Genocide in Nigeria, argued that the Ogoni people faced a slow ecological and cultural destruction amounting to genocide, as oil spills and gas flaring poisoned Ogoni land and waterways while Ogoni communities received little of the resulting wealth. His activism directly challenged both a multinational oil corporation and Nigeria's military dictatorship, and in 1995 he was executed by the Nigerian government following a widely condemned show trial, a killing that drew international outrage and Nigeria's temporary suspension from the Commonwealth. He remains a defining martyr figure for environmental justice movements linking extraction, minority rights, and state violence in the Global South.
