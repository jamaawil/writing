Christopher J. Lee is a historian whose work focuses on decolonization, the Bandung Conference, and the global history of the twentieth century's anticolonial movements. His edited volume Making a World After Empire revisits the 1955 Bandung Conference as a pivotal moment in which newly independent and colonized nations articulated a shared vision of postcolonial solidarity and nonalignment. He situates Bandung not as an isolated diplomatic event but as part of a longer intellectual and political history connecting Africa, Asia, and Latin America. His broader scholarship examines mixed-race identity and colonial history in southern Africa as well. He writes with an eye toward recovering the global South's own theorizing of decolonization, rather than viewing it only through the lens of Cold War great-power politics.
