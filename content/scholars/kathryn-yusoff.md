Kathryn Yusoff is a geographer whose book A Billion Black Anthropocenes or None argues that the concept of the Anthropocene, as commonly framed in mainstream environmental discourse, erases the much older history of geological violence inflicted on Black and Indigenous peoples through slavery, extraction, and colonialism, which she contends should be understood as an earlier and ongoing anthropocene of its own. She insists that treating current ecological crisis as a novel, unprecedented human-caused event obscures the fact that certain populations have already lived through world-ending environmental violence for centuries, and that geology and race have long been entangled categories rather than separate domains. Her work brings together physical geography, Black studies, and critical theory to challenge the racial blindness of much mainstream environmental humanities scholarship. She continues to develop this critique across writing on extraction, race, and the earth sciences. She teaches geography at Queen Mary University of London.
