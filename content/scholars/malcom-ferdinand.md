Malcom Ferdinand is a Martinican philosopher and engineer whose book Decolonial Ecology argues that mainstream environmentalism has largely ignored the entanglement of ecological crisis with the history of slavery and colonialism, particularly in the Caribbean, and calls for an ecological politics that centers this history rather than treating environmental and colonial concerns as separate agendas. He develops the concept of a "double fracture," between the exploitation of the earth and the exploitation of enslaved and colonized peoples, arguing that any environmentalism that fails to address both simultaneously will reproduce the very hierarchies that produced ecological destruction in the first place. Drawing on Caribbean history, political ecology, and decolonial theory, he proposes a "world-ecology" grounded in the specific experiences of plantation societies and their afterlives. His work has been influential in bringing Caribbean and Black ecological thought into broader environmental humanities debates. He continues to research environmental justice and decolonial thought based in France and the Caribbean.
