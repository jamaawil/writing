Susan Quinn is an American writer whose biography Marie Curie: A Life offers a detailed account of the pioneering physicist and chemist's life, examining both her groundbreaking scientific achievements in radioactivity research, which earned her two Nobel Prizes in different scientific fields, and the significant gender-based obstacles and public scandal she faced as a woman succeeding in early twentieth-century science. Quinn draws on personal letters and archival research to present a fuller, more personally complex portrait of Curie than earlier hagiographic accounts, including her relationships, health struggles from radiation exposure, and the intense public scrutiny she faced. The biography situates Curie's scientific achievements within the broader gender politics of the scientific establishment she navigated. Quinn's other biographical work covers figures in twentieth-century American cultural and intellectual history. Her Curie biography remains one of the most substantial and balanced English-language accounts of Curie's life and career.
