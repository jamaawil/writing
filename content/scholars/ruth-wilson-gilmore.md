Ruth Wilson Gilmore is an American geographer and one of the most influential scholars of prison abolition. Her book "Golden Gulag" examines the explosive growth of California's prison system, arguing that mass incarceration emerged not from rising crime but from a convergence of surplus land, labor, capital, and state power seeking new outlets, a process she analyzes through the lens of racial capitalism. Her concept of "organized abandonment," developed further in "Abolition Geography," describes how states withdraw social investment from communities while simultaneously expanding carceral infrastructure to manage the resulting instability. Her geographic approach treats prisons as spatial and economic phenomena rather than simply criminal justice questions. She is a foundational figure in the prison abolition movement and in critical carceral geography as an academic field.
