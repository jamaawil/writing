Frantz Fanon was a Martinican-born psychiatrist and revolutionary theorist whose writing on colonialism, race, and violence became foundational to anticolonial and postcolonial thought worldwide. His early book Black Skin, White Masks drew on psychoanalysis and phenomenology to examine the psychological damage inflicted on colonized Black subjects forced to see themselves through the distorting, dehumanizing gaze of white colonial society. Trained as a psychiatrist, he later joined the Algerian independence movement, and his final book, The Wretched of the Earth, written as he was dying of leukemia, offered a searing analysis of colonial violence and a controversial defense of revolutionary counter-violence as a necessary means of decolonization and psychological liberation for the colonized. A Dying Colonialism examined the transformation of Algerian society under the pressure of anticolonial struggle, particularly the changing role of women. His work remains a central reference point across postcolonial theory, Black radical thought, and revolutionary political philosophy.
