Jackie Wang is a writer, poet, and scholar whose book Carceral Capitalism examines how policing, incarceration, and predatory financial practices have become intertwined in the contemporary United States, tracing how municipalities increasingly fund themselves through fines, fees, and asset forfeiture extracted disproportionately from poor and Black communities, a dynamic starkly illustrated by the Justice Department's investigation into Ferguson, Missouri. She connects this predatory municipal finance to broader histories of racial capitalism, showing how debt and punishment have become mutually reinforcing mechanisms of social control. Her writing moves fluidly between rigorous political-economic analysis, personal narrative, and poetic form, reflecting her background as both an academic and a poet. Her work has been influential in prison abolition and radical political economy circles. She continues to write across academic and literary genres on race, capitalism, and the carceral state.
