Hazel Carby is a British-American literary and cultural critic whose book Reconstructing Womanhood examines nineteenth-century Black women writers in the United States, arguing that dominant feminist literary history, built primarily around the experiences of white middle-class women, had systematically ignored how Black women novelists and intellectuals developed their own distinct traditions of resistance and self-representation under the conditions of slavery and its aftermath. She shows how figures often excluded from the literary canon used fiction and nonfiction to contest both racist and sexist ideologies simultaneously, refusing the separation of race and gender analysis common in earlier scholarship. Her broader work spans Black British cultural studies, the history of empire, and questions of race and nation in Anglo-American culture. She has long taught African American studies at Yale University. Her scholarship remains foundational to Black feminist literary criticism.
