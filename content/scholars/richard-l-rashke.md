Richard L. Rashke is an American author known for investigative nonfiction addressing corporate and government wrongdoing. His book "The Killing of Karen Silkwood: The Story Behind the Kerr-McGee Plutonium Case" reconstructs the case of the nuclear industry whistleblower whose mysterious death while investigating safety violations at a plutonium plant became a landmark story of corporate malfeasance and industrial danger. The book combines investigative reporting with narrative reconstruction to probe unresolved questions about corporate accountability and worker safety in the nuclear industry. Rashke has also written on the Holocaust and on U.S. immigration policy toward Nazi collaborators. His writing consistently centers the human costs of institutional power and secrecy.
