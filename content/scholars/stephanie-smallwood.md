Stephanie Smallwood is a historian whose book Saltwater Slavery traces the transformation of African captives into commodified "saltwater slaves" through the specific process of the Atlantic crossing, examining how the Middle Passage itself functioned as a deliberate process of social death and commodification rather than simply transportation. She follows this process from West African points of capture and sale through the physical and psychological ordeal of the voyage to eventual sale in the Americas, emphasizing the enslaved people's own efforts to preserve kinship, community, and selfhood against this systematic dehumanization. Her research draws extensively on the records of the Royal African Company to reconstruct both the business logic of the slave trade and its devastating human costs in unusual empirical detail. Her work is considered a landmark contribution to the historiography of Atlantic slavery for its close attention to the ocean crossing as a distinct historical process. She teaches history at the University of Washington.
