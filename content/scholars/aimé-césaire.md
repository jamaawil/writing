Aimé Césaire was a Martinican poet, playwright, and politician, and one of the founding figures of the Négritude movement alongside Léopold Sédar Senghor and Léon Damas. His Discourse on Colonialism is a foundational and searing indictment of European colonialism, arguing that the violence colonial powers exported abroad inevitably returned home, a claim that directly shaped later theorists including Frantz Fanon. As a poet he pioneered a surrealist-inflected style that fused avant-garde form with anticolonial and Afro-diasporic consciousness, most famously in Notebook of a Return to the Native Land. He also served for decades as mayor of Fort-de-France and as a French National Assembly deputy, embodying the tension between anticolonial critique and participation in French political structures. He remains one of the most cited voices in postcolonial and Black radical thought.
