Thomas Pakenham is a British-Irish historian whose book The Scramble for Africa offers a comprehensive narrative history of the late nineteenth-century European partition of the African continent, tracing how competing European powers, driven by a combination of national prestige, economic interest, and strategic rivalry, rapidly carved up nearly the entire continent into colonial territories over roughly two decades. His account follows the key explorers, colonial administrators, and African rulers and resistance leaders involved in this process, offering a detailed and accessible synthesis of a complex, multi-country history. The book became one of the most widely read popular histories of African colonization for English-language general readers, praised for its narrative sweep even as later scholarship pushed for greater attention to African agency and perspectives that a Eurocentric narrative structure can obscure. His broader historical writing includes work on Irish and British history. His account remains a widely cited entry point into the history of the colonial partition of Africa.
