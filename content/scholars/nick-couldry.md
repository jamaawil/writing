Nick Couldry is a media and communications scholar whose co-authored book The Costs of Connection argues that contemporary digital platforms extract value from human life itself, treating personal data generated through everyday online activity as a raw material to be captured, processed, and monetized in ways analogous to older forms of resource extraction and colonial appropriation. Working with Ulises Mejias, he develops the concept of "data colonialism," contending that this new form of extraction, while lacking the direct territorial conquest of historical colonialism, similarly involves powerful actors appropriating something intimate and valuable from ordinary people's lives without fair compensation or genuine consent. Their analysis connects the political economy of surveillance capitalism to the longer history of colonial resource extraction, arguing that both share a fundamental logic of treating certain populations and their outputs as available for appropriation. His broader scholarship spans media theory, voice, and the sociology of communication. He teaches media, communications, and social theory at the London School of Economics.
