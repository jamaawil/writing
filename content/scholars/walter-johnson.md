Walter Johnson is an American historian whose book River of Dark Dreams examines the Mississippi River Valley's cotton kingdom in the antebellum period, arguing that slaveholders in this region understood themselves not as a premodern aristocracy but as ambitious agents of an aggressively expansionist capitalist and imperial project, one that included active dreams of extending slavery into Cuba, Central America, and beyond through filibustering expeditions and territorial conquest. He treats slavery as inseparable from a broader ideology of racial capitalism and empire rather than as a regionally contained institution isolated from wider currents of nineteenth-century expansion and modernization. His earlier work, Soul by Soul, examined the internal domestic slave trade and the slave market as a site where enslaved people's humanity was simultaneously denied and, paradoxically, closely scrutinized by buyers assessing their bodies and capacities. His scholarship consistently connects slavery's specific institutional mechanics to broader systems of capitalism and empire. He taught history at Harvard University.
