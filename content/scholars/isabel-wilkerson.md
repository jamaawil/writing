Isabel Wilkerson is an American journalist and writer whose book Caste argues that racial hierarchy in the United States is best understood not merely as prejudice or discrimination but as a rigid caste system comparable in structure, though not identical in content, to India's caste hierarchy and Nazi Germany's racial ordering. Drawing comparative historical analysis alongside individual human stories, she contends that caste operates through fixed, inherited rankings that assign status and worth regardless of individual merit or achievement, a framework she argues better explains the persistence of racial inequality than conventional discussions of racism alone. Her earlier Pulitzer Prize-winning book The Warmth of Other Suns traced the twentieth-century Great Migration of Black Americans from the South through the intimate life stories of several individuals who lived it. Her writing combines rigorous historical research with a novelist's attention to narrative and character. Both books have been widely influential in shaping public understanding of American racial history.
