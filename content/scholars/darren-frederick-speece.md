Darren Frederick Speece is a historian whose book Defending Giants examines the decades-long conflict over logging California's ancient redwood forests, centered on the Headwaters Forest campaign of the 1980s and 1990s. He traces how a coalition of environmental activists, including tree-sitters and grassroots organizers, confronted corporate timber interests and state regulators, reshaping American environmental politics and direct-action tactics in the process. His account situates the redwood wars within broader shifts in environmental law, corporate finance, and the culture of activism in the late twentieth century United States. The book draws on extensive interviews and archival research to reconstruct both the political maneuvering and the on-the-ground activism involved. His work is a detailed case study in how environmental movements translate ecological concern into sustained political and legal confrontation.
