Dian Fossey was an American primatologist whose eighteen years studying mountain gorillas in Rwanda made her one of the most influential field researchers in animal behavior science. Her memoir Gorillas in the Mist combined close ethological observation of gorilla social behavior with an increasingly urgent account of the threats poaching and habitat loss posed to the species' survival. She became a fierce and controversial advocate for gorilla conservation, adopting confrontational tactics against poachers that alienated some local and international observers even as they drew global attention to the gorillas' plight. Her research fundamentally reshaped scientific understanding of great ape social structure and cognition. She was murdered at her research camp in 1985 in a killing that remains officially unsolved, and her work continues to underpin gorilla conservation efforts in the Virunga region today.
