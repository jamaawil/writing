Vivek Chibber is a sociologist whose book Postcolonial Theory and the Specter of Capital offers a sustained Marxist critique of the Subaltern Studies school of postcolonial theory, arguing that its key claims about the fundamental difference of non-Western historical development, distinct from a supposedly universal capitalist trajectory in the West, rest on a mischaracterization of both Western capitalist history and the actual behavior of capital and labor in the postcolonial world. He contends that capitalism's basic dynamics of class conflict and exploitation operate with more universal applicability across different cultural and historical contexts than postcolonial theorists typically allow, and that overemphasizing cultural difference can obscure genuinely universal grounds for cross-national working-class solidarity and political struggle. His intervention generated substantial and often heated debate within left academic circles between Marxist and postcolonial theoretical approaches. His broader scholarship examines industrialization, development, and comparative political economy. He teaches sociology at New York University.
