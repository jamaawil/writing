Séverine Autesserre is a French political scientist whose book The Trouble with the Congo examines why decades of costly international peacebuilding intervention in the Democratic Republic of Congo have repeatedly failed to establish lasting peace, arguing that international actors have systematically prioritized national and regional political explanations for conflict while ignoring the crucial role of local, community-level disputes over land, authority, and resources in perpetuating violence. She contends that international peacebuilders' standardized templates and short-term deployment practices leave them poorly equipped to understand or address these deeply local conflict dynamics, resulting in interventions that address symptoms without touching underlying causes. Her ethnographic research draws on extensive fieldwork within both local Congolese communities and international peacekeeping and diplomatic institutions themselves. Her broader scholarship examines the everyday practices and cultures of international peacebuilding organizations more generally. She teaches political science at Barnard College, Columbia University.
