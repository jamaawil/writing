Antony Anghie is a Sri Lankan-American legal scholar and a leading figure in Third World Approaches to International Law. Imperialism, Sovereignty and the Making of International Law argues that international law was not a neutral framework later distorted by colonialism but was constitutively shaped by the colonial encounter from its inception, with doctrines of sovereignty developed precisely to justify and manage the subjugation of non-European peoples. His work traces this colonial logic forward into contemporary international institutions and doctrines of intervention and development. He has become one of the most cited scholars connecting the history of international law to ongoing structures of global inequality. His scholarship is central to any decolonial reading of international legal order.
