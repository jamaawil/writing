Maria Dzielska is a Polish classical historian whose book Hypatia of Alexandria offers a carefully documented scholarly biography of the fourth-century philosopher and mathematician Hypatia, whose murder by a Christian mob in Alexandria has made her a lasting symbol of the conflict between classical learning and religious fanaticism. Dzielska works to separate the historically documented Hypatia from the many later legends and political appropriations that have accumulated around her, from Enlightenment-era anticlerical polemics to modern feminist reclamation, situating her instead within the specific political and religious tensions of late antique Alexandria. Her account draws on close philological analysis of the limited surviving ancient sources describing Hypatia's life, teaching, and death. The book remains one of the most rigorous scholarly treatments of a figure often more mythologized than historically understood. Her work exemplifies careful classical scholarship applied to a figure who has become a broader cultural symbol.
