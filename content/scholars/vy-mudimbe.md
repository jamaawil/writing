V. Y. Mudimbe is a Congolese philosopher and writer whose book The Invention of Africa argues that "Africa," as a coherent conceptual and geographic category, was substantially constructed through Western anthropological, missionary, and colonial discourse, which imposed categories and frameworks of understanding that shaped, and continue to shape, how Africa is studied and represented, including by African scholars themselves trained within these same Western intellectual traditions. He extends this argument in The Idea of Africa, examining how even well-intentioned attempts to recover an authentic precolonial African identity often remain trapped within categories and oppositions, such as tradition versus modernity, that were themselves products of the colonial encounter rather than independent of it. His dense, philosophically demanding scholarship draws on structuralism, philosophy of science, and African intellectual history to interrogate the very possibility of speaking about "Africa" outside inherited colonial frameworks. His work has been foundational to African philosophy and postcolonial studies for its rigorous interrogation of how knowledge about Africa has been produced. He has held academic positions at Duke University and Stanford University over his career.
