Katherine McKittrick is a Black studies scholar whose edited volume Sylvia Wynter: On Being Human as Praxis gathers and contextualizes the wide-ranging work of theorist Sylvia Wynter, introducing her often difficult writing to a broader audience while situating it within debates over race, humanism, and the limits of Western conceptions of the human. McKittrick's own scholarship, particularly her book Demonic Grounds, develops the field of Black geographies, examining how Black people have historically produced and claimed spatial knowledge and place-making practices even under conditions of enslavement, displacement, and spatial confinement. She argues that geography as a discipline has too often ignored Black spatial thought, treating Black populations as objects located in space rather than as producers of geographic knowledge themselves. Her work bridges Black feminist theory, geography, and cultural studies. She teaches gender studies at Queen's University in Canada.
