Marshall Clough is a historian whose book Mau Mau Memoirs examines the extensive body of personal memoirs and autobiographical accounts written by participants in Kenya's Mau Mau uprising against British colonial rule, treating these firsthand narratives as valuable historical sources that complicate both British colonial propaganda and later nationalist mythologizing of the rebellion. He analyzes how different memoirists, from fighters to detainees to colonial loyalists, constructed competing and sometimes contradictory accounts of the same events, shaped by their subsequent political positions in independent Kenya. His work treats memory and self-narration as historical evidence in their own right, revealing how the meaning of Mau Mau has remained politically contested long after the rebellion's military defeat. His broader scholarship addresses colonial and postcolonial Kenyan history and politics. His analysis contributes to the wider historiographical reassessment of Mau Mau alongside historians like David Anderson and Caroline Elkins.
