Carl O'Coill is a critic of international development practice known for the essay The Missionary Position: NGOs and Development in Africa, which argues that many nongovernmental organizations operating in Africa reproduce the paternalistic and civilizing assumptions of earlier colonial and missionary projects, even while presenting themselves as neutral humanitarian actors. The essay situates contemporary NGO practice within a longer history of Western intervention justified by moral or humanitarian claims. It has been widely cited in critical development studies as a challenge to the presumed innocence of aid and NGO work. The piece contributes to a broader scholarly current questioning the political effects of the international aid industry in Africa. It remains a frequently assigned text in courses critiquing development and humanitarianism.
