James Ferguson is an American anthropologist whose book The Anti-Politics Machine examines a World Bank development project in Lesotho, arguing that development interventions, despite their technocratic self-presentation as neutral economic and administrative solutions, function as an "anti-politics machine" that systematically depoliticizes deeply political problems of poverty, inequality, and state power by reframing them as merely technical challenges requiring expert management. He shows how development projects often fail on their own stated terms while nonetheless succeeding in expanding bureaucratic state power and obscuring the political roots of underdevelopment. His broader scholarship examines globalization, social welfare, and changing conceptions of dependency and mutual obligation in southern Africa. He has taught anthropology at Stanford University. His work remains a foundational critique in the anthropology of development.
