Ehsan Masood is a British science journalist and writer whose book Science and Islam surveys the long history of scientific inquiry in the Islamic world, from the great translation and research centers of medieval Baghdad and Córdoba through the challenges facing science in Muslim-majority countries today. He aims to correct a popular narrative in which Islamic civilization's scientific achievements are treated as a brief golden age disconnected from both earlier and later intellectual history, situating figures like Ibn al-Haytham and al-Khwarizmi within an ongoing, if uneven, tradition of inquiry. He also writes on contemporary science policy, development, and the politics of international science journalism. His work is aimed at bringing the history of Islamic science to a broad general audience. He has served in editorial roles at major science publications including Nature and Research Fortnight.
