Walter Mignolo is an Argentine semiotician and one of the leading theorists of Latin American decolonial thought, whose book The Darker Side of Western Modernity argues that modernity and coloniality are two sides of the same historical process, meaning that the celebrated achievements of Western modernity, reason, progress, human rights, cannot be separated from the colonial violence and exploitation that made them materially possible. His concept of the "colonial matrix of power," building on Aníbal Quijano's coloniality of power, examines how control over knowledge, authority, economy, and gender and sexuality were reorganized together through colonialism in ways that persist into the present global order. His later work, including On Decoloniality co-written with Catherine Walsh, moves toward articulating decolonial options and alternatives to Western modernity rather than only critiquing its coloniality. His extensive scholarship has been central to establishing Latin American decolonial theory as a distinct intellectual tradition alongside postcolonial studies. He has taught literature and cultural studies for decades at Duke University.
