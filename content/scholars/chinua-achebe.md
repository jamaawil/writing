Chinua Achebe was a Nigerian novelist, poet, and critic widely regarded as the father of modern African literature in English. His 1958 novel Things Fall Apart, depicting the collision between Igbo village life and British colonialism, became one of the most widely read novels in the world and a founding text of postcolonial fiction, later extended by Arrow of God and No Longer at Ease into what is often read as a loose trilogy on colonization's disruption of African societies. His essay "An Image of Africa" delivered a landmark critique of Joseph Conrad's Heart of Darkness, arguing that the novel reduced Africa to a formless backdrop for European psychological drama and reflected a deep-seated Western racism. Achebe insisted that African writers had both the right and the responsibility to tell African stories in their own terms, using English while bending it to carry African idiom and worldview. He remains a central figure in debates over language, representation, and literature's relationship to empire.
