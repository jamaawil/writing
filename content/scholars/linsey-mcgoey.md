Linsey McGoey is a sociologist whose book No Such Thing as a Free Gift offers a critical examination of the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation and the broader phenomenon of "philanthrocapitalism," arguing that large-scale corporate philanthropy, despite its benevolent framing, often serves to advance the private economic and political interests of the ultra-wealthy while escaping the democratic accountability that would apply to comparable public spending. She examines how philanthropic giving can shape global health and development policy in ways that reflect donors' particular priorities and market-oriented assumptions rather than genuinely accountable public deliberation. Her analysis raises questions about the increasing privatization of what were once considered public goods and government responsibilities. Her broader scholarship examines ignorance, expertise, and power in economic and scientific institutions. She teaches sociology at the University of Essex.
