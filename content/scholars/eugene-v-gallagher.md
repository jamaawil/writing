Eugene V. Gallagher is an American scholar of religion whose co-authored book Why Waco? examines the 1993 federal siege of the Branch Davidian compound as a case study in how American society and law enforcement misunderstand and mishandle new and minority religious movements. He argues that officials and media treated the Branch Davidians through a distorting lens of "cult" stereotypes rather than engaging with their actual beliefs and community structure, a misreading that contributed to the tragedy's escalation. His broader scholarship focuses on new religious movements, biblical interpretation, and the academic study of religion in American public life. He has taught religious studies for decades at Connecticut College. His work is frequently cited in debates over religious freedom, government response to minority religions, and the sociology of new religious movements.
