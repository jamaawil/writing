Wangari Maathai was a Kenyan environmental and political activist, the first African woman to win the Nobel Peace Prize, who founded the Green Belt Movement, a grassroots organization that mobilized Kenyan women to plant tens of millions of trees to combat deforestation, soil erosion, and the resulting economic and social hardship facing rural communities. Her memoir Unbowed recounts both this environmental organizing and her sustained, often dangerous political opposition to Kenya's authoritarian government under Daniel arap Moi, connecting ecological restoration directly to broader struggles for democratic governance, land rights, and women's empowerment. Her work as documented in accounts of the Green Belt Movement demonstrated how environmental degradation, poverty, and political repression were deeply interconnected in the postcolonial Kenyan context, requiring an integrated rather than narrowly technical environmental response. Her activism drew significant international attention and occasionally violent government suppression, including physical assaults during tree-planting protests. Her combined environmental and pro-democracy legacy made her one of the most internationally celebrated African activists of her generation before her death in 2011.
