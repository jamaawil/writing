Oyèrónkẹ́ Oyěwùmí is a Nigerian sociologist whose book "The Invention of Women: Making an African Sense of Western Gender Discourses" argues that gender as a primary social category was not indigenous to precolonial Yoruba society but was imposed through colonial administration and Western epistemology. She contends that Yoruba social organization was structured chiefly around seniority rather than gender, and that applying Western feminist frameworks uncritically to African contexts can obscure rather than illuminate local social realities. Her work is a foundational text in African gender studies and a frequently cited example of decolonial feminist critique. It has generated substantial debate among African and diaspora feminists over the universality of gender as an analytic category. She continues to be a central reference point in discussions of how Western social science categories travel across cultural contexts.
