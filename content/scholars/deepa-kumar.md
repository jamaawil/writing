Deepa Kumar is a media studies scholar whose work examines how American media and political discourse have constructed Islam and Muslims as objects of suspicion and threat. Her book Islamophobia and the Politics of Empire traces the ideological construction of the Muslim world as a monolithic enemy across multiple historical periods, from Orientalist scholarship through the Cold War to the War on Terror, arguing that Islamophobia functions to justify military intervention and domestic surveillance. She situates contemporary anti-Muslim sentiment within a longer history of racialized threat construction used to legitimate American foreign policy. Her research draws on media studies, critical race theory, and the study of empire. She continues to write and speak on media representation, empire, and the politics of race and religion.
