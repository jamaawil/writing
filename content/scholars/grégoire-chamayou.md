Grégoire Chamayou is a French philosopher whose book A Theory of the Drone examines how the increasing use of unmanned aerial vehicles for targeted killing has transformed the ethics, law, and practice of warfare. He argues that drone warfare removes the traditional reciprocity of combat, in which both sides face mortal risk, replacing it with a one-sided structure closer to hunting or policing than war in any classical sense, which in turn erodes long-standing just war principles built around mutual vulnerability. He traces the intellectual and military history of remote-controlled killing technology alongside its legal and philosophical justifications by military strategists and legal theorists. His analysis raises pointed questions about accountability, the psychological experience of drone operators, and the blurring of boundaries between war and policing. His work has become an important reference point in debates over the ethics of contemporary remote warfare.
