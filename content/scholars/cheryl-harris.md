Cheryl Harris is an American legal scholar whose 1993 Harvard Law Review article "Whiteness as Property" became one of the founding texts of critical race theory. In it, she argued that whiteness itself has functioned in American law as a form of property, a status that confers tangible legal and economic advantages and that courts have historically protected much as they protect other property interests. Her analysis traced this dynamic through slavery, the dispossession of Native land, and later doctrines of segregation and affirmative action, showing how legal neutrality often masked racial privilege. A longtime professor at UCLA School of Law, she continues to write on race, property, and constitutional law. Her work remains a central reference point for understanding how law constructs and protects racial hierarchy.
