Harsha Walia is a writer and organizer whose book Border and Rule argues that border regimes worldwide, far from being neutral instruments of national security, are technologies of racial capitalism and imperial control that manage the movement of labor and displaced populations to serve the interests of wealthy states. She traces how contemporary migration enforcement in North America, Europe, and Australia draws on and extends older colonial and racial logics, connecting mass displacement in the Global South to the very economic and military policies pursued by the wealthy nations that then restrict entry to displaced people. Her earlier book Undoing Border Imperialism similarly linked migrant justice organizing to broader anticapitalist and anticolonial movements. She combines academic argument with long experience as a grassroots organizer in migrant justice and Indigenous solidarity movements in Canada. Her work is influential in activist and academic circles working on borders, migration, and abolition.
