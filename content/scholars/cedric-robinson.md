Cedric Robinson was an American political theorist whose work fundamentally reshaped the study of race, capitalism, and Black radical thought. Black Marxism introduced and developed the concept of racial capitalism, arguing that capitalism did not simply create class divisions onto which racism was later added but was from its European origins already structured by racial hierarchy, making race and capital inseparable rather than sequential phenomena. The book also traced a distinct Black radical tradition running through figures like W. E. B. Du Bois, C. L. R. James, and Richard Wright, arguing that Black political thought could not be reduced to a variant of European Marxism but constituted its own intellectual and political lineage. Long overlooked upon publication, the book has become one of the most influential texts in Black studies and critical race theory in recent decades. His framework of racial capitalism remains central to contemporary scholarship on race, class, and political economy.
