Vijay Prashad is an Indian historian whose book The Darker Nations offers a sweeping history of the Third World as a political project, tracing the shared anticolonial and anti-imperialist aspirations that united newly independent nations across Asia, Africa, and Latin America from the 1955 Bandung Conference through the Non-Aligned Movement, before this project's eventual fragmentation under Cold War pressure and debt crisis. He argues that "Third World" originally named an ambitious political vision of South-South solidarity and an alternative to both capitalist and communist blocs, rather than simply a hierarchical economic category of underdevelopment it later came to signify. His later book Washington Bullets extends this history into an account of specific American-backed coups and assassinations that undermined Third World leaders pursuing independent development paths. His extensive scholarship combines rigorous historical research with an explicitly internationalist, left political framework. He directs Tricontinental: Institute for Social Research, connecting his historical scholarship to ongoing contemporary organizing.
