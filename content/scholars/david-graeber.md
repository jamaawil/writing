David Graeber was an American anthropologist and anarchist activist whose wide-ranging scholarship challenged conventional accounts of economics, work, and the state. His book Debt: The First 5,000 Years argued that money and markets did not arise from barter, as standard economic textbooks claim, but from long histories of credit, obligation, and often violent debt relations, and that periodic debt cancellation has been a recurring feature of human societies rather than an aberration. He was also involved in the Occupy Wall Street movement and wrote provocatively on bureaucracy, bullshit jobs, and the history of hierarchy in works like The Dawn of Everything, co-authored with archaeologist David Wengrow. His writing combined deep anthropological erudition with an explicitly political commitment to imagining alternatives to capitalism and the state. He died in 2020, leaving a body of work that continues to shape debates in anthropology, economics, and radical politics.
