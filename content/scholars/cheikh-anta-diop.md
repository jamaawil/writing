Cheikh Anta Diop was a Senegalese historian, anthropologist, and physicist who became one of the twentieth century's most influential and controversial voices in African studies. Trained in Paris, he argued in works such as The African Origin of Civilization: Myth or Reality and Precolonial Black Africa that ancient Egypt was a Black African civilization and that African societies possessed sophisticated political, cultural, and scientific traditions long obscured by colonial historiography. He drew on linguistics, archaeology, and physical anthropology to challenge Eurocentric accounts of world history and to argue for the cultural unity of Africa across the Sahara. His work was foundational to Afrocentric scholarship and to broader decolonial efforts to reclaim African intellectual and civilizational history, even as many of his specific claims provoked sustained debate among Egyptologists and historians. Diop was also a political figure in postcolonial Senegal, and the university in Dakar bears his name in recognition of his lasting influence on African historiography and pan-African thought.
