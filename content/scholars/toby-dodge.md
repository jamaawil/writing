Toby Dodge is a British political scientist whose book Inventing Iraq examines the artificial construction of the modern Iraqi state by British colonial administrators after the First World War, arguing that the state's institutions were built with limited regard for the country's deep ethnic, religious, and tribal divisions, creating structural weaknesses that would repeatedly resurface throughout Iraq's subsequent history. He draws direct parallels between this early twentieth-century British state-building failure and the American-led effort to rebuild Iraqi institutions following the 2003 invasion, arguing that policymakers repeated many of the same fundamental errors of imposing external governance models without adequate understanding of local political and social realities. His scholarship combines historical analysis of the mandate period with contemporary political science analysis of state-building and civil conflict. His broader work addresses authoritarianism, state formation, and international intervention across the Middle East. He teaches international relations at the London School of Economics.
