Alex Wright is an American writer and information architect who works on the history of knowledge organization and the technologies that preceded the internet. His book Cataloging the World: Paul Otlet and the Birth of the Information Age recovers the largely forgotten Belgian bibliographer Paul Otlet, who envisioned a networked, searchable universal repository of human knowledge decades before digital computing existed. Wright situates Otlet's project within a longer history of libraries, indexing systems, and information science, arguing that many ideas now associated with the internet age have deeper genealogies. His writing bridges library science, media history, and the history of technology for a general audience. The book has become a key reference for readers interested in the prehistory of the information age.
