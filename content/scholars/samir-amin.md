Samir Amin was an Egyptian-French Marxist economist and one of the most influential theorists of dependency and world-systems analysis. His concept of "delinking" argued that peripheral, formerly colonized economies needed to partially disengage from the terms of the global capitalist system in order to pursue autonomous, self-directed development rather than perpetual subordination to core industrial economies. His book "Eurocentrism" critiqued the ideological assumptions embedded in mainstream historical and economic thought that treated European development as the universal model against which all other societies were measured. A committed advocate of Third World solidarity, he wrote extensively in support of the political legacy of the Bandung Conference and non-aligned internationalism. He remained active as a public intellectual and organizer of Global South intellectual networks until his death in 2018.
