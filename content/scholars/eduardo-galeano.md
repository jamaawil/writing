Eduardo Galeano was a Uruguayan writer and journalist whose 1971 book Open Veins of Latin America became one of the most influential texts of Latin American anti-imperialist thought, tracing five centuries of resource extraction, from silver and sugar to oil and copper, through which European and later North American powers enriched themselves at the continent's expense. Written in a passionate, essayistic style blending history, journalism, and literary narrative, the book became required reading across the Latin American left and was famously presented to President Obama by Hugo Chávez in 2009. Galeano later expressed ambivalence about the book's schematic economic determinism, but it remained foundational to popular understandings of dependency theory and extractive colonialism in the region. His other major work, the trilogy Memory of Fire, reimagined Latin American history through fragmentary, poetic vignettes. He remained a prolific and widely translated writer and public intellectual until his death in 2015.
