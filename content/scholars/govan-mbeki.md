Govan Mbeki was a South African anti-apartheid activist, writer, and longtime leader in the African National Congress, imprisoned alongside Nelson Mandela on Robben Island for more than two decades. His book The Peasants' Revolt examines the Pondoland uprising of 1960, in which rural communities in the Transkei region rose up against apartheid-imposed tribal governance structures, situating this rural rebellion within the broader history of Black South African resistance often overshadowed by urban-centered narratives of the liberation struggle. His analysis paid particular attention to the political agency of rural peasants, a group frequently marginalized in accounts of anticolonial and anti-apartheid movements focused on urban labor and student organizing. After his release from prison, he continued in senior political roles in post-apartheid South Africa, including serving in the national government. He was also the father of Thabo Mbeki, who later became South Africa's second post-apartheid president.
