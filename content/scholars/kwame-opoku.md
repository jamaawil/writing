Kwame Opoku is a Ghanaian legal scholar and one of the most prolific and persistent voices in the international campaign for the restitution of African cultural artifacts held in Western museums. His extensive essays on restitution scrutinize the legal, ethical, and historical arguments museums and Western governments have used to resist returning looted objects, from the Benin Bronzes to countless other artifacts taken during colonial conquest, arguing that continued possession represents an ongoing injustice rather than a settled historical fact. He combines rigorous legal analysis of international cultural property law with sharp, often polemical critique of museum institutions' delaying tactics and paternalistic justifications for retention. His writing has closely tracked and critiqued the slow pace of actual restitution even as public rhetoric around the issue has shifted. He remains one of the most consistently engaged commentators on the practical politics of cultural repatriation.
