Dick J. Reavis is an American journalist whose book The Ashes of Waco offers a detailed investigative account of the 1993 siege and fatal fire at the Branch Davidian compound near Waco, Texas. Drawing on interviews, court records, and firsthand reporting, he scrutinizes the actions of federal law enforcement agencies before and during the siege, raising sustained questions about the decisions that led to the deaths of dozens of Branch Davidians, including children. His reporting situates Waco within a broader pattern of government mishandling of religious minorities perceived as threats. The book remains one of the most cited journalistic accounts of the event, valued for its skepticism toward official narratives. His work is frequently read alongside other scholarly and journalistic treatments of Waco as part of the broader literature on state violence against new religious movements.
