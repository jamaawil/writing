Tania Murray Li is an anthropologist whose book Land's End examines the transformation of upland Indonesian communities as they shifted from subsistence farming into cash crop production, particularly cacao, tracing how this transition produced new forms of land dispossession, class stratification, and rural precarity even without the direct, large-scale corporate land grabs often associated with contemporary agrarian change. She shows how seemingly voluntary, incremental market integration can nonetheless generate significant inequality and displacement as land becomes increasingly commodified and concentrated among wealthier community members. Her ethnographic research draws on extensive long-term fieldwork in Indonesia's Sulawesi highlands to trace these changes over decades. Her broader scholarship examines development, resource governance, and agrarian transformation across Southeast Asia. She teaches anthropology at the University of Toronto, where her work continues to shape critical agrarian studies.
