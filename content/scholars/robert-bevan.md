Robert Bevan is a British writer on architecture whose book "The Destruction of Memory: Architecture at War" examines the deliberate targeting of buildings, monuments, and cultural heritage during conflict as a strategy of erasing group identity and historical memory. Drawing on cases from the Balkans, the Middle East, and elsewhere, he argues that architectural destruction in war is rarely incidental but often a calculated act of what has been called cultural or memorial genocide. His work connects the physical fabric of cities to questions of national and ethnic identity, showing how erasing buildings can be a means of erasing peoples. He writes for both architectural and general audiences on urbanism, conflict, and heritage. His book has become a standard reference in the study of heritage destruction in war.
