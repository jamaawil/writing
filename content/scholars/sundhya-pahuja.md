Sundhya Pahuja is a legal scholar whose book Decolonising International Law examines how the concept of "development," as institutionalized in international law and global governance after decolonization, functioned to preserve significant Western control over former colonies even after they achieved formal political independence. She argues that international law's development framework positioned newly independent nations as perpetually catching up to a Western standard of modernity, justifying continued external oversight, conditionality, and intervention in the name of assisting their progress. Her analysis draws on critical legal theory and the history of international institutions to show how ostensibly neutral legal and economic frameworks encoded persistent hierarchies between the Global North and Global South. Her broader scholarship examines international economic law, human rights, and the postcolonial critique of international legal institutions. She teaches law at the University of Melbourne, where her work continues to shape critical approaches to international law and development.
