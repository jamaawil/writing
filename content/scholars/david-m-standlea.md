David M. Standlea is a political scientist whose work examines the intersection of energy policy, globalization, and environmental politics in the Arctic. His book Oil, Globalization, and the War for the Arctic Refuge analyzes the decades-long political battle over drilling in the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge, situating it within broader dynamics of American energy dependence, corporate lobbying, and Indigenous land rights. He examines how competing interests, oil companies, environmental groups, Alaska Native communities, and federal policymakers, have shaped one of the most contested pieces of American public land. His analysis connects local Alaskan politics to the global structure of oil markets and geopolitics. The book remains a detailed case study of how resource extraction politics play out at the intersection of environment, indigeneity, and empire.
