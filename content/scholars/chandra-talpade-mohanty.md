Chandra Talpade Mohanty is a feminist theorist known for her foundational critique of how mainstream Western feminism constructs "third world women" as a single, undifferentiated category of victimhood, most famously in her widely taught essay "Under Western Eyes." Her work, including the influential collection Feminism Without Borders, argues for a transnational feminist practice grounded in specific histories, economies, and struggles rather than an assumed global sisterhood. She has been central to building the field of transnational feminist studies, insisting that gender oppression must be analyzed alongside race, colonialism, and capitalism rather than in isolation. Her critique reshaped how postcolonial and feminist scholars think about representation, solidarity, and the politics of speaking for others. She continues to write and teach on decolonization, anticapitalist feminism, and pedagogy, and remains one of the most cited voices in postcolonial and women's studies.
