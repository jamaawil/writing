Todd Shepard is a historian whose book The Invention of Decolonization examines how France, following the loss of Algeria in 1962, retroactively reconceived its long and violent colonial history as a natural, almost inevitable process of "decolonization," a narrative reframing that helped the French state and public manage the trauma and moral reckoning that a more honest accounting of colonial violence would have demanded. He argues that the very concept of decolonization as a neutral, orderly historical process was substantially a French political and rhetorical invention constructed after the fact, obscuring the extreme violence of the Algerian War and France's reluctance to grant independence until forced to do so. His scholarship examines the specific political and legal mechanisms through which French citizenship, sovereignty, and national identity were redefined in the aftermath of Algerian independence. His broader work addresses the history of sexuality and race in modern France as well. He teaches history at Johns Hopkins University.
