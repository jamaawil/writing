Bernard Cohn was an American historian and anthropologist whose work pioneered the study of colonial knowledge production in British India. Colonialism and Its Forms of Knowledge examines how the British colonial state constructed India as an object of knowledge through censuses, surveys, museums, and legal codification, arguing that these epistemological practices were themselves instruments of colonial power rather than neutral administrative tools. His interdisciplinary method, combining historical archives with anthropological analysis, influenced a generation of scholars working on colonialism, including many associated with subaltern studies. He was among the first to insist that understanding colonialism required studying not just its political and economic structures but its systems of classification and representation. His work remains foundational to the study of colonial epistemology.
