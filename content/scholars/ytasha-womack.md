Ytasha Womack is an American writer and filmmaker whose book Afrofuturism offers one of the most accessible general introductions to Afrofuturism as a cultural and artistic movement, surveying how Black artists, musicians, and writers across music, literature, film, and visual art have used science fiction, fantasy, and speculative imagery to explore Black identity, history, and imagined futures outside the constraints of a past defined primarily by slavery and racial oppression. She traces Afrofuturism's expression across figures ranging from musician Sun Ra to novelist Octavia Butler to visual artists reimagining African cosmology and technology together, treating the movement as both an artistic aesthetic and a broader philosophical stance toward Black possibility and self-determination. Her writing is aimed at bringing Afrofuturism to a broad popular audience beyond specialized academic or fan communities. Her broader creative work spans fiction, documentary filmmaking, and dance, reflecting her own engagement with Afrofuturist themes across multiple media. Her book remains one of the most widely cited popular introductions to the Afrofuturist movement.
