A. Adu Boahen was a Ghanaian historian who became one of the leading voices in the postwar generation of African scholars who rewrote the continent's history from an African rather than a colonial vantage point. He is best known for African Perspectives on Colonialism, a concise but influential reassessment of the colonial period that insisted on African agency, resistance, and initiative rather than treating Africans as passive recipients of European rule. He also served as an editor and contributor to UNESCO's General History of Africa project, helping to institutionalize a continent-centered historiography in university curricula worldwide. Beyond his scholarly work, Boahen was active in Ghanaian public life, including a period as a pro-democracy politician, which lent his historical arguments about self-determination a lived political edge. His writing remains a touchstone for anyone studying the intellectual decolonization of African history as a discipline.
