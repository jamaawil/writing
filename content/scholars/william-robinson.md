William Robinson is an American sociologist whose book Promoting Polyarchy examines how the United States shifted its approach to intervention in the Global South during the late twentieth century, moving away from direct support for authoritarian dictatorships toward promoting formally democratic but tightly constrained political systems, which he terms "polyarchy," that permit competitive elections while ensuring outcomes remain favorable to American economic and geopolitical interests. He argues that this shift reflected a more sophisticated form of hegemonic control, one that maintains the appearance of democratic legitimacy while foreclosing more radical or genuinely popular political and economic alternatives, particularly land reform or nationalization efforts that might threaten transnational capital. His broader scholarship on globalization and the transnational capitalist class examines how global elites increasingly operate across national boundaries in ways that reshape state sovereignty and democratic accountability. He teaches sociology and global studies at the University of California, Santa Barbara.
