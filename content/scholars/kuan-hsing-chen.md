Kuan-Hsing Chen is a Taiwanese cultural studies scholar whose book Asia as Method proposes that Asian societies should look to one another, drawing on shared experiences of colonialism, Cold War division, and postcolonial nation-building, as primary reference points for understanding their own histories and futures, rather than routing all comparison and theory through the West. He argues that even critical, anti-imperialist Asian scholarship has often remained trapped in an implicit dialogue with Euro-American theory, and calls instead for horizontal, inter-Asian frameworks of comparison and solidarity. His work draws on his own positioning between Taiwan, mainland China, and the broader East Asian region shaped by Cold War geopolitics. He has been influential in building inter-Asian cultural studies networks and journals. He teaches at National Chiao Tung University in Taiwan and remains a central figure in decentering cultural studies away from Western theoretical dominance.
