Sidney Mintz was an American anthropologist and a founding figure in the anthropology of food. His book "Sweetness and Power" traces the history of sugar from a rare luxury to a mass commodity, showing how the rise of sugar consumption in Europe was directly tied to slave labor on Caribbean plantations and to broader transformations in industrial capitalism and working-class diet. The book demonstrated how a single commodity's history could illuminate connections between colonialism, labor, consumption, and everyday cultural practice, becoming a model for later commodity histories. His fieldwork in the Caribbean, particularly Puerto Rico, grounded his theoretical interests in the concrete social relations of sugar production. His work helped establish food and commodity studies as a serious field within anthropology and history.
