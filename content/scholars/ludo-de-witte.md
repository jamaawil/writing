Ludo De Witte is a Belgian sociologist and writer whose book The Assassination of Lumumba presents a meticulously documented account of the 1961 killing of Patrice Lumumba, the first democratically elected prime minister of the newly independent Congo, arguing on the basis of declassified documents that Belgian officials and the Belgian government bore direct and substantial responsibility for the assassination, working alongside Congolese rivals and with American intelligence knowledge. His research was instrumental in prompting a formal Belgian parliamentary inquiry into the killing, which in 2001 concluded that Belgium bore moral responsibility for Lumumba's death, a significant acknowledgment of colonial-era complicity in postcolonial political violence. His work situates Lumumba's assassination within the broader pattern of Western intervention against left-leaning postcolonial leaders during the Cold War. His scholarship remains a landmark case of investigative history directly prompting official state accountability. He continues to write on Belgian colonial history and its afterlives.
