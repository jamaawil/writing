Carl Akeley was an American naturalist, sculptor, and inventor best known for revolutionizing taxidermy and museum diorama techniques used to display African wildlife to the public. In Brightest Africa recounts his expeditions collecting specimens and observing wildlife, including his encounters with mountain gorillas that shifted his views from big-game hunter toward early conservationist. His advocacy contributed to the establishment of one of Africa's first gorilla sanctuaries in what is now the Democratic Republic of Congo. His dioramas at institutions such as the American Museum of Natural History shaped how generations of Western museumgoers visually understood African landscapes and animals. His career reflects the complicated overlap of exploration, science, spectacle, and conservation in early twentieth-century natural history.
