Louis-Jean Calvet is a French linguist whose book Linguistics and Colonialism examines how European linguistic scholarship developed hand in hand with colonial administration, arguing that the very tools and categories linguists used to study colonized peoples' languages were shaped by and served colonial power relations rather than constituting neutral scientific inquiry. He shows how colonial linguistics often treated indigenous languages as objects to be classified and managed rather than living systems belonging to their speakers, paralleling broader colonial patterns of extraction and control applied to language itself. His broader scholarship examines global language politics, linguistic imperialism, and the relationship between language and power more generally. He has written extensively on sociolinguistics and the political economy of world languages. His work remains an important early contribution to the critical study of linguistics' entanglement with colonial power.
