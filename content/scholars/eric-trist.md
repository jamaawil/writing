Eric Trist was a British social scientist and a founding figure of the Tavistock Institute of Human Relations, known for developing sociotechnical systems theory, an approach to organizational design that considers the social and technical aspects of work jointly rather than treating workers as mere extensions of machinery. His research, including influential studies of coal mining and industrial work, argued that productivity and worker wellbeing improved when technical systems were designed around cohesive social groups rather than fragmented, individualized tasks. His work at the Tavistock Institute helped found the field of organizational development and influenced later thinking on group dynamics, quality of working life, and participatory workplace design. He also contributed to broader theories of social ecology and turbulent organizational environments. His legacy endures in contemporary organizational psychology and systems thinking.
