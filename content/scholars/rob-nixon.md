Rob Nixon is a scholar of environmental humanities and postcolonial studies best known for coining the term "slow violence" in his book "Slow Violence and the Environmentalism of the Poor." The concept describes forms of environmental harm, such as toxic contamination, deforestation, and climate change, that unfold gradually and invisibly over long time spans, disproportionately affecting poor and marginalized communities whose suffering rarely registers as newsworthy disaster. His work bridges literary analysis and environmental politics, examining writers and activists across the Global South who have made such slow-moving catastrophes visible. He situates contemporary environmental struggles within histories of colonial resource extraction and dispossession. His scholarship has become foundational to environmental humanities and postcolonial ecocriticism.
