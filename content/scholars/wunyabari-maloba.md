Wunyabari Maloba is a Kenyan historian whose book Mau Mau and Kenya offers an analytical account of the Mau Mau uprising against British colonial rule in Kenya during the 1950s, examining it as a genuine peasant revolutionary movement with coherent political and social goals rather than the atavistic or purely criminal "savagery" that British colonial propaganda at the time portrayed it as. He situates Mau Mau within broader theoretical frameworks of peasant revolution and anticolonial resistance, analyzing its social base, organizational structure, and political objectives with the analytical tools historians typically reserve for more conventionally recognized revolutionary movements. His scholarship contributed to a broader historiographical reassessment of Mau Mau that took the movement's political seriousness on its own terms, complementing later archival work by historians like David Anderson and Caroline Elkins documenting British colonial violence during the conflict. His work situates Kenyan anticolonial resistance within comparative frameworks of global peasant and revolutionary movements. He has held academic positions in African history in the United States.
