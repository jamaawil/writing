Samuel Moyn is an American historian whose scholarship reexamines the origins and political function of human rights discourse. His book "The Last Utopia" controversially argues that human rights as a mass political movement is a surprisingly recent invention, emerging as a coherent global project mainly in the 1970s rather than descending in unbroken form from Enlightenment or postwar declarations, and that its rise partly coincided with the decline of more transformative political utopias like anticolonial socialism. In "Humane," he extends this critical genealogy to examine how efforts to make war more legally humane may have inadvertently made endless war more sustainable rather than curbing it. His work challenges human rights advocates and scholars to consider the discourse's historical contingency and political trade-offs rather than treating it as a timeless moral given. He remains an influential and often provocative voice in legal and intellectual history.
