W. E. B. Du Bois was an American sociologist, historian, and civil rights activist, the first Black person to earn a doctorate from Harvard University, whose vast body of scholarship and activism shaped virtually every subsequent field of Black studies. His book Black Reconstruction in America offered a landmark revisionist history of the Reconstruction era, arguing against the then-dominant racist historiography that formerly enslaved people had actively and skillfully built democratic institutions during Reconstruction, only to have them violently dismantled by a resurgent white supremacist backlash, a reframing decades ahead of mainstream historical scholarship's eventual acceptance of similar conclusions. His later book The World and Africa situated African history within a global framework, arguing for Africa's central rather than peripheral role in world civilization and connecting Pan-African political consciousness to a reclaimed African historical narrative. A co-founder of the NAACP and prolific sociologist whose earlier work The Philadelphia Negro pioneered empirical urban sociology, he moved toward more radical Pan-Africanist and eventually socialist politics later in life, ultimately emigrating to Ghana, where he died in 1963. His scholarship remains foundational across sociology, history, and Black political thought.
