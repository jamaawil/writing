Sarah Haley is an American historian whose book "No Mercy Here" examines the imprisonment, convict leasing, and forced labor of Black women in the Jim Crow South, an area of carceral history long overshadowed by scholarship focused on Black men's incarceration. She argues that the punishment of Black women functioned as a central, rather than marginal, mechanism for enforcing racial and gendered hierarchies after emancipation, tying the convict lease system directly to slavery's afterlives. Her research recovers individual women's experiences within the penal system to illuminate broader structures of racial capitalism and gendered violence. Her work has expanded the field of carceral studies to more fully account for the specific ways Black women were targeted by the postbellum punishment regime. It contributes to a wider body of scholarship connecting slavery, Jim Crow, and the modern prison system.
