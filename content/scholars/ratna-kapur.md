Ratna Kapur is an Indian legal scholar working at the intersection of gender, sexuality, and postcolonial critiques of human rights law. Her book "Gender, Alterity and Human Rights" argues that mainstream liberal human rights frameworks often reproduce colonial and Orientalist assumptions about victimhood and agency, particularly with respect to women in the Global South. She has written extensively on how international law's supposedly universal categories can obscure local complexity and reinforce paternalistic interventions. Her scholarship draws on postcolonial and queer theory to unsettle conventional legal feminism's assumptions about freedom, consent, and rescue. She has taught and lectured internationally on law, gender, and postcolonial theory.
