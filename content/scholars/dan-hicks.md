Dan Hicks is a British archaeologist and curator whose book The Brutish Museums argues forcefully for the return of looted colonial-era artifacts, focusing on the Benin Bronzes taken during Britain's 1897 punitive expedition against the Kingdom of Benin. He reconstructs the violence of that raid in detail, arguing that museums which display such objects as neutral art or ethnographic specimens obscure the theft and bloodshed through which they were acquired. As a curator at Oxford's Pitt Rivers Museum, he combines institutional insider standing with a sharply critical stance on museum practice, treating restitution as a matter of historical justice rather than a diplomatic favor. His work has been influential in the broader European debate over decolonizing museum collections. He continues to write and campaign on repatriation and the politics of cultural heritage.
