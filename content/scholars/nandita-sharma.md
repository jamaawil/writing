Nandita Sharma is a sociologist whose book Home Rule examines how the concept of national self-determination, celebrated as a triumph of decolonization when colonies won independence, also produced new categories of exclusion by making national citizenship, rather than a broader shared humanity, the basis for rights and belonging. She argues that postcolonial nation-states, in defining who counted as a rightful "native" citizen, often created new migrant and minority categories subject to exclusion and precarity, replicating some of the exclusionary logic of colonialism under a nationalist rather than imperial framework. Her broader scholarship examines migration, citizenship, and the analytical distinction between "migrants" and "natives" as categories she argues are themselves colonial constructs requiring critical interrogation rather than acceptance as natural. Her work connects critiques of borders and migration control to a broader critique of nationalism and the nation-state form itself. She teaches sociology at the University of Hawaii.
