Matthew Parker is a British historian whose book The Sugar Barons examines the rise of the British sugar plantation economy in the Caribbean during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, tracing how immense fortunes built on enslaved labor transformed both Caribbean colonies and British society and politics, funding country estates, political careers, and cultural institutions back in Britain. He follows several prominent planter families across generations to show how sugar wealth and the brutal slave labor system that produced it became deeply woven into British economic and political life, not a distant colonial matter separate from the metropole. His narrative combines detailed family and economic history with attention to the extraordinary violence and mortality that sustained the sugar economy for enslaved Africans. His broader historical writing addresses British colonial and military history more generally. His work contributes to reassessments of how directly slavery's profits shaped British domestic wealth and institutions.
