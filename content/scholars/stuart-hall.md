Stuart Hall was a Jamaican-British cultural theorist and one of the founding figures of British cultural studies, whose work at the Centre for Contemporary Cultural Studies in Birmingham helped establish the serious academic study of popular culture, media, and identity as sites of ideological struggle rather than trivial entertainment. His memoir and essays, gathered in Familiar Stranger, reflect on his own experience of migration from colonial Jamaica to postwar Britain, using this personal history to illuminate broader questions of diasporic identity, race, and belonging that ran throughout his theoretical work. His collected writing, including in Essential Essays, developed influential concepts such as encoding and decoding in media reception and articulation theory, showing how meaning is actively constructed and contested rather than simply transmitted or received. He was also a prominent public intellectual commentator on Thatcherism, race, and multicultural British identity. His work remains foundational across cultural studies, media studies, and postcolonial theory internationally.
