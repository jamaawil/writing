Sanjay Subrahmanyam is an Indian historian known for pioneering the method of "connected history," which traces the entangled, mutually constitutive histories of early modern societies across Eurasia, the Indian Ocean, and the Atlantic rather than treating them as isolated national or regional stories. His book "Explorations in Connected History" lays out this methodological approach, showing how court cultures, trade networks, and religious movements linked distant societies long before the modern era of globalization is conventionally said to begin. His research spans Mughal India, Portuguese imperial expansion, and the broader early modern world, drawing on multiple linguistic and archival traditions. His work has been influential in pushing global history away from Eurocentric diffusionist models toward genuinely multipolar and reciprocal accounts of cross-cultural contact. He is regarded as one of the most versatile and prolific historians of the early modern world.
