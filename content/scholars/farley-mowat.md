Farley Mowat was a Canadian author and naturalist known for popular nonfiction blending environmental advocacy with vivid narrative storytelling. His book Woman in the Mists recounts the life and death of primatologist Dian Fossey and her fight to protect Rwanda's mountain gorillas, bringing her story and the broader plight of endangered great apes to a wide popular readership. Across a prolific career, Mowat wrote extensively on Arctic wildlife, Indigenous peoples, and Canadian natural history, often taking creative liberties with strict factual accuracy in service of a larger conservationist and humanist message, a practice that drew both popular acclaim and scholarly criticism. His work played a significant role in shaping twentieth-century popular environmental consciousness in North America. He remains one of Canada's most widely read authors on nature and wildlife.
