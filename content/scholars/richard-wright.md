Richard Wright was an African-American novelist whose works "Native Son" and "Black Boy" established him as a major voice in twentieth-century American literature confronting racism and violence. His later nonfiction book "The Color Curtain" reports on the 1955 Bandung Conference of newly independent Asian and African nations, reflecting as an African-American observer on the promise and limits of Third World solidarity against colonialism and white supremacy. Having relocated to Paris, Wright wrote from a vantage point straddling African-American and international anticolonial politics, engaging with figures across the emerging Non-Aligned Movement. His fiction is known for its unflinching depiction of racial violence and psychological interiority under oppression. He remains a central figure in both American literary history and in the broader history of Black internationalist thought.
