Michael L. Sanders is a historian and co-author, with Philip M. Taylor, of British Propaganda During the First World War, a detailed study of how the British government developed sophisticated, previously secret institutions and techniques of wartime propaganda between 1914 and 1918, aimed at sustaining domestic morale, shaping neutral opinion abroad, particularly in the United States, and demonizing the German enemy. Their research draws on official records to reconstruct the organizational history of British propaganda efforts, showing how techniques developed during the war laid groundwork for twentieth-century state propaganda and public relations practices more broadly. The book is considered a foundational scholarly account of the British state's wartime information apparatus. Their work situates British propaganda within the broader history of mass persuasion emerging alongside industrialized total war. It remains a key reference for historians of propaganda, media, and the First World War.
