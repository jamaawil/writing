John Mbiti was a Kenyan philosopher and theologian whose book African Religions and Philosophy was one of the first comprehensive scholarly surveys to treat traditional African religious and philosophical systems as coherent, sophisticated bodies of thought worthy of serious academic study, rather than as primitive superstition to be contrasted unfavorably with world religions. He examined shared themes across diverse African religious traditions, including conceptions of time, community, ancestry, and the divine, arguing for underlying philosophical commonalities across the continent's enormous cultural diversity, a claim that later scholars would both build upon and critique for potential overgeneralization. As an ordained Anglican priest and theologian, he also worked extensively on the relationship between Christianity and African traditional religion, contributing to African theology as a distinct field. His work helped establish African traditional religion as a legitimate object of academic and theological study. He held academic and ecclesiastical positions across Africa, Europe, and the United States over his long career.
