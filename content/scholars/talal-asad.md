Talal Asad is an anthropologist whose work has fundamentally reshaped the anthropological and theoretical study of religion, secularism, and colonialism. His edited volume Anthropology and the Colonial Encounter helped establish a self-critical turn within anthropology, examining how the discipline itself developed in close relationship with, and often service to, colonial administration and power. His later work, including Is Critique Secular? and Formations of the Secular, argues that secularism should not be understood as religion's neutral absence but as its own historically specific and power-laden formation, one that has been exported globally alongside colonialism and continues to structure what counts as legitimate religious versus political expression, particularly in relation to Islam. He is similarly critical of liberal assumptions embedded in concepts like human rights and free speech, examining how they can function to delegitimize non-Western and religious forms of political and ethical life. His rigorous, genealogically minded scholarship has been enormously influential across anthropology, religious studies, and postcolonial theory. He has taught anthropology for decades at the CUNY Graduate Center.
