Julia Lovell is a British historian and translator of Chinese whose book The Opium War examines the mid-nineteenth-century conflicts between Britain and Qing China, arguing that the war's memory has been shaped and reshaped for different political purposes on both sides ever since, from British imperial self-justification to Chinese nationalist narratives of humiliation and resurgence. She traces how the opium trade, which Britain fought to protect despite its devastating effects on Chinese society, became a foundational episode in China's modern "century of humiliation" narrative, a story still actively invoked in contemporary Chinese politics. Her broader scholarship and translation work bridges British and Chinese historical perspectives, drawing on both English and Chinese-language sources. She has also written on Maoism's global influence and translated major works of modern Chinese literature. She teaches modern Chinese history at Birkbeck, University of London.
