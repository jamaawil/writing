Gregory Cajete is a Tewa scholar from Santa Clara Pueblo whose book Native Science argues that Indigenous knowledge traditions constitute genuine, sophisticated systems of understanding the natural world, one grounded in relationship, place, and reciprocity rather than the detached, extractive stance often associated with Western scientific method. He treats Native science not as a primitive precursor to Western science but as a distinct and valid epistemology in its own right, with its own logic, methods, and criteria of validity developed over millennia of close observation of specific ecosystems. His broader work focuses on Indigenous education, arguing for pedagogical approaches that honor Native ways of knowing rather than assimilating Indigenous students into purely Western scientific frameworks. He has spent much of his career developing Native science and education programs at Indigenous-serving institutions. His work has been influential in both Indigenous studies and broader conversations about pluralizing what counts as legitimate scientific knowledge.
