Gyanendra Pandey is an Indian historian and a founding member of the Subaltern Studies collective whose book Remembering Partition examines the 1947 partition of India not through the lens of official political history but through the fragmented, often traumatic memories of ordinary people who lived through the violence and displacement it caused. He argues that conventional historical narratives, focused on high political negotiations between colonial and nationalist elites, systematically marginalize the experience of communal violence and its long psychological aftermath for survivors. His approach draws on oral history and attention to silence and fragmentary testimony as historical sources in their own right, reflecting the broader Subaltern Studies commitment to recovering the perspectives of those excluded from elite historiography. His work has been influential in the study of partition, communal violence, and historical memory in South Asia. He has taught history at Emory University.
