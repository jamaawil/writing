Nicholas Dirks is a historian and anthropologist whose book Castes of Mind argues that the rigid, all-encompassing caste system now associated with Indian society was not simply an ancient, unchanging feature of Indian civilization but was substantially reshaped, systematized, and hardened by British colonial administration, particularly through census-taking and ethnographic classification that sought to render Indian society legible and governable to colonial administrators. He contends that colonial rule transformed caste from a more fluid, regionally variable set of social practices into a fixed, quasi-racial category central to how India was administered and understood, with lasting effects into the postcolonial present. His broader scholarship examines the history of colonial knowledge production and its relationship to anthropology as a discipline. He has held senior academic administrative positions, including as chancellor of the University of California, Berkeley. His work remains influential in South Asian studies and the history of colonial knowledge.
