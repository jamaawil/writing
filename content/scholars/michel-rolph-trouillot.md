Michel-Rolph Trouillot was a Haitian anthropologist and historian whose book Silencing the Past examined how historical narratives are actively produced through selective silences at multiple stages, from the creation of sources to their archiving to their eventual retrieval by historians, using the near-total erasure of the Haitian Revolution from mainstream Western historical memory as his central case study. He argued that the Haitian Revolution's radical challenge to prevailing assumptions about race and human capacity for self-governance made it literally unthinkable within the dominant Western intellectual framework of its time, leading to its systematic minimization in subsequent historical writing. His broader scholarship, including Global Transformations, examined anthropology's own colonial entanglements and the discipline's relationship to a rapidly globalizing world. His work bridged anthropology and history with unusual theoretical sophistication about how power shapes historical knowledge production itself. He taught anthropology at the University of Chicago before his death in 2012.
