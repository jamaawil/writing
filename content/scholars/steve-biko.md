Steve Biko was a South African anti-apartheid activist and the founder of the Black Consciousness Movement, whose collected writings, published as I Write What I Like, argued that psychological liberation from internalized racial inferiority was a necessary precondition for genuine Black political liberation from apartheid. He contended that Black South Africans needed to reject white liberal paternalism, including within multiracial anti-apartheid organizations, and instead build independent Black political and cultural institutions grounded in pride, self-reliance, and solidarity. His philosophy drew on and paralleled other Black consciousness and Black Power movements internationally while remaining specifically rooted in South African conditions. He was arrested and killed in police custody in 1977, his death under torture becoming a defining international symbol of apartheid's brutality and galvanizing global anti-apartheid solidarity movements. His writing remains foundational to Black Consciousness philosophy and to broader traditions of psychologically oriented liberation thought.
