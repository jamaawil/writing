María Lugones was an Argentine-American philosopher whose concept of the "coloniality of gender," developed in essays including "Heterosexualism and the Colonial/Modern Gender System" and later work toward a decolonial feminism, argued that the modern Western gender binary itself was imposed through colonialism rather than existing as a universal human social structure prior to European conquest. She built on and critiqued Aníbal Quijano's concept of the coloniality of power, arguing that his framework had insufficiently accounted for how colonialism specifically reorganized gender and sexuality, imposing rigid binary categories on societies that had previously organized gender and sexual difference in more varied and fluid ways. Her work insisted that any adequate decolonial theory had to take gender and sexuality as seriously as race and class, rather than treating them as secondary concerns. Her philosophy combined rigorous theoretical argument with sustained commitment to grassroots feminist and antiracist organizing. Her scholarship remains foundational to decolonial feminist theory.
