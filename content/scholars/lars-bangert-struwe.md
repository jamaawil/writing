Lars Bangert Struwe is a Danish scholar of security studies whose essays on Somali piracy examine the political economy underlying maritime piracy off the coast of Somalia, situating it within the collapse of Somali state institutions, the depletion of local fishing stocks by foreign vessels, and the broader breakdown of coastal governance following decades of civil conflict. His work analyzes how international naval responses to piracy addressed the symptom of maritime insecurity without engaging seriously with these underlying economic and political drivers on land. He examines the networks of financing, protection, and local complicity that sustained piracy as a functioning economic enterprise during its peak years. His scholarship contributes to the broader academic literature situating Somali piracy within regional political economy rather than treating it purely as a criminal or security phenomenon. He has focused his research on maritime security and Horn of Africa politics.
