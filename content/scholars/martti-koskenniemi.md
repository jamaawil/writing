Martti Koskenniemi is a Finnish legal scholar whose book The Gentle Civilizer of Nations offers a critical intellectual history of international law from the late nineteenth century through the twentieth, examining how the discipline's founders explicitly framed international law as a tool for spreading European "civilization" to the rest of the world, a colonial and hierarchical project embedded in the field's very origins. He traces how international lawyers across the twentieth century oscillated between idealistic hopes that law could constrain power and a more cynical recognition of law's frequent complicity with the interests of dominant states, without ever fully resolving this tension. His scholarship combines detailed archival and biographical research on key international law figures with broader theoretical reflection on law's relationship to politics and empire. He has taught international law extensively in Finland and internationally. His work remains foundational to critical histories of international law and its colonial entanglements.
