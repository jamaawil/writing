Tan Chung is an Indian scholar of Chinese descent whose book China and the Brave New World examines the arrival of Western imperialism in China during the nineteenth century, particularly around the Opium Wars, from a perspective informed by his dual positioning within both Indian and Chinese intellectual traditions. His scholarship situates Chinese responses to Western imperial pressure within a broader Asian context, drawing comparisons and connections to India's own experience of British colonialism that scholars working from a purely Western or purely Chinese vantage point might overlook. His family background, spanning Chinese heritage and life in India, gave his historical writing distinctive comparative sensitivity to shared and divergent experiences of Western imperialism across Asia. His broader academic career focused on Sino-Indian relations and comparative Asian civilizational studies. He held academic positions in India focused on Chinese and comparative Asian studies for much of his career.
