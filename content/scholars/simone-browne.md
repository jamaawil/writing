Simone Browne is a scholar of Black studies whose book "Dark Matters: On the Surveillance of Blackness" traces the deep historical roots of modern surveillance technology in the policing and control of enslaved and free Black populations. She argues that practices developed to monitor and restrict Black movement and bodies, from branding and slave passes to lantern laws requiring Black New Yorkers to carry light after dark, prefigure and inform contemporary biometric and digital surveillance systems. Her work insists that surveillance studies cannot be properly understood without centering anti-Blackness as a foundational, rather than incidental, feature of watching and control. She draws connections between historical practices of racial surveillance and contemporary technologies like facial recognition and airport security screening. Her scholarship has been influential in bringing critical race theory more fully into the interdisciplinary field of surveillance studies.
