Salman Sayyid is a British scholar of Pakistani descent working at the intersection of postcolonial theory and Islamic political thought. His book "Recalling the Caliphate" examines the political imagination of contemporary Islamism, arguing that longing for a caliphate should be understood as a decolonial political project responding to the dominance of the Western secular nation-state model rather than simply as reactionary nostalgia. His broader scholarship engages critically with secularism, Islamophobia, and the category of "the West" as it functions in political and academic discourse. He has been an active contributor to debates over how Islamic political movements should be theorized outside frameworks that treat secularism as the unmarked, neutral norm. His work sits within a wider decolonial critique of how area studies and political theory have historically approached Muslim-majority societies.
