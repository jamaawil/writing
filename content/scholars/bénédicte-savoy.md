Bénédicte Savoy is a French-German art historian specializing in the history of cultural property and museum collections. Co-authored with the Senegalese economist Felwine Sarr, The Restitution of African Cultural Heritage was commissioned by the French government and became a landmark report recommending the return of African artifacts looted during the colonial era, significantly shifting international debate on museum restitution. Her broader scholarship examines the history of art transfers and appropriation across Europe, situating contemporary restitution debates within longer histories of conquest and collecting. The report's recommendations have influenced restitution policy discussions well beyond France. She remains one of the most prominent voices in the global debate over decolonizing museum collections.
