Amílcar Cabral was a Guinea-Bissauan and Cape Verdean agronomist, poet, and revolutionary theorist who led the PAIGC in its armed struggle for independence from Portuguese colonial rule. Return to the Source and Unity and Struggle collect his speeches and writings, in which he developed an influential theory of national liberation grounded in the specific social and cultural conditions of African societies rather than imported Marxist orthodoxy. He argued that culture was itself a site of resistance and that the liberation struggle had to be waged as much on the terrain of identity and self-representation as on the battlefield. His practical experience as an agronomist studying Guinean soil and society shaped a theory of revolution attentive to material conditions on the ground. He was assassinated in 1973, months before Guinea-Bissau's independence, and remains one of the most studied theorists of African anticolonial revolution.
