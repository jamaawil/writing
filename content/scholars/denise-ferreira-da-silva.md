Denise Ferreira da Silva is a Brazilian critical theorist whose book Toward a Global Idea of Race examines how modern philosophical and scientific reason has been constitutively racial, producing categories of the human that relegate non-European peoples to a status outside full rationality and, therefore, outside full political subjecthood. Working across philosophy, law, and critical race theory, she argues that liberal frameworks of rights and recognition cannot adequately address racial subjugation because the very concept of the modern rational subject was built through racial exclusion. Her dense, philosophically demanding style has made her a significant if challenging figure in Black studies and critical theory. Her work continues to influence debates on race, coloniality, and the limits of liberal humanism. She has taught law and critical theory at institutions in the United States and Canada.
