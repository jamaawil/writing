Alondra Nelson is an American sociologist whose work examines the intersections of race, science, technology, and social movements. Body and Soul traces the Black Panther Party's health activism, showing how the movement built community health clinics and challenged medical neglect as part of its broader politics of liberation. Her later work, including on the social life of DNA, examines how genetic ancestry testing has become entangled with Black communities' search for reparative history and identity after slavery. She has also served in senior U.S. science policy roles, bringing her scholarship on science and social justice directly into government. Her research consistently links technical fields to questions of power, memory, and racial justice.
