Costas Douzinas is a Greek-British legal scholar and philosopher known for his critical writing on human rights and their relationship to imperial power. His book Human Rights and Empire argues that the contemporary human rights framework, despite its emancipatory rhetoric, has often been deployed to justify military intervention and to extend the influence of powerful states over weaker ones, echoing older civilizing missions of empire. Drawing on critical legal theory and continental philosophy, he traces the genealogy of rights discourse from Enlightenment natural law through its modern institutionalization. He has also been an active public intellectual and political figure in Greece, engaging directly with debates over austerity and sovereignty. His work is a touchstone for scholars skeptical of treating human rights as a politically neutral good.
