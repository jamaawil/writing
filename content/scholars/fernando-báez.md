Fernando Báez is a Venezuelan writer and librarian whose book A Universal History of the Destruction of Books surveys the long history of libraries being destroyed, from ancient Sumer and the burning of the Library of Alexandria through the looting of Iraq's National Library during the 2003 invasion, an event he witnessed and investigated firsthand. He treats the deliberate destruction of books and libraries as a recurring feature of conquest and political upheaval, arguing that attacking a people's textual heritage is often a deliberate strategy of domination rather than incidental collateral damage. His work combines historical survey with personal reportage, giving particular weight to the modern Iraqi case as a contemporary instance of an ancient pattern. He has worked as a librarian and cultural historian across Latin America and the Middle East. His book remains one of the most comprehensive accounts of bibliocide across world history.
