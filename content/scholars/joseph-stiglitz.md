Joseph Stiglitz is an American economist and Nobel laureate whose book Globalization and Its Discontents offered an influential insider's critique of the International Monetary Fund and the broader institutions governing global economic policy, drawing on his own experience as chief economist of the World Bank. He argued that IMF-imposed structural adjustment policies, often demanding austerity, privatization, and rapid market liberalization as conditions for loans, frequently worsened economic crises in developing countries rather than resolving them, prioritizing the interests of international creditors over the wellbeing of local populations. His broader academic work on asymmetric information in markets, which earned him the Nobel Prize in Economics, provided theoretical grounding for skepticism toward simplistic free-market assumptions. He has continued to write widely on inequality, globalization, and alternatives to neoliberal economic policy. He teaches economics at Columbia University and remains an influential public intellectual on global economic governance.
