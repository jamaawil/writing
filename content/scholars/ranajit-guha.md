Ranajit Guha was an Indian historian and the founding intellectual force behind the Subaltern Studies collective, which transformed the historiography of colonial South Asia beginning in the early 1980s. His book "Elementary Aspects of Peasant Insurgency in Colonial India" argued that peasant rebellions possessed their own coherent political consciousness and grammar, rather than being merely spontaneous or pre-political reactions awaiting elite leadership to become "real" politics. This work insisted historians read colonial archives against the grain to recover the agency of subaltern actors erased by both colonial and nationalist elite historiography. The Subaltern Studies series he edited brought together historians across India and eventually influenced postcolonial theory globally, including through the participation of Gayatri Spivak and Partha Chatterjee. He remains one of the most influential figures in the historiography of colonialism and peasant resistance.
