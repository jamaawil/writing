Uma Narayan is an Indian-American philosopher whose book Dislocating Cultures examines how Western feminist discourse often represents non-Western women's oppression through simplified, culturally essentialist narratives, for instance treating practices like dowry deaths or sati as uniquely revealing of an entire "Indian culture's" oppressiveness toward women in ways that similar forms of gender violence occurring in Western contexts are never treated as revealing about "Western culture" as a whole. She argues that this double standard reflects a colonial legacy in which non-Western cultures are treated as static, homogeneous, and defined by their most patriarchal practices, while Western societies are granted internal diversity and historical dynamism. Her work calls for feminist analysis attentive to how gender oppression operates similarly, though through different specific practices, across cultural contexts, without reproducing colonial hierarchies of cultural judgment. Her broader philosophical work addresses postcolonial feminism, epistemology, and the philosophy of culture. She teaches philosophy at Vassar College.
