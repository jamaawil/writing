Michael Pollan is an American writer whose book How to Change Your Mind examines the resurgence of scientific and medical research into psychedelic substances like psilocybin and LSD, tracing both the promising clinical findings on their use in treating depression, addiction, and end-of-life anxiety, and the substances' complicated cultural history since their criminalization following their association with 1960s counterculture. He combines rigorous science journalism, drawing on interviews with leading psychedelic researchers, with a personal narrative of his own guided psychedelic experiences undertaken in the course of researching the book. The book played a significant role in shaping renewed mainstream public and scientific interest in psychedelic-assisted therapy. His broader body of work, including The Omnivore's Dilemma, examines food systems and industrial agriculture with a similar combination of investigative rigor and personal narrative. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential contemporary science and food journalists writing for a general audience.
