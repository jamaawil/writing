Teju Cole is a Nigerian-American writer and photographer whose widely circulated essay "The White Savior Industrial Complex" argues that much Western humanitarian engagement with Africa, including celebrity-driven charity campaigns, functions primarily to satisfy the emotional and moral needs of Western participants rather than to address the actual political causes of African poverty and conflict, which he contends often trace back to Western policy and historical exploitation in the first place. Writing initially as a rapid sequence of tweets responding to a specific viral American charity campaign, the essay became a widely cited touchstone in broader critiques of humanitarian intervention, voluntourism, and celebrity philanthropy. His broader literary work, including the novel Open City, is known for a meditative, essayistic prose style examining memory, displacement, and urban experience. He also works as a photographer and photography critic. His writing consistently interrogates the relationship between Western representation and African reality across multiple genres and media.
