Edward L. Bernays was an Austrian-American pioneer of public relations and mass persuasion, often called the father of modern public relations, who drew explicitly on the psychoanalytic theories of his uncle Sigmund Freud to develop techniques for shaping public opinion. His 1923 book Crystallizing Public Opinion laid out an early systematic theory of how experts could professionally manage and manipulate mass sentiment on behalf of corporations, governments, and other institutions. He orchestrated numerous influential campaigns, including efforts to normalize women's smoking in public and promote American corporate and Cold War interests abroad. His frank embrace of engineering consent as a legitimate professional practice makes his writing an unusually candid historical source for understanding how modern advertising, propaganda, and public relations industries were consciously constructed. His influence extends through the advertising and public relations professions into contemporary political communication and propaganda studies.
