Michael Rothberg is a literary scholar whose book Multidirectional Memory challenges the common assumption that different histories of collective trauma, such as the Holocaust and colonial violence, compete with one another for public attention and moral recognition in a fixed, zero-sum "competitive memory." He argues instead that memories of different atrocities have historically been mutually constitutive and productive, with, for example, Holocaust memory and decolonization-era memory of colonial violence actively shaping and enabling one another's articulation in postwar public discourse rather than simply crowding each other out. His framework has been influential in memory studies for offering an alternative to zero-sum thinking about whose historical suffering deserves recognition. His broader scholarship examines Holocaust literature, trauma theory, and the politics of comparative memory. He teaches English and comparative literature at the University of California, Los Angeles.
