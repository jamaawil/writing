James D. Tabor is a scholar of biblical studies and Christian origins whose co-authored book Why Waco? examines the 1993 federal siege of the Branch Davidian compound near Waco, Texas, from the perspective of someone who was directly involved in efforts to negotiate a peaceful resolution based on theological dialogue with the group's leader, David Koresh. He argues that federal negotiators fundamentally misunderstood Koresh's apocalyptic biblical worldview, and that a more theologically informed approach to negotiation might have prevented the eventual fatal assault on the compound. His broader academic work focuses on ancient Judaism, early Christianity, and apocalyptic thought, including scholarship on the historical Jesus and Dead Sea Scrolls. His firsthand involvement in the Waco negotiations gives his account a distinctive combination of scholarly and eyewitness authority. He has taught religious studies for decades at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte.
