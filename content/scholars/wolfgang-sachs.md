Wolfgang Sachs is a German sociologist whose edited volume The Development Dictionary offers a critical genealogy of the core vocabulary of international development, examining terms like "progress," "poverty," and "growth" to show how each carries hidden Western assumptions and value judgments that shape development policy in ways presented as neutral and technical but that are, in fact, deeply ideological. The collection argues that the entire development enterprise, launched in the aftermath of the Second World War, rested on the presumption that the Global South's societies were deficient and needed to be transformed to resemble Western industrial societies, a framing that obscured both the destructive effects of development interventions and the value of existing non-Western social and economic arrangements. This work became a foundational text of post-development theory, which questions the very premise that "development" as conventionally defined is a universally desirable goal. His broader scholarship addresses environmental sustainability and the ecological limits of Western-style development models. He has been affiliated with the Wuppertal Institute for Climate, Environment, and Energy in Germany.
