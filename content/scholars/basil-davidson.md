Basil Davidson was a British historian and journalist who became one of the most prolific and influential Western writers on African history in the twentieth century. The Black Man's Burden critically examined the imposition of the European nation-state model on Africa, arguing that this borrowed political form, rather than being a neutral vessel for postcolonial governance, actively contributed to the continent's political crises after independence. The Liberation of Guiné documented the anticolonial struggle led by Amílcar Cabral in Guinea-Bissau, reflecting Davidson's direct engagement with African liberation movements as both observer and sympathetic chronicler. His broader body of work spanned precolonial African civilizations, the slave trade, and independence movements, helping to popularize serious African history for Western readers at a time when the field was marginal in Western universities. He remains a foundational, if sometimes debated, figure in the popularization of African history.
