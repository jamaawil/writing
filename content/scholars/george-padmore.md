George Padmore was a Trinidadian-born journalist and political theorist who became one of the most influential organizers and thinkers of twentieth-century Pan-Africanism. His book Pan-Africanism or Communism argued that African and Caribbean liberation required an independent Pan-African political movement rather than subordination to Soviet-directed international communism, a position that reflected his own political trajectory from committed Communist International organizer to disillusioned critic of Moscow's instrumental treatment of African liberation. He was a close advisor to Kwame Nkrumah and played a central role in organizing the 1945 Pan-African Congress in Manchester, which helped set the political agenda for African decolonization in the following decades. His writing and organizing work connected Caribbean, African, and African American radical political networks across the Atlantic. He remains a foundational, if sometimes underrecognized, architect of the Pan-African political tradition.
