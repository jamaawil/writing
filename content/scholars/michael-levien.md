Michael Levien is a sociologist whose book Dispossession without Development examines land acquisition for Special Economic Zones in contemporary India, arguing that much of this land dispossession, ostensibly justified as necessary for industrial development and job creation, has in practice enriched real estate speculators and displaced agricultural communities without delivering the promised economic benefits to affected populations. He develops a broader theoretical framework distinguishing "accumulation by dispossession" that serves genuine productive investment from dispossession that primarily serves land speculation, arguing contemporary Indian land grabs frequently fall into the latter category. His ethnographic and statistical research draws on detailed case studies of specific dispossession conflicts across India. His work contributes to broader global debates over land grabbing, development, and dispossession in the Global South. He teaches sociology at Johns Hopkins University.
