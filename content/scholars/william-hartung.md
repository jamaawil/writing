William Hartung is an American policy analyst whose book Prophets of War examines the history of Lockheed Martin and the broader American military-industrial complex, tracing how defense contractors have cultivated deep political influence through lobbying, campaign contributions, and strategically distributed manufacturing jobs across congressional districts to sustain consistently high levels of military spending regardless of shifting strategic needs. He argues that this influence has produced a defense procurement system driven as much by corporate profit and political self-interest as by genuine national security requirements, resulting in expensive, sometimes poorly performing weapons systems that persist due to entrenched institutional and political interests. His research draws on detailed analysis of defense budgets, lobbying expenditures, and specific weapons programs to substantiate this critique. His broader work as a policy analyst focuses on arms sales, military spending, and the political economy of the defense industry. He has directed research on arms and security policy at several Washington-based policy institutes.
