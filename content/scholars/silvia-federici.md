Silvia Federici is an Italian-American feminist scholar and activist whose book "Caliban and the Witch" reinterprets the early modern European witch hunts as a central mechanism of the transition to capitalism, arguing that the persecution of women was essential to the process of "primitive accumulation" by disciplining female bodies and reproductive labor into the service of a new capitalist wage economy. Her analysis links the control of women's labor, particularly unpaid domestic and reproductive work, to the emergence of capitalist social relations more broadly, building on and extending Marxist feminist theory. She has been a longtime organizer within the Wages for Housework movement, connecting her scholarly work directly to activism around the recognition of unpaid domestic labor. Her work remains foundational to Marxist feminist historiography and to contemporary debates over social reproduction. She continues to write and speak on capitalism, gender, and the commons.
