Alfred McCoy is an American historian whose research exposes the entanglement of state power, empire, and illicit economies. The Politics of Heroin documented the complicity of intelligence agencies in the global narcotics trade during the Cold War, a groundbreaking and controversial work when first published. Policing America's Empire examines how surveillance and policing techniques developed in the colonial Philippines were later imported into domestic American law enforcement and security practice, tracing a circulation of imperial methods often obscured in standard histories. Across his work he insists that the tools of American global power were forged and tested first at the periphery. He remains one of the most rigorous historians linking empire, security, and covert power.
