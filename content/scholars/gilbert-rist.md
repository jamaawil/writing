Gilbert Rist is a Swiss sociologist whose book The History of Development offers a critical genealogy of "development" as a concept, tracing it from its emergence in mid-twentieth-century modernization theory through decades of international policy, and arguing that development discourse has functioned less as a neutral technical project than as a way of extending Western economic and cultural authority over the postcolonial world under the guise of universal progress. He examines how successive development paradigms, from industrialization to structural adjustment to sustainable development, have repeatedly promised transformation while often reproducing existing global hierarchies. His analysis draws on discourse analysis and the history of ideas to denaturalize a concept usually taken for granted as self-evidently good. His work is a key text in post-development theory, which questions the very premise that all societies should aim at a single model of economic growth. He long taught international development studies in Geneva.
