Sol Plaatje was a South African intellectual, journalist, and political organizer who became the first Secretary-General of the South African Native National Congress, the organization that would later become the African National Congress. His book "Native Life in South Africa," published in 1916, documented the devastating effects of the 1913 Natives Land Act, which severely restricted Black South Africans' rights to own or lease land, combining firsthand testimony with political argument aimed at British and international audiences. A gifted linguist, he was also among the first to translate Shakespeare's plays into Setswana and worked to preserve and develop Tswana language and literature. His journalism and political organizing placed him at the center of early twentieth-century Black South African resistance to segregationist legislation, decades before the formal establishment of apartheid. He remains a foundational figure in South African political and literary history.
