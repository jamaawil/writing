Ray Desmond was a British librarian and historian whose work "The History of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew" provides a comprehensive institutional history of one of the world's most important botanical research centers. His scholarship traces how Kew Gardens developed from royal pleasure grounds into a scientific institution central to imperial botany, plant collection, and the economic exploitation of colonial territories through crops like rubber and quinine. As a librarian at Kew, he had privileged access to archival material documenting the institution's role in the networks of empire, science, and commerce. His work situates the history of botanical science within the broader history of British imperial administration. It remains a standard reference for historians of science examining the entanglement of botany and empire.
