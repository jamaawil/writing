Timothy Mitchell is a historian and political theorist whose work reshaped how scholars think about colonialism, expertise, and energy in the modern Middle East. His first major book, Colonising Egypt, examined how techniques of spatial order and discipline in architecture and urban planning reshaped everyday life under colonial rule. In Rule of Experts he extended this argument into economics and technoscience, showing how "the economy" became a governable object through the work of experts, statistics, and infrastructure. His later book Carbon Democracy traced the material entanglement of oil, labor, and mass politics, arguing that the possibilities and limits of modern democracy were shaped by the physical properties of coal and oil extraction and transport. Long associated with Middle East studies and science and technology studies in the American academy, Mitchell's blending of Foucauldian analysis with close attention to technology and infrastructure has made him a central reference point for scholars studying empire, the postcolonial state, and the politics of expertise.
