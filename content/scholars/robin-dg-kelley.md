Robin D.G. Kelley is an American historian whose scholarship spans Black radicalism, jazz, and transnational solidarity movements. His book "Freedom Dreams: The Black Radical Imagination" recovers the utopian and revolutionary visions embedded in twentieth-century Black political movements, arguing that the imaginative, surrealist, and internationalist dimensions of Black radicalism deserve as much attention as its more familiar reformist strands. His biography of Thelonious Monk situates the jazz pianist's life and music within the social and political worlds of twentieth-century Black America. His essays on Afro-Asian solidarity examine historical alliances between Black American activists and anticolonial movements in Asia and Africa. He remains one of the most prominent historians writing on the Black radical tradition today.
