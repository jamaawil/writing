Dipesh Chakrabarty is an Indian historian associated with the Subaltern Studies collective whose book Provincializing Europe became a foundational text of postcolonial theory, arguing that European historical thought, despite claiming universal validity, is itself a provincial tradition that must be decentered rather than treated as the default framework for understanding all of world history. He calls for historical writing that takes seriously non-Western categories of time, community, and meaning on their own terms rather than measuring them against a European standard of modernity. More recently, in essays gathered under titles like The Climate of History in a Planetary Age, he has turned to how climate change unsettles both postcolonial and standard historical thought by introducing a nonhuman, geological timescale that exceeds human political and cultural history. His work bridges postcolonial studies and the emerging field of the environmental humanities. He remains one of the most cited theorists in global historical and postcolonial scholarship.
