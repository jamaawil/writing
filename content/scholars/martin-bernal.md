Martin Bernal was a British scholar whose multivolume work Black Athena argued that ancient Greek civilization owed substantial debts to Egyptian and Phoenician, and by extension African and Semitic, influences that later European classical scholarship had systematically minimized or erased in favor of a myth of Greek civilization arising in racial and cultural isolation from Africa and the Near East. He contended that this erasure was driven less by evidence than by nineteenth-century European racism and antisemitism, which sought to claim Greek civilization as a purely "Aryan" achievement. The book generated intense and lasting controversy among classicists, many of whom disputed his specific historical and linguistic claims even while acknowledging his broader point about Eurocentric bias in classical scholarship's historiography. His work became a touchstone in debates over Afrocentrism, the politics of classical scholarship, and how ancient history gets constructed to serve modern racial ideologies. He taught government and Near Eastern studies at Cornell University.
