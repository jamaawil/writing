B. R. Ambedkar was an Indian jurist, economist, and social reformer who chaired the drafting committee of the Indian Constitution and became the foremost intellectual and political leader of the Dalit movement against caste oppression. Annihilation of Caste, originally written as a speech he was barred from delivering, is a searing critique arguing that caste could not be reformed from within Hinduism's religious framework and had to be abolished rather than gradually softened. Born into an untouchable community himself, he combined rigorous legal and economic training with lived experience of caste discrimination to build both a philosophical and a practical program for Dalit liberation. Late in life he led a mass conversion to Buddhism as a religious and political rejection of caste hierarchy. He remains one of the most significant political thinkers of modern India and a foundational figure for anti-caste and social justice movements worldwide.
