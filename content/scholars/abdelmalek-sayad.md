Abdelmalek Sayad was an Algerian sociologist whose work reshaped how scholars think about migration, colonialism, and the condition of the uprooted. Working for years alongside Pierre Bourdieu, he brought a rigorous sociological lens to the experience of Algerian workers in France, insisting that immigration could not be understood apart from the colonial relationship between Algeria and the metropole that produced it. His central concept of the immigrant as a figure suspended between two societies, tolerated in neither, gave later scholars a vocabulary for thinking about displacement as a structural condition rather than an individual misfortune. His major work, translated into English as The Suffering of the Immigrant, remains a touchstone in migration studies and postcolonial sociology. He matters because he refused to treat immigration as a self-contained topic, always tracing it back to the violence and inequality of French colonial rule in Algeria.
