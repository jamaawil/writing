Olaudah Equiano was a formerly enslaved man whose 1789 autobiography, "The Interesting Narrative of the Life of Olaudah Equiano," became one of the most influential texts of the British and Atlantic abolitionist movement. The narrative recounts capture as a child, the horrors of the Middle Passage, enslavement in the Americas, and his eventual purchase of his own freedom, combining first-person testimony with pointed moral argument against the slave trade. Equiano became an active campaigner in Britain's abolitionist circles, using the book's popularity to press the case for abolition directly to a European readership. Some historians have since debated aspects of his claimed African birth, but the narrative's impact on the emerging genre of slave narrative is undisputed. He remains a foundational figure in the history of Black Atlantic writing and abolitionist testimony.
