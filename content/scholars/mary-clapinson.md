Mary Clapinson is a British librarian and historian whose book A Brief History of the Bodleian Library traces the development of Oxford University's principal library from its medieval origins and near destruction during the English Reformation, through its refounding by Sir Thomas Bodley in 1602, to its status today as one of the world's great research libraries. Drawing on her own long career working within the Bodleian's collections, she combines institutional history with close attention to the library's manuscripts, acquisition policies, and evolving role within Oxford's academic life across four centuries. Her account situates the Bodleian within the broader history of European library development and the changing purposes libraries have served, from private scholarly collections to public research institutions. Her insider familiarity with the collection gives the book a level of specific institutional detail rare in general library histories. The work remains a standard concise reference on one of the world's oldest continuously operating libraries.
