Jack Goody was a British social anthropologist whose book The Theft of History argued that European and Western historians have systematically claimed for Europe alone historical developments, such as capitalism, individualism, romantic love, and the very idea of historical progress, that in fact had precedents or parallels in other world civilizations, particularly in Asia. He was a persistent critic of what he saw as a deep-seated Eurocentric bias running through much of Western historical and social scientific writing, including the work of major sociologists like Max Weber. His earlier anthropological research examined literacy, kinship, and family structures comparatively across Africa and Eurasia, exploring how the technology of writing itself shaped social and cognitive development. He taught social anthropology at Cambridge University for much of his career. His comparative, globally minded scholarship remains an influential corrective to narrowly Eurocentric historical narratives.
