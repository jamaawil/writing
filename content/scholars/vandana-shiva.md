Vandana Shiva is an Indian environmental activist and scholar whose book Staying Alive argues that Western-style industrial development and the Green Revolution's agricultural technologies have systematically undermined both ecological sustainability and women's traditional roles as knowledgeable stewards of agricultural and ecological systems in India and across the Global South. She contends that Indigenous and traditional farming practices, often maintained and transmitted by women, embody sophisticated ecological knowledge that industrial monoculture farming displaces at significant environmental and social cost, connecting ecofeminist analysis directly to critiques of colonial and neocolonial development models. Her broader activism has focused heavily on opposing the patenting of seeds and genetically modified crops by multinational agribusiness corporations, which she argues represents a new form of biological colonization over farmers' traditional seed-saving practices. She founded Navdanya, an organization promoting seed sovereignty and organic farming in India. She remains one of the most internationally prominent voices connecting environmentalism, feminism, and anti-globalization activism.
