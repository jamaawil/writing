Nir Rosen is an American journalist whose book Aftermath offers an on-the-ground account of the sectarian violence and social collapse that followed the 2003 American invasion of Iraq, extending his reporting to examine similar dynamics of conflict, displacement, and instability across the broader Middle East in the invasion's aftermath. Reporting frequently while embedded within local communities rather than with American military forces, he offers perspectives on the war's human costs that diverge significantly from official American accounts of progress and stabilization. His fluency in Arabic and willingness to report from deeply dangerous, non-official vantage points gave his journalism unusual access to ordinary Iraqis' and other Middle Eastern civilians' experiences of war and its aftermath. His work has been influential in documenting how American intervention reshaped sectarian and political dynamics across the region well beyond Iraq's borders. He has continued war reporting across multiple Middle Eastern and North African conflicts.
