Stephen Platt is a historian of China whose book Imperial Twilight offers a detailed narrative reassessment of the origins of the First Opium War between Britain and Qing China, examining the complex web of trade, diplomacy, and miscommunication that preceded the conflict rather than presenting it as a simple story of British aggression against a passive China. He pays close attention to the individuals on both sides, British merchants, diplomats, and Chinese officials, whose decisions and misjudgments contributed to the war's outbreak, while still situating the conflict within Britain's broader determination to protect its lucrative and illegal opium trade. His narrative history draws on extensive archival research in both English and Chinese sources to reconstruct this pivotal moment in the history of Sino-Western relations. His broader scholarship examines the Taiping Rebellion and other major episodes of nineteenth-century Chinese history. He teaches history at the University of Massachusetts Amherst.
