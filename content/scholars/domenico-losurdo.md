Domenico Losurdo was an Italian Marxist philosopher and historian of political thought whose book Liberalism: A Counter-History argued that classical liberalism, from its origins through the nineteenth century, was historically compatible with, and often actively supportive of, slavery, colonialism, and the exclusion of women and the propertyless from political rights. He contended that liberal theorists celebrated for defending individual liberty frequently confined that liberty to a narrow community of white, propertied men while justifying domination over those outside it. His revisionist history was controversial for its selective use of sources and polemical tone, but it sparked significant debate over the relationship between liberal political theory and historical practices of exclusion and empire. He wrote extensively on Hegel, Nietzsche, and the history of communism as well. He died in 2018, having spent much of his career at the University of Urbino.
