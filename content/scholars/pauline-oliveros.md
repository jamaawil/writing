Pauline Oliveros was an American composer and accordionist who became a central figure in postwar experimental and electronic music. She developed "Deep Listening," a practice and philosophy of attentive, expansive listening to sound and silence that she framed as both an artistic method and a form of consciousness work, elaborated in her book of the same name. Her compositions and improvisational practice drew on meditation, feminism, and technology, and she was an early and influential woman working in electronic music studios. She founded ensembles and institutes dedicated to Deep Listening that continue to train musicians and listeners in her approach. Her work reshaped how composers and scholars think about sound, attention, and the body.
