> he next day, Pope Pacelli announced his program on Vatican Radio. He said that in a time when the dark clouds of war were already gathering on the horizon, he wanted to work as a pope of peace:  For the peace that Our Predecessor of blessed memory so urgently recommended, which he tried to call down with ardent prayer and for which he most unselfishly even offered his own life; for peace, God’s most magnificent gift, which surpasses all understanding; for the peace that is founded on justice and love. So we urgently call all people to this peace. Indeed, it alone unites and inflames hearts to communion with God. In the holy love of Christ, it forms and leads the domestic church and the family. It creates among the nations such great and peaceful renewal that the individual races, by mutual trust, bonds of friendship, and common activity, aspire to the welfare and safety of the whole human family with the light and power of God’s grace.[1](The_Pope_and_the_Holocaust_0039.xhtml#fn0148)
