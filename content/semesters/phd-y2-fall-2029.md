### DISS-711 — DISS 711 · Dissertation · Methods Chapter Draft

*DISS pillar · PhD Year 2 · Fall 2029*

By the end of the semester, the dissertation's methods chapter (~12,000 words) is drafted and submitted to the shadow committee for written feedback. The intro from EXAM 711 is paired with this for the year's research output.

#### Fiction
`XD` **Atwood, M. (1985). The handmaid's tale. McClelland & Stewart.**

[Equity + Communication] Atwood's theocratic Gilead governs by communication infrastructure: scripture as state language, banned literacy for women, ritual greetings, the Wall as broadcast medium. The pillars synthesize through the figure of the Handmaid as both reproductive property and prohibited reader. A foundational late-twentieth-century text on how communication regimes and gendered inequity reinforce each other, and a critical precursor to the platform-era questions about whose speech, body, and data are governable.
`XD` **Tiptree, J. Jr. (1985). Brightness falls from the air. Tor Books.**

[Equity + Communication + AI] A small group of human caretakers protect the Damiem, a peaceful alien species whose tear secretions produce a hallucinogen marketed by genocidal hunters. Tiptree (Alice B. Sheldon) binds extraction economy, communication breakdown, and the proto-AI of the "Stars Tears" trade. The pillars meet in the political question of whose biological signal becomes intoxicant for whom.
`XD` **Yu, C. (2020). Interior Chinatown. Pantheon.**

[Communication + Equity] Yu's novel-in-screenplay-form positions a Taiwanese-American actor as "Generic Asian Man" trying to ascend to "Kung Fu Guy" status. The pillars synthesize in the formal experiment itself: screenplay as both communication object and ideological cage. A foundational cross-pillar novel about how media format determines racial possibility.
`XD` **Whitehead, C. (2023). Crook manifesto. Doubleday.**

[Equity + Communication] Sequel to Harlem Shuffle: Ray Carney navigates 1970s Harlem real estate, organized crime, blaxploitation film production, and the politics of who profits from neighborhood mythologies. Whitehead binds film IP (a blaxploitation production crew rents the furniture store) to the equity politics of urban Black entrepreneurship and to the communicative work of being seen on screen. The pillars meet in the camera dolly tracking down Lenox Avenue.
`XP` **Saramago, J. (1995). Blindness (G. Pontiero, Trans.). Harcourt. (Original work published in Portuguese)**

Illuminates Communication: Saramago's novel of a city suddenly blinded is a study in what communication infrastructure does when one sensory channel collapses. Placed beside disability studies and platform accessibility, Blindness illuminates how civic order is sensorially constructed. A cross-pollination for thinking about platform design as a sensory politics.
