**Braided extracurricular reading**

#### Fiction
`XD` **Ng, C. (2022). Our missing hearts. Penguin Press.**

[Equity + Communication + IP] A near-future U.S. under "PACT" laws polices Asian Americans, censors books, and removes children from "subversive" families. Ng follows a 12-year-old son searching for his mother, a poet whose verse is repurposed as protest. The pillars meet in censorship (communication regulation), targeted ethnic persecution (equity), and the strange afterlife of a poem as both political IP and contraband. A novel about how authorship and political speech are inseparable in a surveillance state.
`XD` **Jemisin, N. K. (2016). The obelisk gate. Orbit.**

[Equity + Communication + AI] Jemisin's middle volume widens the cross-pillar argument: the obelisks are revealed as ancient AI infrastructure built by an earlier, oppressed orogene class. The pillars synthesize in the network's politics—who built the machine intelligence, and at what cost. A novel about how communication systems, AI, and slavery rhyme across millennia.
`XD` **North, C. (2017). The end of the day. Orbit.**

[Equity + Communication + AI] The Harbinger of Death travels the world delivering greetings and warnings—a corporate communications role for the apocalypse. North binds the inequity of who receives the harbinger's visit (climate refugees, displaced indigenous peoples) to the soft AI of bureaucratic protocol. The pillars meet in the email signature.
`XP` **Knausgaard, K. O. (2009). My struggle: Book one (D. Bartlett, Trans.). Archipelago Books. (Original work published in Norwegian)**

Illuminates Communication: Knausgaard's 3,600-page autofictional project is the limit case of what self-narration can be in the age of the feed. Placed beside platform-studies work on personal branding and confessional content, My Struggle illuminates the structural pressure to convert life into ongoing text. A cross-pollination for thinking about the autofiction industry and the platform economy.
#### Nonfiction
`XD` **Citron, D. K. (2014). Hate crimes in cyberspace. Harvard University Press.**

[Communication + Equity + AI] Citron documents the gendered and racialized character of online harassment and proposes legal and platform interventions. The pillars synthesize through the platform's communication architecture as a site of harm production.
`XD` **Florini, S. (2019). Beyond hashtags: Racial politics and Black digital networks. NYU Press.**

[Communication + Equity + AI] Florini documents the cross-platform circulation of Black political discourse from Twitter to podcasts. The pillars synthesize in the network rather than in the single platform.
`XD` **Collins, P. H. (1990). Black feminist thought: Knowledge, consciousness, and the politics of empowerment. Routledge.**

[Equity + Communication] Collins articulates the "matrix of domination" and theorizes Black feminist epistemology as a communicative practice. The pillars synthesize in the standpoint theory of Black women's intellectual labor. Canonical.
`XP` **Scott, J. C. (1998). Seeing like a state. Yale University Press.**

Illuminates AI/Equity: Scott's foundational text on legibility is the canonical cross-pollination with critical-AI scholarship. A primer for any data-justice unit.

**36. Gianluca Falco, Cathy Simmers, Jatin Nathwani, Gunes Ercal, Oluwaseun Sode.** *Governing AI Safety through Independent Audits* 


> Draws on nuclear, aviation, and financial-audit governance models to design a framework for independent AI auditors; proposes credentialing, conflict-of-interest rules, and minimum audit standards; addresses the revolving-door problem.

**37. Michael Gaddis.** *Audit Studies: Behind the Scenes with Theory, Method, and Nuance* 


> Reviews the long tradition of correspondence and in-person audit studies in housing and employment discrimination research; examines validity threats (outcome coding, tester variability, ecological validity) and proposes quality standards directly applicable to digital audit design.

**38. Ben Green.** *Constructing "Best Practices" in Machine Learning Auditing: Challenges and Pathways* 


> Analyzes how "best practice" discourse in algorithmic auditing constructs legitimacy without necessarily improving outcomes; argues that audit professionalization can become a shield against stronger legal accountability; calls for civil-society participation in setting audit standards.

Connections between audit methodology and adjacent fields: intellectual property, journalism, design justice, and AI governance policy.

> Surveys legal barriers — trade-secret privilege, CFAA, contractual NDAs — that third-party auditors face when attempting to access AI systems; compares financial-audit and environmental-audit models; proposes a federal compelled-disclosure statute.

**41. Rebecca Wexler.** *The Right to Audit AI* 


> Argues that trade-secret law is systematically used to block defense access to algorithmic evidence in criminal proceedings; proposes a compelled-disclosure doctrine under due-process and confrontation clause grounds; extends to civil audit access.

**42. Amanda Levendowski.** *How Copyright Law Can Fix Artificial Intelligence's Implicit Bias Problem* 


> Argues that copyright's fair-use doctrine and work-for-hire rules enable training-data practices that embed bias; proposes copyright reform — broader fair use for bias testing, mandatory licensing with bias audits — as a mechanism for upstream bias mitigation.

**43. Angèle Christin.** *Metrics at Work: Journalism and the Contested Meaning of Algorithms* 


> Ethnography of US and French digital newsrooms shows how traffic metrics are interpreted, resisted, and instrumentalized by journalists; demonstrates that algorithmic accountability requires understanding the organizational context of metric use.

**44. Sasha Costanza-Chock.** *Design Justice: Community-Led Practices to Build the Worlds We Need* 


> Argues that design processes systematically reproduce matrix-of-domination inequalities unless communities most affected by a technology lead the design; proposes Design Justice principles as an upstream audit and co-design methodology.

**45. Ben Green, Yiling Chen.** *The Misuse of AUC: Validation in High-Stakes Prediction*

**46. Jamila Jefferson-Jones.** *Artificial Intelligence and Civil Rights: Toward a Framework for Equity Audits* 


> Proposes "equity audits" as a distinct category from bias audits: while bias audits test statistical parity, equity audits assess whether a system advances or retards historically marginalized groups' substantive access to opportunity; links to FHA affirmative obligation.

**47. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).** *NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*

**Braided extracurricular reading**

#### Fiction
`XD` **Adjei-Brenyah, N. K. (2018). Friday Black. Mariner Books.**

[Equity + Communication + AI] In the title story, retail workers fight zombified Black Friday shoppers; in "The Finkelstein 5," a daily walk is reimagined as a televised dare. Adjei-Brenyah braids racialized labor, virality, and a coming algorithmic gaze. The pillars synthesize through brand-and-spectacle: how consumerism's communication systems extract from Black bodies in conditions of platformed visibility. A debut collection that reads as a single argument about cross-pillar terror.
`XD` **Coates, T.-N. (2019). The water dancer. One World.**

[Equity + Communication] Hiram Walker, born to an enslaved woman and her plantation master, discovers he can "conduct" people via memory across geography. Coates binds Black archival recovery to the communicative magic of memory itself, and to the inequity of forgetting as state policy. The pillars meet in the conducting itself: communication as freedom infrastructure.
`XD` **Ozeki, R. (2013). A tale for the time being. Viking.**

[Communication + Equity + AI] A Canadian novelist on a Pacific island finds a Tokyo schoolgirl's diary washed up after the 2011 tsunami. Ozeki binds transpacific communication infrastructure (the diary, the Hello Kitty lunchbox, the email-back-and-forth) to the inequity politics of the bullied schoolgirl and her kamikaze great-uncle. The pillars meet in the quantum question of who is reading whom.
`XP` **Murata, S. (2016). Convenience store woman (G. Tapley Takemori, Trans.). Granta. (Original work published 2016)**

Illuminates AI/Equity: Murata's slim novel of a woman who works at a Tokyo konbini and finds personal meaning in its scripted protocols is a study in human-as-API. Placed beside ghost-work scholarship, the book illuminates how scripted retail labor produces a different theory of selfhood. A cross-pollination for thinking about LLM-as-assistant from inside the worker's perspective.
#### Nonfiction
`XD` **Lessig, L. (2008). Remix: Making art and commerce thrive in the hybrid economy. Penguin Press.**

[IP + Communication + Equity] Lessig's argument for a "hybrid economy" of remix and commerce binds copyright reform to vernacular communication and to amateur production economics. The pillars synthesize in the "Read-Write" culture argument. A canonical text for cross-pillar IP-communication scholarship.
`XD` **Banet-Weiser, S. (2018). Empowered: Popular feminism and popular misogyny. Duke University Press.**

[Equity + Communication] Banet-Weiser argues that popular feminism and popular misogyny are dialectically linked online. The pillars synthesize in the meme economy.
`XD` **Cooper, B. (2018). Eloquent rage: A Black feminist discovers her superpower. St. Martin's Press.**

[Equity + Communication] Cooper's essays bind Black feminist anger to the public communication economy of academic and popular writing. The pillars synthesize in the public-intellectual register.
`XP` **Sharpe, C. (2016). In the wake: On Blackness and being. Duke University Press.**

Illuminates Equity: Sharpe's meditation on the afterlives of slavery is a cross-pollination text for any Black-studies AI-ethics unit.
